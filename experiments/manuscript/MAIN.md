# Open replicators evolve closure in a digital primordial soup

*Working draft, 2026-10-08 (night). Venue to be decided; written to Nature/Science length and structure (main text ≈ 3,500 words, four main figures, Methods, Extended Data). Every number is taken from a generated table named in square brackets; numbers still to be filled from tables are marked `[fill: table]`. Sections marked PENDING depend on runs still in progress.*

## Abstract

Theories of the origin of life disagree about what came first, but agree that at some point a self-propagating pattern became an individual: something whose persistence depends on itself rather than on its surroundings. Here we watch that step happen, thousands of times, in a system simple enough to dissect completely. Random 8-bit (Z80) machine-code programs interact pairwise in a soup with no fitness function; nothing is selected except what persists. In 3,560 worlds across 13 instruction-set ablations, tape lengths from 3 to 100 bytes, execution budgets and mutation rates, the same sequence occurs. A sterile "tar" of self-written zeros forms first. Then a two-byte program — an immediate load and a stack push, `LD rr,nn ; PUSH rr` — becomes the first heritable replicator in every permissive world: it is a *literal* that writes itself, it is open (its instruction pointer runs through its partner and its success depends on the partner's bytes, copying into 54–82% of random partners and damaging itself in up to 37% of encounters), and it has no membrane, no loop and no test of its environment. Closure evolves afterwards: a control-flow cycle (`RET NZ`, `JR NZ`, `DJNZ`, or a hardware loop) keeps the pointer inside the organism, and the successor copies into 100% of random partners with no self-damage, independent worlds converging on byte-identical closed genomes. We give a formal definition of closure as forward invariance of the organism's bytes under execution across contexts, prove when an open replicator can and cannot exist, and show that compression-based life detectors fail because tar is more orderly than life. In the Brainfuck-family soup of Agüera y Arcas et al., which lacks a literal write channel, the theory predicts and we find that replicators are born closed (28 of 28 first replicators loop-bearing, none open); adding one literal-writing instruction produces an open, loop-free first replicator in 12 of 12 worlds — which then collapses, because that system's tar halts any program that runs into it. Individuality, in these systems, is not a boundary in space but a closed cycle in a program's control flow, and the open phase that precedes it is a window whose length the chemistry of the tar decides.

## Main

Life is often defined by a list — metabolism, compartment, heredity — and origin-of-life research by which item came first. A different tradition asks instead what makes a living thing an *individual*: Maturana and Varela's operational closure, Rosen's closure to efficient causation, and the information-theoretic individuality of Krakauer and colleagues all locate life in a system's dependence on itself rather than on its environment. Neither tradition has had an experimental system in which the transition from pattern to individual can be watched, replicated, and taken apart.

Soups of random programs offer one. Agüera y Arcas et al. recently showed that random Brainfuck-like programs executing on a shared tape give rise to self-replicators without any fitness function, and earlier work found the same in Core War–like chemistries and in random Lisp programs. These systems have nothing to protect: no energy, no membrane, no spatial structure, no explicit selection. Whatever becomes an individual there does so by the logic of self-reference alone.

We built such a soup from a real instruction set, the Zilog Z80, on the GPU, with an assay that measures heredity by intervention rather than by inspection, and we ran it as an experiment: thirteen instruction-set ablations, tape lengths from 3 to 100 bytes, execution budgets from 32 to 512 steps per encounter, mutation rates over two decades, and pre-registered confirmatory stages with kill criteria. Below we report what emerged, in the order it emerged.

### A soup with nothing to select for

The soup holds 20,000 programs ("tapes") of L bytes each, randomly initialised (Fig. 1a). At each step 8,192 random pairs are drawn, of which about 3,980 are active; each pair is concatenated into a 2L-byte ring, executed by a Z80 core for 128 instructions starting at the first byte of the first tape with the stack pointer at the last byte of the second, and written back. A background mutation replaces 8,192/2^k random bytes per step. There is no fitness function and no replacement: a tape changes only by being executed or by being written into. The simulator runs 1.2–1.5 × 10⁹ pair-slots per second on a laptop GPU and the sweeps ran on 50 parallel GPUs (Methods).

Replication is measured by a *culture test* (Fig. 1b). A tape is executed against 64 random partners; the fraction of partners that become ≥ 75% copies of it is its copy success, and the tape is *heritable* if its offspring, executed against fresh random partners, in turn produce copies (gen2 ≥ 0.3). This matters because the most common byte pattern in a soup is often not a replicator: a program may write an inert pattern into its neighbours, and a return-address smear can occupy 0.5% of the soup without inheriting anything [results/REPORT_stageA.md]. We define emergence by the first heritable exemplar among the three most common tapes with a share ≥ 0.5% (`t_rep`) and track the heritable fraction of random cells as the population-level measure.

### The order of events

In every unablated world the same three regimes follow one another (Fig. 1c).

**Tar.** Within a few hundred steps, 24–34% of all bytes are zero at every tape length [results/stageG/NUMBERS.md §3; stage B/E]. The zeros are self-written: `PUSH`, `CALL` and `RST` of zero-valued registers and return addresses write zeros onto whatever the stack pointer touches, and zero is itself a no-op. This "zero flood" is highly compressible, spreads by writing, and is sterile — the culture test gives gen2 ≈ 0 for its carriers. It is the digital analogue of Benner's tar: order without heredity, produced by the same chemistry that will later produce life.

**The open replicator.** The first heritable replicator is the two-byte program `01 c5` — `LD BC,nn ; PUSH BC` — tiled across the tape, in 19/20, 12/20, 14/20 and 18/20 of the pre-registered Stage G worlds at L = 16, 20, 50 and 64 [results/stageG/stageG/NUMBERS_G.md], and in every permissive arm of the ablation atlas, with register variants (`11 d5`, `21 e5`) making up the rest. Its mechanism is a tautology (Fig. 1d): the two-byte literal `01 c5` is both the instruction `LD BC,$01c5` and the data it loads; `PUSH BC` writes the two bytes `01 c5` to the stack, which lies in the partner, and the instruction pointer advances to the next copy of the same literal. Code equals data equals literal. It needs no loop, because the ring brings the pointer back to the start; no counter, because every byte of the tape is the same two-byte word; and no test of its environment, because it never reads one. It appears at a Kaplan–Meier median of 450–750 steps regardless of L between 16 and 100 [results/stageG/NUMBERS.md; results/stageB/FINDINGS.md]. It is also essentially the only thing of its size that can replicate: of all 65,536 two-byte words tiled to 16 bytes, exactly five copy into at least half of 256 random partners, and all five are load–push pairs (`01 c5`, `11 d5`, `21 e5`, and the memory-load variants `2a e5`/`e5 2a`); none copies into more than 81% [results/census2/NUMBERS_CENSUS2.md]. A further 246 words pass the heredity threshold without producing a single 75% copy — `CALL` instructions smearing return addresses — which is why we require faithfulness as well as heredity before calling anything a replicator.

**Closure.** The pusher does not stay. By 300,000 steps the dominant tape at L = 16 carries a control-flow instruction in 20/20 Stage G worlds and 17 of them end on the identical sequence `ad e3 21 e3 21 c0 ad c0` — `XOR L ; EX (SP),HL ; LD HL,$21e3 ; RET NZ` — whose `RET NZ` returns the pointer to the organism's own body; at L = 50 all 20 worlds carry `JR NZ,-16` inserted into the pusher tiling, 14 of them byte-identical [NUMBERS_G.md]. Across the 233 unablated worlds of the exploratory stages, 92 gained a control-flow instruction between the first replicator and the final dominant and 3 lost one (exact McNemar p = 7 × 10⁻²⁴) [results/closure/NUMBERS_CLOSURE.md].

### The first replicator is open; the successor is closed

Why does a replicator that already fills the soup get replaced? Because it is open. Executed against 256 random partners, the first replicator copies into a median 0.66–0.73 of them (range 0.56–0.84 across 80 worlds and four tape lengths) and loses a quarter or more of its own bytes in a median 0.06–0.43 of encounters (Fig. 2a) [NUMBERS_G.md]. The failures have a single cause: after writing its copy, the pointer runs on into the partner and executes the partner's bytes, which can poison the register pair before the next push. Against all-zero partners the pusher copies perfectly; its success falls as the partner's density of active bytes rises [THEORY.md P4], and no execution budget rescues it (0.69 at 512 and 1,024 steps) [results/closure].

The closed successors copy into 1.00 of 256 random partners with 0.00 self-damage, at every tape length, whatever instruction implements the cycle — `RET NZ`, `JR NZ`, `DJNZ`, `JP`, `JP (HL)`, `RET`, or the hardware loops `LDIR`/`LDDR` (Fig. 2a; Extended Data Table 1) [NUMBERS_G.md; NUMBERS_CLOSURE.md]. The heritable fraction of random cells tells the population-level story (Fig. 2b): at L = 16 it stays between 0.07 and 0.19 for the first 20,000 steps after emergence — the pusher's reign is a world of damaged copies — and rises to 0.75–0.94 when the closed successor takes over [results/stageG/c4; results/stageC/FINDINGS.md §C4].

We call this property *closure*: the organism's bytes are a forward-invariant set under execution for every context (Definition 2, Methods). It is the operational closure of Maturana and Varela made countable, and the individuality of Krakauer et al. made measurable by intervention: the closed organism's future depends on its own past alone. Note what closure is *not*. It is not a membrane — the closed organism's bytes are as exposed to writes as before. It is not a test of the environment — `RET NZ` tests a flag the organism itself set. It is a cycle in the organism's control flow that never leaves the organism. Individuality here is a topological property of a program, not a geometric property of a region.

Closure takes longer the larger the organism. At L = 20, 19/20 worlds are closed by one million steps; at L = 64, 8/20, with 12/20 still dominated by the open pusher — which at L = 64 is also the least damaged open replicator (self-damage 0.06) and so under the weakest pressure to close [NUMBERS_G.md]. The pre-registered expectation that ≥ 10/20 worlds would close by one million steps at both lengths was met at L = 20 only behaviourally — the dominant family there closes with `LDIR`, a hardware loop that our syntactic criterion did not count — and not at L = 64; both counts are reported (Methods, Pre-registration).

### Three load-bearing parts of the instruction set

Which instructions does this need? The ablation atlas (Fig. 3a; 13 ablations × budgets × mutation rates, seed-paired, Stage C) finds three load-bearing parts and nothing else [results/stageC/NUMBERS_C.md]. Removing *immediate loads* leaves 2/10 worlds alive at 300,000 steps; removing *stack writers* delays emergence 20–100-fold at 128–512 steps per encounter (push-only ×992, write-only ×382, all stack writes ×160 in median emergence step) and hands the soup to a different replicator built on the block-copy instruction `LDIR`; removing *all copying* (no load, push or block copy — 112 opcodes) or leaving only read-modify-write instructions produces no replicator in 40 worlds at one million steps. Every other ablation — block copy, calls and returns, memory loads, I/O, exchanges — is within seed-paired noise of the unablated soup. The mutation rate chooses the family that holds the soup (LDIR clouds at 1/4, `EX (SP),HL` lineages at 1/16–1/64), and the budget decides whether an organism can be faithful at all: at L = 100 the `LDIR` regime is faithful in 1/10 worlds at 128 steps per encounter and 10/10 at 256 (the pusher, needing two bytes of code per two bytes written, is faithful in 9/10 already at 128), and at 64 steps the pre-registered emergence event never fires yet 6/10 final soups are heritable populations with no clone — the top genotype below 0.07% of cells, 69–100% of random cells heritable, none faithful: heredity carried by partial copies, the communal regime that Woese placed before the Darwinian threshold [results/stageE/FINDINGS.md].

Where the stack-writing instructions are removed, something else happens at L = 9: the soup does *better* without them. The exploratory observation (9/10 and 10/10 worlds alive without stack writes against 4/10 and 1/10 with them, Stage B) was confirmed with 20 new seeds per arm: 12/20 vs 6/20 at 128 steps and 19/20 vs 5/20 at 512 steps, Cochran–Mantel–Haenszel p = 3.9 × 10⁻⁶ (Stage D) [results/stageD/FINDINGS.md]. The mechanism is that the tar the stack writers produce destroys nascent `LDIR` copiers before they assemble: an `LDIR` tape reaches the ten most common tapes in 5/20 worlds, at a median 118,500 steps, with stack writers present, against 20/20 within 3,400 steps without them, and programs are destroyed (half their bytes changed in one encounter) 6.8 vs 1.3 times per 1,000 interactions [results/stageD/FINDINGS.md]. Tar is not a nursery; it is a hazard that only an open literal-writer, which cannot be poisoned by zeros, gets through.

### No size floor, and a geometry that can be switched off

Emergence time is flat from L = 16 to L = 100 (Fig. 3b): the pusher's cost is two bytes however long the tape, because tiling makes a large organism out of a small word. Below 16 there is no monotone floor but a dead zone: life at L = 5 (whole-tape copiers), at L = 8 and at L ≥ 13, none at L = 3, 4 and 7, and marginal at 9–12, where the pusher's own isolated heritability dips to 0.20–0.32 [results/stageE/FINDINGS.md]. The dead zone is geometric, not informational: the stack pointer descends two bytes per push while the instruction pointer ascends two bytes per instruction pair, and on a ring of 2L bytes they race; when a push lands on the literal the pointer is about to read, the register pair is poisoned. Padding the pair memory to a longer ring without changing the organism moves the collision and switches the dead zone off: at L = 12, rings of 28 or 36 bytes give 10/10 worlds alive at 350–600 steps where the native 24-byte ring gives none [results/stageF/FINDINGS.md]. A pre-registered alternative — that tiling periods obey an arithmetic law on the ring — was falsified: the period divides the pair length 2L, not the ring [stageF F1].

Closure, too, has a geometric channel. The `RET NZ` closer of L = 16 returns to an absolute address (0x21E3 ≡ 3 mod 32) and copies 0.00 of partners on any ring but its own; the `DJNZ` closer (relative jump) keeps control closed on every ring but still writes through the stack pointer, whose path across a padded ring is geometric (0.12–0.73) [THEORY.md P3]. Control can be made coordinate-free; the reproductive channel of this machine cannot.

### Why compression does not detect life

The transition of Agüera y Arcas et al. is detected by a drop in high-order entropy (byte entropy minus compressed size). In our unablated soups that detector is *anti-correlated* with life at L = 25, 49, 64 and 81 (AUC 0.07–0.34 against the culture-test ground truth), because the zero flood compresses better than the replicators that replace it; the fraction of non-unique tapes and the raw bits per byte are robust (AUC ≥ 0.84 at every L but one), and occupancy of the dominant genotype, the natural "species" measure, is a poor proxy for a heritable population (AUC 0.61–0.95 by tape length, 0.71 pooled) [results/detectors/FINDINGS.md]. Tar is more orderly than life. Any agnostic biosignature that scores order without testing propagation will be fooled by the same chemistry that makes life possible.

### Generality: predictions for other machines — PENDING

The theory (Methods, Lemmas 1–2) says when the open phase can exist: a straight-line program must be able to write at least its own length within one encounter, and the content it writes must not depend on the memory it overwrites — a literal in the instruction stream, as in `LD rr,nn ; PUSH rr`. The Brainfuck family of Agüera y Arcas et al. fails the first condition as published (its instruction pointer makes one pass and stops) and fails the second even if the pointer is made to wrap (it has no literal store; `+`/`−` increment in place). An exhaustive search over 16.4 million structured straight-line programs under a wrapping pointer found only one-symbol fills — the BFF analogue of tar — and no program that copies a genome of period ≥ 2 [results/bff_search/NUMBERS_SEARCH.md]. The prediction, fixed before the runs, was therefore that BFF replicators are *born closed*, with a loop, in both the published and the wrapping variant, and that an open phase appears only when a machine has a literal write channel.

Sixty soups of 2¹⁷ programs run for 16,384 epochs test this (Fig. 4) [results/bff/NUMBERS_BFF.md]. In the published system, 9 of 24 worlds produced a heritable replicator; every first replicator contains a loop (9/9), none is open (0/9), and seven of nine are closed by our strict criterion from the first sample in which they appear. Making the pointer wrap produces more transitions (19/24, Fisher p = 0.008) but the same birth: 19/19 loop-bearing, 0/19 open, 16/19 closed. Adding one instruction that writes its own two operand bytes below the write head — the literal channel — changes everything about the beginning: in 12/12 worlds the first replicator is the one-byte tiling of that instruction, whose operands are two more copies of itself, open (its pointer enters the partner in 100% of encounters), loop-free, and present at the first sample (epoch 64) with 53–60% of the soup. It is the `01 c5` of BFF. And then it dies: within about 200 epochs every one of the 12 soups turns into tar that halts a pointer within a few dozen steps (an unmatched `]` terminates BFF execution), copies into such partners are cut short and sterile, and the open replicator persists only as a 0.2% minority to the end, heritable in isolation and unable to spread. No closed class ever evolves. The open phase is therefore a *window*, not a stage: BFF's tar is lethal to open organisms, the Z80's zeros are harmless no-ops, and closure can only be found while the open form survives. One instruction toggles the open phase on; the chemistry of the tar decides whether it lasts long enough to close.

### Discussion

Three claims rest on these results. First, in soups of random machine code, heredity precedes individuality: the first replicator is a self-writing literal that is open to its surroundings and depends on them, and closure — the organism's control never leaving the organism — is an evolved refinement selected by the damage the open form suffers. Second, closure is topological rather than geometric: it is a cycle in control flow, not a boundary in space, and it is reached by different instructions in different worlds that converge on the same function and often on the same bytes. Third, the elements a substrate must offer are few and specific — a literal that is its own code, a write channel aimed at a neighbour, and a cycle — and the stages they produce (tar, open replicator, closed individual) are the stages origin-of-life theory has argued over in the abstract: Benner's tar, Eigen's error threshold (which we see as a curve in the LDIR regime), Woese's communal heredity before vertical descent, and Fontana and Buss's organisational closure, here arising *from* a copier rather than in its absence.

Whether anything of this transfers to chemistry is an open question we do not answer. What the system gives is a definition of closure that can be checked by intervention — execute the candidate in many contexts and ask whether its own bytes survive — and that definition is substrate-independent. It is also a warning for life detection: the first ordered, spreading, compressible thing in these worlds is not alive, and the first living thing is a two-byte tautology that no inspection of its structure would flag.

## Methods

*(Condensed; full protocols in `experiments/PLAN.md`, `experiments/THEORY.md`, and the per-stage FINDINGS files.)*

**Soup.** 20,000 tapes of L bytes; 8,192 pairs per step (≈ 3,980 active, 0.395 active interactions per cell per step); pair memory a ring of 2L bytes (padded to P ≥ 2L in Stage F; padding is never written back); execution by the Zilion Z80 core (`@neovand/zilion` 0.2.0, WGSL) for 128 instructions per encounter (32–512 in budget arms) from byte 0 with SP at the last byte of the partner; mutation 8,192/2^k random bytes per step. Instruction-set ablations replace opcodes by no-ops through a prefix-aware fetch hook (Methods table; 13 ablations). The emulator was validated against an independent Z80 reference with zero divergences in 5,000-pair randomised tests; it is not treated as ground truth for any claim about the Z80 itself.

**Culture test.** A tape is run as the first program against 64 uniformly random partners for the condition's budget; copy success is the fraction of partners that become ≥ 75% copies at the best cyclic shift; gen2 is the copy success of the produced copies against fresh partners, times the copy fraction; a tape is heritable if gen2 ≥ 0.3 and faithful if additionally ≥ 50% of partners became copies. Partner-independence tests use 256 partners and record self-damage (≥ 25% of the organism's bytes changed).

**Events.** `t_rep`: first sample at which a top-three exemplar with share ≥ 0.5% is heritable (a clonality criterion by design); `t_faith`, `tq_10` (quasispecies occupancy ≥ 10%) recorded alongside. Kaplan–Meier medians with censoring at the horizon; Wilson intervals; Fisher and Cochran–Mantel–Haenszel tests; seed-paired sign tests for ablation effects; exact McNemar for paired gain of control flow.

**Definitions.** *Self-writer*: a tape fixed under its own execution in some context. *Closure* (Definition 2): the organism's bytes are forward-invariant under execution for every context in the class of uniformly random partners. *Lemma 1*: open self-writers of two bytes exist whenever an instruction set has an immediate operand and a neighbour-directed write. *Lemma 2*: closure requires a control-flow cycle confined to the organism. *Criterion*: an open full replicator exists only if a straight-line program can write at least its own length per encounter with content independent of the overwritten memory. Formal statements and proofs are in `THEOREMS.md`: Theorem 1 (no straight-line self-replicator in one-pass BFF; at most 43 copied bytes), Theorem 2 (a closed replicator executes some address twice whenever bytes written per code byte < 1 — which is why hardware repeats such as `LDIR` count as loops), Theorem 6 (for any von Neumann machine, a self-consistent literal-write instruction decides whether the first replicator is open or born closed), Lemma 7 (closure as zero information inflow I(o; e | x) from context to offspring, the limit perfect fidelity requires), Propositions 3–4 (the exact two-byte fixed points of the Z80; the open-first count) and Model 5 (the closure window q∫n dt).

**Pre-registration.** Stages D, F and G were pre-registered with predictions and kill criteria before launch; the change log records every amendment with its date. Two pre-registration failures are reported: the heritable-fraction thresholds of G1(c) did not transfer from L = 16 to L = 50, and the syntactic loop criterion of G2 omitted hardware loops.

**Compute and cost.** 3,560 Z80 soup runs (Stages A–G) and 60 BFF soups on Modal L40S plus local runs; ≈ $380 at list price, estimated from the preflight logs and the recorded run times. The BFF soup is bitwise reproducible across machines (identical epoch statistics on an Apple GPU and an L40S for six shared seeds); the Z80 soup is not (GPU scheduling races), so every Z80 claim is a population claim over seeds. All code, conditions, raw summaries and generated tables are in the repository; figures are produced by the analysis scripts from generated tables.
