# Response to the second-round reviews: Heredity before individuality in a digital primordial soup (revision 2)

*To the reviewers. Thank you for a second careful round. We verified every factual point against our code and data before acting, and the reviews were right on every point we could check. They led to four new pre-registered tests, one exploratory population analysis defined before it ran, and a threshold and uncertainty analysis. Two of the new tests overturned claims of revision 1, and we report both plainly. The second second-round report had not reached us when this letter was written; it will be answered separately.*

## What changed, in brief

1. **Serial allele retention (the test you asked for).** Every single-byte mutant of 14 genomes was followed through eight serial transfers into fresh random partners, with every lineage counted as lost, alive with the allele erased, or alive carrying it (Fig. 4a–c). The pusher's mutations reach its first copies but not their descendants: 96–100% of its lineages stop copying within eight transfers, and with partners drawn from its own world its alleles are gone by the fourth transfer as well. "Pushed again by the copy" was wrong; it is withdrawn. The evolved closers keep every lineage alive but erase the mutation in 60–88% of mutant lineages and keep it in none. Closers with a short copying core followed by bytes they never run keep the allele in 46–63% of mutant lineages after eight transfers.
2. **Random cells instead of the most common tape.** The most common tape misrepresents a diverse population: a regenerating population is nearly clonal, a transmitting one is a cloud of distinct genomes. Sampling random cells (Fig. 4d,e) changed two conclusions. **L = 50 does not close:** the closed tape's offspring carries a fourth jump and enters the partner in every encounter, the lineage stops copying at the third transfer, and only 18–39% of heritable cells are confined. **Closure does not always erase variation:** in 9 of 10 lethal-tar worlds most heritable cells copy bytes they never run (median 11.5 such sites), and in the ten-million-step worlds at L = 20 such transmitters held at least a tenth of heritable cells at some snapshot in 10 of 10 and the majority in 4, before regenerators took over again.
3. **The 8080 counterexample, run and used.** Your 20-byte closer works in our executor under the 8080-like restriction (exact copies into 256 of 256 random partners, confined, eight serial transfers exact). "Expresses no closer" was false and is withdrawn. Seeded at 1% into the 8080 pusher worlds at L = 32 it takes over in 5 of 5 soups (its loop in half the cells within 300–320 steps); the barrier was discovery, not expressibility.
4. **Regeneration against transmission, head to head.** Under the full Z80 at L = 32 the evolved block-copy tiling, seeded at 1% into a world of the payload-carrying closer, held 58–80% of cells after 50,000 steps in 5 of 5 soups, and the payload carrier did not spread in the tiling's world. Without that competitor (8080 subset) the payload carrier's descendants kept 15–17 never-executed bytes, different from cell to cell, through eight transfers.
5. **The invasion marker validated, and it failed.** At L = 50 the jump word reaches 73–75% of cells while only 19–25% of cells are confined, and only 26–33% of marker carriers are; the pre-registered agreement criterion failed. The L = 50 invasion is now reported as the spread of the jump, not of closure.

## Point by point

**Serial transmission (skeptical §2, optimistic §3, synthesis §2).** Done as described above, with the five-byte window rule (alignment-free, so inserted jumps do not hide the allele), every lineage in the denominator, the three-way partition you proposed and a chance background from unmutated lineages (Methods, Serial allele retention; `serial_retention.py`, registered before the run). Our registered prediction that the pusher's sites persist failed and is reported. Retention conditional on a live lineage and unconditional retention are both in the tables.

**"Capacity" (all three).** Renamed the single-mutant variation score, defined as a summary of single mutants that are heritable and reach first-generation copies, explicitly not a channel capacity (Methods; Extended Data Fig. 6). "Inherits nothing", "canalised" and "smaller closers" are gone; Fig. 4 is now titled "Closure regenerates or transmits", and the text separates lethality, erasure and persistent transmission.

**Competition and the marker (skeptical §3, optimistic §4, synthesis §3).** The marker was checked against traced confinement before use and failed (above). The summary no longer claims one jump or fifty encounters; the main text describes a jump that spreads without making its carriers closed. We did not build one-, two- and three-jump constructs, because with the claim narrowed they would test a statement the paper no longer makes.

**Robustness and Cicala et al. (skeptical §3, optimistic §5, synthesis §3).** The comparison is now stated within our assay only ("a single-mutant robustness advantage does not explain the succession under our conditions"), with the differences in architecture, budget, partners and criteria named. Cicala et al. are credited for showing that a block-copy prefix carries 28 arbitrary bytes; what we add is which design arises, where, and what becomes of it.

**"Genomes lose to smaller closers" (skeptical §3, optimistic §6, synthesis §3).** Replaced by population data: transmitter shares of random heritable cells over time (Fig. 4e), with the modal-tape analysis labelled as such. In the one direct contest we ran, the regenerator won; we did not compete the three transient genomes against their own successors.

**The 8080 subset (skeptical §4, synthesis §4).** The counterexample is reported in Methods with its source; M4 is withdrawn in the registration record; the subset is called an 8080-like restriction of the same emulator throughout, the summary included; Extended Data Fig. 5 and the main text say that no closer emerged, not that none exists.

**Proposition 4 and "counting fixes the order" (skeptical §5, synthesis §5).** The evolutionary corollary is removed. Proposition 4 now says that it counts the two-byte word, not the tiled self-writer (whose expected number at step 0 is of order N/256^L^), that nucleation is not modelled, and that the order of events is an empirical result explained, not forced, by the counts. The summary and "What is proved" say the same. Theorem 6(iv) carries the same caveat.

**Theorem 1 (skeptical §5, optimistic §8, synthesis §5).** Restated with explicit quantifiers in two parts. (i) A single instruction sequence that copies a 64-byte tape into *every* partner of the class P (partners holding none of the copy's bytes in place) runs at least 129 instructions; such a sequence is what a program executes while its pointer stays out of the partner, so "prescribed" is now well defined, and a confined copier must loop. (ii) In the no-op context, an execution that runs each address at most once cannot copy a tape with more than 32 non-zero bytes, by counting modifications and head moves (increments are counted, so the step you questioned is not needed). The false gloss about the culture test is gone; the theorem bounds exact copies, not the test's verdict. Theorems 2 and 6 cite the part they use.

**The BFF map and the closed-first rule (skeptical §6, optimistic §8, synthesis §5).** Fig. 5e now says "born loop-bearing · 7 of 12 soups (5 confined, 1 partly, 1 open)" and "born loop-bearing · all 28 replicators … partner entered in at most 34%". The main text says that the absence of a literal channel predicts a loop, not confinement.

**Theorem 2's boundary (skeptical §6).** The saturating BFF copiers are now described as independent of the partner without a cycle and without confinement; the text states that confinement and independence are different properties that coincide in the Z80's loop-bearing finals.

**Model 5 (skeptical §7, optimistic §8, synthesis §6).** The window is now at most about 1.5 × 10⁷ tape-epochs (0.6 N over the 192 epochs of the decline, N = 2¹⁷), not 10¹⁹; the lethal-tar medians are the Kaplan–Meier 38,000 against 450. These were our errors: the round-1 audit covered the main text and not the Supplementary Information. This time the audit included the Supplementary Information and every legend. Model 5 remains a qualitative interpretation; we no longer say its ingredients are measured.

**Statistics (skeptical §7, synthesis §6).** The exact gen2 equation is in Methods. Extended Data Table 3 gives medians with 95% bootstrap intervals across worlds for the principal quantities, Kaplan–Meier medians with bootstrap intervals, and Wilson intervals for the principal counts. A threshold grid re-dates the first replicator in all 170 benign-tar worlds of 16–64 bytes from the recorded runs: the load–push word comes first in 170 of 170 for heredity thresholds 0.2–0.4, 155 of 169 at 0.5 and 107 of 164 at 0.6, because the pusher's own gen2 is 0.46–0.62 (Methods). The informative-partner threshold (0.65–0.85) changes nothing. The 246 smearing words of the census pass the threshold without producing a single 75% copy (Extended Data Fig. 2), which is why heredity is never claimed from gen2 alone.

**"Final dominant" (skeptical §7).** Defined in Methods as the most heritable of the three most common tapes; at L = 50 it is a stage of a lineage rather than a lineage, as stated; population claims now rest on random cells.

**Executed versus operand (skeptical §2, optimistic §9).** "Executed" now means fetched as instruction stream, opcode or operand, as stated in Methods and the legends; data reads are not recorded, and confinement concerns the instruction pointer only. Execution was traced for the unmutated parent, not for each mutant; the legends say so. We did not separate opcode from operand fetches.

**Code, data and registrations.** All are in the project repository with dated commits for every registration. [Access for the reviewers is the authors' decision and is arranged through the editor.]

## What we did not do

- One-, two- and three-jump constructs and reversions at L = 50: the claim they would test was withdrawn instead.
- Barcoded competition of the three transient genomes against their own successors, with and without mutation: we ran one head-to-head contest of a payload carrier against an evolved regenerator instead.
- Separate opcode, operand and data-read maps.

## Errors we found ourselves

- **L = 50 closure.** Revision 1 counted all 20 L = 50 worlds as closed, from the final dominant. Random cells and the lineage trace show that the closed tape is a passing stage: the closure count of Stage G falls from 67 to 47 of 80 worlds at the population level.
- **Variation from the most common tape.** Revision 1 measured variation on the most common tape, which selects regenerators. The claims that the first closed organisms are "nearly blind to their own mutants" and "stay canalised" do not hold for lethal-tar worlds and for some benign-tar worlds; they are replaced.
- **The constructed closer in our own first reading.** Our first look at the 8080 invasion soups took the most common tape (a tiled form holding under 1% of cells) for the population and concluded that the payload had been lost. Random cells showed the opposite; nothing of the first reading reached the manuscript.
- **Registered metrics that failed on definition.** The exact-core share of the constructed closer (the core mutates within a few hundred steps while its descendants hold the soup) and, as before, the nearest-class share at L = 50. Both are reported as misses, and the post hoc replacements are labelled.
