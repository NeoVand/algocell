**Substantive defects**

- **Panels a–b: the central outcome needs a short definition.** “Confined worlds have a first confined founder” is circular: the image never explains what makes a founder *confined*. Likewise, “later conversions” does not identify the transition being counted. “Open-first” receives a definition, but its relationship to confinement remains implicit. Add one concise operational definition of confinement and label the conversion’s starting and ending states. Full methods are unnecessary.

- **Panel a: the sampled-line population is ambiguous.** The table distinguishes 20 worlds from 17 or 20 confined worlds, but does not say whether the 1,088 and 1,280 lines come from all worlds or only confined worlds. This matters because those lines are nested observations, whereas panel b counts founders and events. Rename the column to identify the actual sampling population, or add a short sampling label directly beneath the table. The existing “Lines” column already supplies the denominator for “Open-first lines”; the missing information is which worlds contributed those lines.

- **Panel f, N6-5′: the mutation observation needs its counting unit and population.** “0.0064 expected” concerns mutation founders, while “55 recorded mutations” introduces a different count without identifying whether these are events, records or another unit, or where they were counted. The footer separately reports 0/20 mutation founders. Place that observed founder count alongside the expectation, and qualify the 55 count with its actual unit and population. This would distinguish the registered result from its supporting input without reproducing the separate proof.

**Layout defects and optional polish**

- **Panels a–d have excessive internal whitespace.** Short tables occupy their upper edges, while explanatory labels sit far below them. The gaps are especially conspicuous in a–c and visually detach the qualifications from their results. Compact these panels and place each definition immediately beneath its table. Use the recovered space to give e and f more room.

- **Panel f is comparatively compressed.** Multiple wrapped requirements and observations form a dense ledger beneath much sparser panels. Increase row separation and introduce subtle horizontal rules or alternating row shading. Align the corresponding observation lines consistently.

- **Panel f’s verdicts are visually underemphasized.** The orange N6-4 statement in d establishes a useful emphasis, but “not met” in the complete ledger looks almost identical to the surrounding “met” entries. Repeat the orange emphasis on that verdict and bold it.

- **Panel e’s low-value cluster obscures individual marks.** Several points near the lower-left overlap or nearly touch, making the stated one-point-per-founder representation difficult to audit visually. Smaller outlined markers or restrained transparency would improve separation. The image does **not** establish that any observations are missing.

- **Panel c’s dominant result could be easier to scan.** The 18-versus-2 split is substantive but visually understated. Adding “18/20” and “2/20,” optionally with a compact proportional bar, would make the result more immediate while retaining the category definitions and the distinction from causal source classes.

No panel is empty. The figure does not need full methods, the separate archived proofs, or the unrelated 8086-law statement.
