## Abstract

Darwinian evolution requires reproduction to transmit the differences on which selection acts. Self-copying programs arise spontaneously in machine-code soups, but their survival and spread do not show which parental differences their copying transmits. Using Zilion, an execution substrate for comparing copying mechanisms across processor architectures, we followed introduced differences through descendants. A representative early Z80 copier sustained lineages, yet none of 512 tested variants met the registered carriage criterion [results/branching/SUPP_TABLE.md]. For a recurring class of overlapping copiers, we derive a conditional count of freely transmissible byte positions set by an inherited displacement byte; registered Z80 interventions and eligible evolved 6502 copiers matched it [results/capacity/CAPACITY.md; results/evolved6502/RETENTION.md]. In 8086 populations the predicted emergence route failed and registered copying-law tests remained untested, although exploratory interventions agreed with the rule [results/s8086/tables/worlds.csv; results/s8086/tables/law_denominators.csv; results/s8086/exploratory/law_assay_summary.csv]. Independence from partner information, a strict analogue of one aspect of Woese’s Darwinian threshold, is therefore distinct from transmission of parental variation.

## Main text

Sustained evolution by natural selection requires heritable variation in reproductive success [1, 2]. Replication alone does not meet this requirement, because a system can reproduce reliably while passing on few of the differences that arise in it. Von Neumann’s self-reproducing automaton copies a description that it also interprets [3], and the distinction between limited and unlimited heredity asks how many alternatives a reproducing system can carry [2]. Some compositional systems return towards built-in states and fail to preserve selectable variants [4], whereas appropriately organized autocatalytic networks can support limited heredity and adaptation [5]. Woese placed the onset of Darwinian evolution proper at a threshold, where cellular organization became integrated enough for vertical descent to dominate the horizontal exchange of an earlier communal regime [6].

Self-reproduction and heredity have long been studied in cellular automata [7], in evolving machine-code systems such as Tierra and Avida [8, 9] and in Fontana’s algorithmic chemistry [10]. Earlier digital experiments also examined spontaneous origins and architecture-dependent evolvability [11, 12].

Artificial chemistries of interacting programs have made the origin of replication itself observable. In task-free program soups, self-replicators arise through mutation and interaction without an imposed fitness function [13, 14]; Cicala and colleagues also studied task-selected conditions, but our comparisons concern their task-free controls [14]. These studies trace where copied bytes come from and have already demonstrated load–push and block-copy architectures, faithfully copied random tails and robustness to repeated cycles of mutation and replication [13, 14].

We ask instead which introduced parental alternatives remain recoverable through successive descendants, and whether an inherited change in the copying operation alters that set. Abundance alone cannot answer this. A successful copier can restore its pattern so effectively that it erases most variants introduced into it, and faithful copying of an unmodified parent does not show which introduced variants would remain. We therefore track introduced differences separately from lineage survival, and derive a conditional count of independently variable positions for a specified family of overlapping copiers. To make such comparisons across processors, we built a substrate that traces copying operations in machines with different instruction sets.

Using this substrate, we show that in soups of Z80 machine code, what reproduction preserves is a measurable property of the copying mechanism. Branching populations of a representative first replicator persist while losing tested variants by the registered carriage criterion. In a recurring family of later copiers, a single geometric property of the copy decides which parental bytes reach descendants. The parameter that sets this geometry is itself inherited, and the chemistry of the environment shifts which designs prevail. Comparing processors then separates how replication begins from what it transmits.

### Measuring what reproduction preserves

Zilion separates a processor’s instruction semantics from the world in which programs meet (Fig. 1a). An encounter places two tapes of L bytes on a ring of 2L bytes, resets the processor to a declared state, executes a bounded number of execution steps starting in the first tape and writes both tapes back. The soup supplies encounters between lattice neighbours, together with background mutation. It has no fitness function and does not replace unsuccessful programs, so a program reproduces only if its execution writes, into some partner, a tape that reproduces in turn [algocell_exp/machines/; /Users/neo/repos/zilion/docs/ARCHITECTURE.md].

A versioned semantic contract specifies everything on which such a claim depends: address reduction on the ring, initial registers and stack phase, the unit of execution and the treatment of undefined instructions. The world supplies cell boundaries, execution time and reset state; the code that exploits them evolves. The distinction matters because an evolved copier can depend on a register value supplied at reset as strongly as on an instruction in its own tape (Methods).

We validated the processor cores against per-instruction reference tests: emulator-generated vectors for the Z80 and 6502 and hardware-recorded vectors for the 8088. The historical populations analysed here were produced with Algocell kernels, and we distinguish their assays from fresh reruns with the corrected Z80 core (core C1) and from current Zilion implementations. The corrected-core reruns retained 19 of 22 claim verdicts and changed 4 of 36 registered components; we report historical failures and corrected-core outcomes side by side [results/tier2/T2_CLAIMS.csv; results/tier2/T2_COMPONENTS.csv] (Fig. 1d; Methods).

Emergence curves date the culture-based detection of replicators (Fig. 1b), and one illustrative world shows the accompanying changes in sequence similarity (Fig. 1c). Isolated assays then ask what reproduction preserves (Fig. 2), using four measurements that we keep separate throughout. A culture test asks whether a tape’s offspring itself reproduces. An execution trace asks whether instruction-stream fetches stay within the replicator’s own bytes. Inflow, measured with the parent fixed, is the variation of offspring across partners: how much the partner contributes. Transfer assays follow a deliberately introduced parental difference through repeated reproduction.

These properties dissociate in both directions. A program can read a partner’s bytes without executing them, and it can execute partner addresses after overwriting their contents, so none of the measurements stands in for another (Methods).

### Carriage can fail despite survival

In all 80 historical worlds of the registered benign-chemistry cohort, the first detected replicator was open in the sampled encounters: its execution ran on into the partner it was overwriting [results/inflow_insoup/INFLOW_INSOUP.md, X2-2]. Its most common form tiles the tape with `01 c5` [results/stageG/stageG/stage_g_runs.csv], a load–push motif reported for the 8080 by Agüera y Arcas and colleagues and subsequently assayed in the Z80 by Cicala and colleagues [13, 14]. The tile decodes as `01 c5 01`, the instruction `LD BC,$01C5`, followed by `c5`, `PUSH BC`, so the same byte values serve both as opcodes and as the literal data pushed into the partner’s memory.

To ask what such a replicator transmits, we carried out a registered branching-transfer experiment (X1), in which every surviving member of a population reproduced against two fresh partners per generation, so that loss of a mutation could be distinguished from extinction of its lineage (Fig. 2a). Under uniform random partners, 48 of 64 unmutated populations of the 16-byte pusher survived for eight generations. Among mutant populations, 38% were still alive at generation eight, yet for none of the 512 tested mutation types did a population meet the registered criterion for carrying its mutation. The registered prediction that the open replicator would retain heritable variation (X1-2) therefore failed, and we report it as failed [results/branching/branching_summary.csv, pusher16, arm U; results/branching/SUPP_TABLE.md; REVISION_PREREG.md, X1]. Failure of this majority-carriage criterion does not establish that every copy of a marker was lost; it shows that the pusher rebuilds its form while losing these variants by the registered criterion.

Three confined Z80 replicators gave contrasting results under the same protocol (Fig. 2a). Two regenerators, one returning control to itself and one using a block copy, preserved every unmutated population and scored no transmissible site, whereas an evolved block-copy transmitter carried nine scored sites through all eight generations [results/branching/branching_summary.csv, ret16, ldir32, genome20_s6003]. A site scores when at least half of the tested identifiable alternative values at that position meet the registered retention criterion. This standard is demanding, and rare variants can persist below it: the 64-byte pusher retained 5 of 2,048 tested mutant types with uniform partners, and the block-copy regenerator retained a few working core variants [results/branching/SUPP_TABLE.md].

The contrast is therefore not between all and nothing but between copiers that preserve broad classes of alternatives at specific positions and copiers that preserve almost none. The confined regenerators and the transmitter differ in what reproduction preserves despite identical survival of their unmutated populations. Historical serial transfers and byte maps show the corresponding retention contrast (Fig. 2b,c), and an ordered trace follows an introduced byte through its overwrite and the descendant’s next reproduction (Fig. 2d). We next ask what, in the copying operation, decides which differences survive.

### Overlap sets what is inherited

For the most tractable family of evolved copiers, the answer is geometric (Fig. 3a). Consider a forward copy that begins at the parent’s first byte and writes each byte a fixed displacement d ahead of where it was read, where d divides the tape length L, the ordered writes cover the ring and no other write interferes. The first d reads obtain original parental bytes. Thereafter the read pointer reaches bytes that have already been written, so it copies copies: the first d bytes propagate around the whole ring, and every byte beyond them is overwritten before it can be read. A small displacement therefore regenerates a short pattern regardless of what else the parent held, whereas a displacement spanning the whole parent reproduces the entire parental tape.

With the copying core of c bytes held fixed inside the first d, and under the stated initialization, offspring alignment and forward coverage, the number of independently free payload positions is r = d − c [theorem: manuscript/SI_v8.md, Supplementary Note 1; Methods]. The proof traces every byte of the final ring back to its source and holds for jointly arbitrary payload values. It counts free payload only; working variants of the core itself are additional possibilities that it does not count.

The Z80 soups evolved copiers with this geometry. In the four-byte core `XX 5e ed b0`, the first byte executes as a harmless instruction and is then read as data by `LD E,(HL)` to set the displacement, and `LDIR` performs the overlapping copy. The variant `1e NN ed b0` carries the displacement as an immediate operand. Cicala and colleagues described the four-byte immediate-displacement copier and offset-reading block-copy architectures [14]; here we derive and test the conditional payload count r = d − 4 [theorem: manuscript/SI_v8.md, Supplementary Note 1]. The core occurs in 2,347 of 15,655 sampled confined heritable cells, so it is a recurring architecture rather than the whole ecology [results/offset/CORE_FAMILIES.md].

In an exploratory regrouping of the cells that carry this motif with a displacement dividing the tape length (Fig. 3b), sampled site scores closely match the prediction. Of 2,343 such cells, 2,342 had exactly d − 4 scored unexecuted transmissible positions. In all 53 cells whose total score exceeded d − 4, the additional sites lay at positions fetched as part of the instruction stream. The single deficit preserves its predicted payload in targeted transfers but fails the whole-tape similarity gate of the population assay [exploratory: results/crystal/cells.csv, results/crystal/cell_exceptions.csv, results/crystal/offset_examples.csv; results/offset/offset_cells.csv]. Motif and divisibility alone do not certify every hypothesis of the theorem, and these are sampled scores, not exhaustive capacities (Methods).

The law also corrects a tempting intuition: what matters is the displacement of the copy, not the visible period of the tape. One evolved tape repeats every four bytes, all of them core, yet has six scored transmissible sites, four of them unexecuted payload positions, because its copy displacement is eight [exploratory: results/crystal/cell_exceptions.csv, K, L = 32, seed 5006]. Hereditary capacity belongs to the family of sequences that a copying operation preserves, not to the appearance of any one member.

The same operation separates two properties that are easily conflated. Under the theorem’s hypotheses, every byte of the offspring descends from the parent’s protected prefix, whatever the partner initially held. Such an overlapping regenerator can therefore exclude partner information entirely while erasing variation outside its protected prefix. Closure removes an external source of difference; transmission preserves an internal one.

### The copying rule is inherited

If the displacement is encoded in the replicator’s own bytes, it should be copied with the core, and a change in it should be inherited. Archived serial-transfer interventions on an evolved regenerator, ldir32, show that it is: when its displacement byte was changed from `0x04` to `0x08` or to `0x60`, the mutant markers persisted through eight transfers [results/serial_retention/sr_mutants.csv.gz, ldir32, gen 8, pos 0, val 8 and 96]. Under the theorem these changes predict more free payload, and the Z80 displacement study (registered experiment C1) measured that capacity directly (Fig. 3b,c).

From 22 archived parents we made 101 frozen displacement settings, including original-value controls, with forward and reciprocal passages. Eligibility and predicted coordinates were fixed from code and traces before retention outcomes were observed. In 16 lineages per setting and direction, followed for eight generations, the edited displacement and the whole core were inherited. Every tested alternative at each predicted free coordinate in actual generation-eight descendants was transmitted, sampled joint assignments were transmitted, and erased-coordinate controls lost their original ancestry. Across 1,067,608 direct cases there was no mismatch, with fixed-core capacities from 0 to 60 positions [registered: results/capacity/edit_eligibility.csv; results/capacity/serial.csv.gz; results/capacity/capacity_cases.csv.gz; results/capacity/CAPACITY.md]. An inherited change to a copier with no free payload can thus permit most of its tape to vary, and a reciprocal edit restores the original erasure.

An exploratory scan of all 256 values of this byte shows how wide the dial is (Fig. 3d) [exploratory: results/crystal/alleles_ldir32.csv]. Ninety-nine values give a working replicator, and 50 of these match the edited value at the aligned position in at least half of first-generation offspring; such equality alone does not certify serial inheritance. The 11 values that pass the sampled eligibility checks span displacements of 4 to 32 bytes and predicted payloads of 0 to 28 positions. The dial is specific to this family: in the same exploratory scan, an evolved transmitter that copies backwards with `LDDR` had only 15 working values, and it lies outside the forward theorem [results/crystal/alleles_genome20_s6003.csv].

This is an evolved instance of an old architectural idea. Von Neumann’s automaton requires a description that is both used and copied [3]; the displacement byte is used to construct the copy and is copied along with it. Within this copying family it is a heritable trait whose predicted phenotype is the capacity to transmit arbitrary payload. We do not claim that selection favours it for that reason: whether a larger payload benefits a lineage depends on the environment.

### Environments sort hereditary designs

The competitions below use two frozen, census-defined byte classes, R and T, corresponding to partial-copy (d < L) and full-tape (d = L) settings; these differ from the site-score classes used in random-population assays (Methods). Without mutation, final shares in mixtures varied widely between worlds, and the registered E1 stability criterion failed: only 4 of 10 corrected-core benign worlds at each length ended in the 0.3–0.7 interval, against the required 7 of 10. This pattern is consistent with drift but does not establish neutrality [results/tier2/scored/E/REPORT.md, mutation off; results/tier2/T2_COMPONENTS.csv, E1]. The registered mutation-on prediction E2 also failed: the T share was to fall below its start and end between 0.05 and 0.4, but did so in only 2 of 10 benign worlds at L = 16, 0 of 10 lethal worlds at L = 16 and 5 of 10 benign worlds at L = 32, against the required 7 of 10. At L = 16 under benign chemistry, core-carrying cells fell to a median of 5% of all cells, so the share is not interpretable there [results/tier2/T2_COMPONENTS.csv, E2; results/tier2/scored/E/REPORT.md, L = 16, mutation on]. With mutation, the chemistry of the zero byte shifts their relative abundance. In the corrected-core reruns of the registered competitions at tape length 32, started with equal shares, median full-tape-class shares, T/(R + T), were 0.05 under benign chemistry, where a zero opcode executes harmlessly, and 0.76 under lethal chemistry, where fetching a zero opcode halts execution. All ten lethal worlds exceeded one half, meeting the registered endpoint [results/tier2/scored/E/REPORT.md, L = 32, mutation on; results/tier2/scored/E/offset_switch.csv; results/tier2/T2_COMPONENTS.csv, E-L32L] (Fig. 4a,b).

Both invasion directions ended at similar environment-specific medians, with five worlds per direction: 0.09 and 0.08 under benign chemistry and 0.81 and 0.77 under lethal chemistry. The benign T-into-R median of 0.08 uses four defined ratios because one world has R + T = 0; that world retains zero absolute abundance and tail diversity in Fig. 4b [manuscript/figures/out/v8/data/competition_endpoints.csv]. These finite-horizon endpoints are consistent with convergence from either rare starting design but do not establish equilibrium [results/tier2/scored/E/REPORT.md, L = 32]. In the corrected-core lethality dial, transmitters formed a majority of heritable cells in 8 of 10 fully lethal worlds, against 6 of 10 historically, when this registered endpoint failed [results/tier2/T2_DZ.csv; REVISION_PREREG.md, DZ3].

The lethality dial also changes observed route frequencies and confinement times (Fig. 4c), although it does not impose a fixed sequence. With the corrected core, median times to first confinement rose with lethality apart from a single reversal, meeting a registered timing criterion that the historical run had failed with two reversals; the registered endpoint is biased against confinement at high lethality (Methods) [results/tier2/T2.md, DZ2; REVISION_PREREG.md, DZ2]. In an exploratory reanalysis of historical benign worlds, regenerators were the final majority of heritable cells in 17 of 20 worlds at L = 16 and 14 of 20 at L = 20, whereas at L = 50 open cells remained the majority in all 20 worlds. Among the 42 benign worlds containing both classes, regenerators were detected first in 30, an order of detection rather than of ancestry [exploratory: results/ladder/population_worlds.csv; results/ladder/LADDER.md]. Under lethal chemistry the picture differs. In an exploratory assay of the separate historical lethal-chemistry cohort, all ten first detected tapes were confined, had zero sampled partner inflow and carried at most four scored sites, yet by the final sample transmitters were a majority of heritable cells in nine of the ten worlds [exploratory: results/crystal/first_cells.csv, Z80 I; results/ladder/population_worlds.csv, stage I].

Why lethal chemistry favours transmitters remains open. Registered tests failed to explain the environmental balance by the specified toxin effect, by backup cores or by isolated mutational flows; halting payload bytes provided a small protective effect, below the registered explanatory threshold [results/toxin/CAUSAL.md; results/n2/BACKUP.md; results/offset/OFFSET_FLOW.md; REVISION_PREREG.md, N1, N2 and E-mech]. Exploratory encounter assays locate an asymmetry in how the two designs fare against core-free intruders, but they do not yet explain the balance [exploratory: results/offset/INTRUDER.md]. Because a tape is both a copier and material that other programs execute and overwrite, what a replicator preserves changes what its neighbours encounter. A mechanism for the balance will have to predict how the balance moves after an intervention.

### Available copiers need not emerge

The comparison across processors separates an available copying mechanism from the route that evolution takes (Fig. 5a–c). The 6502’s accumulator load–push channel is one byte wide. A retrospectively derived theorem excludes an infinite periodic self-writer in this single-register grammar, but not every finite encounter or every instruction family [theorem: results/literal_channel/CHECK.md]. Before the soups were run, a validated census found no nonzero exact zero-partner self-writer of period one to four at L = 16 under the declared 128- and 512-step arms [results/census6502/table_main.csv]. In registered S1 primary arm A, all 20 first detected replicators were confined, with a median detection time of 1,125,000 updates. Only 14 met the additional registered loop criterion, so prediction P1 was not supported; the ring-position explanation of the remaining six is post hoc [results/s6502/v2/arm_comparison.csv; results/s6502/CORRECTION.md].

The copying law nevertheless transferred. Constructed 6502 controls gave the predicted sampled carriage in 296 qualifying cases, which are distinct from exact capacities. The complete panel of the 6502 retention study (registered experiment C2) sampled 942 roles from all 80 S1 worlds and, by frozen code-and-provenance rules, admitted 42 sampling roles representing 41 distinct tapes from 11 worlds. Their constrained footprints spanned 6–10 bytes and predicted payloads 0–9 positions. All tested predictions held through generation eight, with no mismatch in direct or serial assays (Fig. 3b; Methods) [construction: results/census6502/table_closers_law.csv; registered: results/evolved6502/eligibility.csv; results/evolved6502/geometry.csv; results/evolved6502/law_verdict.csv; results/evolved6502/exact_cases.csv.gz; results/evolved6502/exact_serial.csv.gz]. Roles and interventions are nested observations, not independent worlds.

The coherent-fetch 8086 supplied the prospective challenge. Its exact pusher was available: under the frozen reset, the tile `B9 51` decodes as `MOV CX,$B951; PUSH CX` [results/census8086/table_hits.csv; algocell_exp/machines/worlds_8086.json]. Yet its first detected replicators were predominantly confined (Fig. 5a,c). Registered study S2 found copiers in 14 of 20 worlds, meeting R0, but only 2 of those 14 began open, failing R1; the 12 confined beginnings triggered the route kill rule, and the census-motivated open-beginning claim is withdrawn. Detection had a Kaplan–Meier median of 652,500 updates, with six worlds censored at the fixed horizon (Fig. 5b); the timing comparison is descriptive [registered: results/s8086/tables/worlds.csv; results/s8086/tables/kaplan_meier.csv; results/s8086/S2.md]. Availability had supplied a possible route, not its probability of arrival or establishment.

The registered law tests did not resolve the question. L1 found no eligible evolved member of its narrow REP family and is untested. Amended L2 also admitted none: 724 certificates remained unresolved, 708 of them at the unmodelled `POP ES`. L2 is therefore untested with support suspended; it is neither a law failure nor a pass with no mismatches. The R2 measures of mechanism occurrence and persistence were demoted to descriptive status before population data were obtained (Methods) [registered: results/s8086/tables/law_denominators.csv; results/s8086/tables/law_candidates.csv; results/s8086/S2.md].

Exploratory, post hoc follow-ups address both the missing pusher and what the frozen extractor missed. No full `B9 51` tile in either phase, nor any alternating run of at least four bytes, was observed in 11,700 scheduled top-three sample records or 2,000,000 checkpoint cells. The archive therefore suggests a bottleneck in arrival or establishment, although its historical cause is not identifiable. Planted-pusher encounters show phase- and role-dependent vulnerability, not a measured population growth rate [exploratory: results/s8086/exploratory/motif_scan.csv.gz; results/s8086/exploratory/encounter_fates.csv; results/s8086/EXPLORATORY.md].

The dominant first-copier family instead evolved the same regenerating overlap geometry as the Z80, on a different instruction set. Ten of the 14 first copiers tile `5F A4 x y` (Fig. 5d). Post hoc tracing identifies `POP DI; MOVSB; branch`: the stack read initializes a displacement d = 4, and subsequent copies propagate the first four parental bytes. With this four-byte footprint fixed, free payload is zero, yet alternative branch bytes are transmitted through eight passages. Zero free payload is again not zero heritable variation [exploratory: results/s8086/exploratory/first_family.csv; results/s8086/exploratory/first_family_copies.csv; results/s8086/exploratory/family_transmission.csv; results/s8086/exploratory/family_serial.csv.gz].

Extending the dependency verifier post hoc admitted 600 genotype–world candidates: 590 full-tape d = 16 copiers and 10 d = 4 regenerators. Predictions were committed before assays on a purposive panel of 32 genotypes from 20 worlds, and every selected case agreed, with no mismatch in 2,224,640 encounters. The full-tape cases preserve arbitrary tested payload under held footprints; they are not smaller-seed regenerators. These results support transfer of the geometric explanation while leaving registered L1 and L2 untested [exploratory: results/s8086/exploratory/eligibility_counts.csv; results/s8086/exploratory/eligibility.csv; results/s8086/exploratory/law_panel_predictions.csv; results/s8086/exploratory/law_assay_denominators.csv; results/s8086/exploratory/law_assay_summary.csv].

### Closure and heredity are distinct

In a recurring family of spontaneous copiers, overlap determines which parental bytes reach descendants; an inherited displacement controls free payload, and chemistry shifts the prevalence of the resulting designs. The processor comparison sharpens this claim. The prospective 8086 route prediction failed despite an available exact pusher, whereas exploratory analysis recovered the same overlap geometry with exact post hoc agreement. Availability does not decide the route; the copying law transfers where its geometric and execution conditions are met. A successful explanation of transmission need not be a successful prediction of evolutionary arrival.

The results separate closure to partner information from broad transmission of parental variation. With the parent and encounter contract fixed, closure means that offspring no longer depend on the partner’s initial contents. A qualifying regenerator can achieve closure while preserving few tested alternatives, among them working core alleles, whereas transmitters preserve broad classes of variants at specific sites. Zero partner inflow is our deliberately strict analogue of Woese’s transition towards stable vertical descent; Woese did not require horizontal transfer to disappear [6]. Confinement alone does not establish this closure, and the operational criterion is neither a necessary condition for Darwinian evolution nor a reconstruction of the historical cellular transition. Within these limits the Darwinian-threshold framing has content: stable, genealogically continuous reproduction can coexist with limited transmission of the variation on which selection could act.

The same distinction matters wherever replication is studied without genes. Recovery of a sequence or composition could reflect transmission, reconstruction or recapture from the environment. Following an introduced difference separately from the survival of its carriers, and identifying the source of recovered material, distinguishes these possibilities. We do not claim that chemistry uses a displacement byte. Ours is an inspectable case in which it is exactly decidable which alternatives reproduction preserves and which its own operation removes.

The remaining limits are substantive. Exact registered support comes from edited Z80 descendants and the eligible evolved 6502 panel; the 8086 follow-ups are exploratory and require a new prospective test with adequate extraction coverage. Finite joint challenges do not enumerate every possible payload, and neither useful payload function nor selection for increased capacity has been demonstrated: to establish adaptive use, an inherited difference must alter performance in a specified environment. Material that is both copied and interpreted, as in von Neumann’s description, offers one route; whether such use evolves in these soups without an imposed task remains open [3]. Zilion makes these questions testable across explicitly specified processor contracts: not only whether a program reproduces, but which differences its reproduction preserves.

## Methods

### Execution contracts

Zilion separates instruction cores, shared encounter interfaces, execution engines and machine manifests. Research code implements population pairing, mutation and scientific classifications. Manifests bind semantic version, compatibility, chip variant, generated shader and host identities to reset, address mapping, budget, traps and observations. Historical Algocell populations and assays retain their actual kernels; fresh reruns with corrected cores are reported separately. Architecture and machine-specific contracts are archived separately from experiment results [algocell_exp/machines/; /Users/neo/repos/zilion/docs/ARCHITECTURE.md].

An encounter places focal tape A and partner tape B on a ring, resets the processor, enters A, executes its budget and writes both halves back. Architectural wrapping precedes ring reduction; a wrap need not advance to the neighbouring ring byte for arbitrary ring sizes. The historical Z80 resets registers and flags to zero except SP, set to the largest architectural address aliasing the partner’s last byte. That pointer convention does not itself specify the first written byte. Fetch maps include prefixes, operands and displacements, not merely instruction starts. Repeating block moves consume budget per repetition; I/O reads return zero, writes have no external effect and no external interrupts occur. A fetched zero opcode executes normally in benign chemistry; in lethal chemistry, a fetched zero opcode halts. The 8080 subset is an ablation within this core, not independent hardware validation [results/emulator/RING_DIFFTEST.md; algocell_exp/shader/z80_test_L16.wgsl; algocell_exp/shader/z80_test_lethal_L16.wgsl; results/review_r2/RUN_TABLE.md].

The NMOS 6502 starts with clear accumulator/index registers, status `20` hexadecimal and a first push aligned to the partner’s last byte. Zero-page and stack-page architectural accesses map into the ring. Faithful BRK vectoring and halt-before-BRK are separate contracts; JAM halts, without external IRQ/NMI. BFF’s published contract halts on unmatched brackets and pointer run-off; its literal, bandwidth, pointer and halting variants remain separate [results/s6502/CORRECTION.md; algocell_exp/machines/m6502_pair_maps_L16.wgsl; results/bff/runs.csv].

### Coherent-fetch 8086

S2 used the Zilion 8086 core frozen at public commit `05bd6fa2219c90b34574189864631a42ef80ea66` (recorded in the frozen manifests under its byte-identical pre-publication identity `39a60429a9b16c77cd845f6087062c4e2cc12c53` [algocell_exp/machines/ZILION_PUBLIC_COMMITS.md]), compatibility `0.3`, with `prefetch=coherent` and `hidden_latch=zero_ea`; package status was experimental. ### Forward-overlap theorem

**Theorem.** On a ring of P bytes, perform ordered writes M[s+d+k] ← M[s+k], modulo P, with 0 < d < P and no interfering operation. Before k = d, reads obtain original source bytes; subsequently each source was last written d assignments earlier. Induction gives ancestral coordinate s+(k mod d). With d dividing P and coverage at least P−d, the protected seed tiles the ring; with P = 2L and d dividing L, the offspring has the required phase. If a fixed constrained footprint C lies in the protected seed, independent free payload is r = d−|C|. This describes a product family of 256^r sequences, or 8r ideal noiseless bits, not ecological neutrality or measured Shannon capacity [theorem: manuscript/SI_v8.md, Supplementary Note 1; results/capacity/geometry_traces.csv.gz].

For the Z80 `XX 5e ed b0` specialization, L ≤ 64, d = XX mod 2L, 4 ≤ d ≤ L and d divides L. The first instruction must pass control to address 1, preserve the needed source/base state, write only outside the protected prefix and leave HL ≡ 0 and 256D ≡ 0 modulo 2L, with BC zero or at least 126. Under the declared initialization, 126 block moves remain; the immediate `1e NN ed b0` form leaves 127. Architectural address increments must preserve displacement. Core preservation keeps execution in the copy when overwritten instructions are revisited. Free coordinates then survive jointly and serially; reverse `LDDR` requires another derivation [theorem: manuscript/SI_v8.md, Supplementary Note 1, Z80 specialization; results/capacity/candidate_eligibility.csv].

Working core alleles lie outside fixed-footprint payload. Visible sequence period is not displacement. Motif/divisibility screening supplies candidates, not every proof hypothesis. The exploratory population comparison retains its whole-tape gate deficit and additional fetched-site scores rather than redefining the theorem around measured sites [results/crystal/cell_exceptions.csv; results/crystal/offset_examples.csv; results/offset/offset_cells.csv].

### Literal-channel results

**Theorem.** A repeated pair with distinct one-byte load/push opcodes loads w literal bytes into one register and pushes a fixed permutation without changing it, using a separate stack pointer that moves by −w per push. Under contiguous noninterfering writes and the prescribed executed stream, input/output periods are w+2 and w. The Fine–Wilf periodicity theorem [15] requires N ≥ 2w+2−gcd(2,w) for finite-window equality. Odd widths then force identical opcodes, a contradiction; even widths permit compatible alternating constructions. Descending stacks reverse block order in ascending memory, not internal byte order. Placement, bootstrap and coverage remain separate [theorem: results/literal_channel/CHECK.md; results/literal_channel/pair_layouts.csv; results/literal_channel/finite_bound_counterexamples.json].

**Theorem.** For width-one periodic mixtures through a shared register, a minimal decoder cycle visits at most d residues of primitive input period d. If it contains loads and pushes, emitted length n_P satisfies 0 < n_P < t ≤ d, incompatible with d dividing n_P for an equal infinite stream. Multiple opcode identities do not change this. The degenerate all-push writer initialized with an opcode-valued register is excluded. Width-three mixtures provide counterexamples to extending parity beyond repeated pairs. A finite width-one factor witness exists but fails archived zero-partner bootstrap. Only three of the five nontrivial Z80 period-two writers are literal; the others are memory-fed. None of these statements proves an evolutionary arrival order or excludes every finite 6502 copier [theorem/construction: results/literal_channel/verification.csv; results/literal_channel/finite_search.csv; results/literal_channel/6502_finite_harness.json; results/literal_channel/census_audit.csv].

Competition summaries use world-level medians; ratios with R+T = 0 are undefined and excluded only from ratio summaries. Sample sizes and exclusions are given for each cohort. The benign corrected-core T-into-R subgroup has four defined ratios from five worlds.
