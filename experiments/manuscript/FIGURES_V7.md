### fig1

The new figure is comparably polished and information-rich, but **panel d still falls short in self-explanation**. Its numerical agreement is readable; the scientific outcomes being compared are not sufficiently identified.

At 180 mm width, the main text and ticks are broadly consistent with the references’ typography. I see no clear clipping, text collisions, empty panels, or placeholders. The space beneath panel b reads as uneven spacing, not missing data. The added explanatory labels are useful and should be retained.

**Substantive defect**

- **Panel d — the counted outcomes need short definitions.** “Ring outcomes,” “Assay outcomes,” “Registered verdicts,” and “Registered subtests” identify evaluation categories without identifying the scored event or property. The notes establish the comparison—original Z80 versus corrected core—and explain that red means changed, but readers still cannot tell what those changes concern. “Verdicts count registered predictions” does not resolve this. Unlike the references’ explicitly named endpoints, such as lineage survival and allele carriage, these rows require an external caption to understand their scientific result.  
  **Fix:** Expand each row label or add a compact secondary description naming its scored endpoint. Briefly distinguish a registered verdict from a registered subtest. Full protocols and preregistration details can remain in the manuscript legend.

Panel d’s **area encoding itself is sound**: the visible segment proportions agree with the printed fractions, denominators are provided, and the normalization is explained. The extremely narrow ochre segment in the first row follows from its near-total agreement; it is not evidence of missing content.

**Optional polish**

- **Panel a:** “e3 swaps stack memory” leaves the other side of the exchange unnamed. Identify what is exchanged with stack memory, and introduce BC as a register. This would improve the mechanism explanation without adding a full instruction-set lesson.
- **Panel c:** The three-item legend stretches beyond the plot toward panel d. Keep it more clearly within c’s territory, using tighter spacing or two lines. “Zero-valued bytes” would also read more naturally than “Zero-valued / bytes” under the shared Fraction axis.
- **Panel c:** Identifying the illustrative world’s tape length and explicitly connecting its archive to panel b would improve conceptual provenance. The existing “one archive seed” and “separate from a” notes already help.
- **Panel d:** Add spaces around the slashes in the numeric column, especially the long first fraction, to improve scanning at print size.
- **Overall:** The comparatively large blank area below b could be reduced through modest vertical rebalancing. Preserve the informative notes and populated panels.

The figure reaches the references’ level of visual polish and information density. The unresolved endpoint definitions in d prevent it from reaching their level of self-explanation.

### fig2

**Below standard, narrowly.** The new figure matches the reference’s information density, and its typography is comparably readable at 180 mm. Every panel contains substantive content. The category headers, numeric columns, inline definitions and site tracks are effective. The remaining substantive defects concern panel d’s self-explanation.

**Substantive defects**

- **Panel d: the address system is undefined.** The block-copy design is labelled \(L=32\), but “Ring write address” runs to 64, with repeated sweeps before and after the offspring transition at step 128. The image does not explain what occupies this address space or what each wrap represents. **Fix:** identify the ring’s constituent regions, label their address boundaries and explain the wrap. Distinguish address wrapping from the parent-to-offspring transition.

- **Panel d: the causal erasure event is insufficiently identified.** The orange circle, “Address 8: 0xA7 replaced by 0x04,” “Instruction 6; copy offset = 4 bytes,” and the fall from one marker-derived byte to zero appear related, but their relationship is not explicit. In particular, the replacement label could describe either the introduced mutation or the subsequent overwrite. The address trace shows writing activity without identifying the source that causes erasure. **Fix:** distinguish the initial mutation from the erasing write, directly label the circled event, and give the relevant source → destination relationship. Connect that event to the loss in the adjacent trace.

- **Panel d: “marker-derived bytes” needs a local definition.** Panel b defines a marker as a five-byte mutant window, whereas panel d begins with one marker-derived byte. “Ancestry, not value match” usefully identifies the measurement principle but does not establish what was initially ancestry-labelled. **Fix:** state what receives the initial ancestry label and clarify whether this measurement differs from the window-retention measurement in panel b. Rename the axis accordingly if appropriate.

**Optional polish**

- Harmonize panel c’s “Byte executed / Never executed” key with its “Fetched bytes” column and opcode-fetch explanation. Consistent wording would remove an unnecessary interpretive step.
- Expand “Sites g8” to “Sites at generation 8”; there is room for a two-line header.
- Give panel d’s x-axis labels and the final explanatory footer slightly more separation.
- Replace “pusher retention prediction contradicted” with a brief statement of the predicted and observed outcomes, making the comparison more immediate.

The figure is informative and largely polished, but panel d still requires readers to supply essential connections between the mechanism, coordinates and measured outcome. Resolving those connections would bring its self-explanation up to the supplied reference standard.

### fig3

**The new figure is comparably informative, but does not yet match the references in self-explanation.** All four panels contain substantial content; none is empty or a placeholder. The mechanism diagrams, intervention example, and complete displacement grid are valuable. At 180 mm width, the typography appears broadly comparable to the references’ 5–7 pt text. I see no clear clipping or text overlap.

The substantive defects are concentrated in the definitions of measurements.

1. **Panel b: the vertical axis does not clearly accommodate all plotted measurements.** “Exact capacity / sampled sites (bytes)” identifies two quantities, while the key also introduces “6502 carriage: 296 controls.” The image does not explain what a carriage control contributes to this axis or how its measurement differs from exact capacity. This matters because agreement with the prediction is the panel’s main result.  
   **Fix:** Give each series an explicit measured quantity in the key—for example, distinguish exact transmissible positions, sampled qualifying positions, and carried positions, using the scientifically correct definitions. Use a common axis title only if all three legitimately share that interpretation.

2. **Panel b: the exceptional point remains conceptually ambiguous.** The key calls it a “sampled similarity-gate deficit,” whereas its annotation says “Tape gate fails: 0 score, 4 carried.” Neither gate is defined, and the change in terminology obscures whether these are the same criterion. “Score” also appears on an axis otherwise expressed as byte positions. A reader can locate the exception but cannot confidently explain what the zero represents or why four carried positions are plotted there.  
   **Fix:** Use one gate name consistently and explicitly distinguish the reported sampled count from independently observed carriage. A short statement of what the gate tests is enough; the full protocol is unnecessary. If “score” is a different quantity from the axis measurement, correct the plotting or labeling accordingly.

3. **Panel d: the criterion separating working from nonworking values is insufficiently defined.** “Gen-2 match gain / available headroom ≥ 0.3” supplies a threshold, but does not identify what is matched, the baseline for “gain,” or what constitutes the available headroom. Consequently, the reader cannot interpret the scientific meaning of the 157 versus 99 classification from the image. The exhaustive grid and category counts themselves are clear and useful.  
   **Fix:** Name the matching target and baseline in the subtitle or a compact inline definition. Define headroom as the remaining possible improvement in that same measurement, if that is the intended meaning. Retain the threshold and counts.

Optional polish:

- **Panel c:** “Core preserved” sits beneath an example that explicitly edits byte 0, within the displayed core. Clarify that preservation is relative to the edited parent, if intended. The surrounding context makes this inferable, but the wording could be sharper.
- **Panel b:** The one-cell size-key disc is barely visible at print scale. A fine leader identifying its location would help without distorting the area encoding.

The figure already meets the references’ standard for information density and largely matches their visual polish. Resolving the measurement definitions above would bring its self-explanation to the same level.

### fig4

**The new figure is densely informative and close to the references in polish, but it falls short in self-explanation.** Its typography is broadly consistent with the supplied 5–7 pt publication standard at 180 mm width. There are no empty or placeholder panels, conspicuous gaps suggesting missing results, or evident clipping or overlapping labels.

Two substantive defects remain:

1. **Panel c: the Kaplan–Meier row does not identify the event being timed.** “KM median (thousands of updates)” supplies the unit, and the footnote supplies censoring information, but neither explicitly states what must happen to register an event. The separate definition of “Confinement” suggests a connection without establishing it. Consequently, readers cannot confidently interpret what the differences between 32.5, 212.5 and 115 mean.
   
   **Fix:** Name the event in the row label—for example, “Median updates to first confined copier” **if that is the intended endpoint**—and explicitly tie the Kaplan–Meier/censoring note to that event. Keep the existing numerical detail.

2. **Panels a–c: “core” has competing meanings that obscure the provenance of the comparison.** Panel a identifies `5e ed b0` as the “copy core” and measures “core-bearing” cells. Panel b then calls filled symbols “corrected core,” while panel c specifies “10 worlds per core” but reports C1 versus archive. Elsewhere, C1/archive are described through instruction flags, refresh and execution semantics. Readers therefore have to resolve whether “core” means the copied sequence or the execution implementation—and whether the sequence, implementation, or both changed.
   
   **Fix:** Consistently name C1/archive as execution implementations or versions, using the scientifically correct term. In b, replace “corrected core” with “C1”; in c, replace “per core” with “per implementation” if appropriate. Reserve “copy core” and “core-bearing” for the sequence-based measurement. The existing flags/refresh explanation can remain.

Optional polish:

- **Panel a terminology:** Expand R and T once as “regenerator (R)” and “transmitter (T),” alongside the useful short-pattern/payload descriptions. The mechanisms are described, but the category names shown in the references disappear.
- **Panel a trajectory discrimination:** Where six curves converge, the light/dark and dashed/dotted distinctions become difficult to follow at print size. Slightly stronger archive contrast and clearer spacing of open versus filled markers would help. The numerical summaries and panel b already make the endpoints readable.
- **Panel a numerical summaries:** Align the three rows into compact columns headed “T share” and “core-bearing share,” with C1/archive subcolumns. This would preserve the desired information density while reducing punctuation decoding.

The figure matches the references’ information richness and largely matches their visual polish. The unspecified time-to-event endpoint and overloaded “core” terminology prevent it from matching their self-explanation.
