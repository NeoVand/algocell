# Adversarial pre-submission review of MAIN_v7

Reviewed 12 October 2026. This review concerns `MAIN_v7.md`, both exported `LEGENDS_V8.md` copies, the five supplied v8 figures, their plotting inputs, the cited result/status records, `REVISION_PREREG.md`, and relevant execution/scoring code. Literature and journal guidance were checked against primary sources. No manuscript, figure, experiment or registration was changed.

Line numbers below refer to the reviewed manuscript, SHA-256 `c4fd7b5e15d24a993b8a4a328735241c935383ea45857fe915683dcccd906866`. Repository paths beginning `results/`, `manuscript/` or `algocell_exp/` resolve from `experiments/`. External links are evidence, not substitutes for the missing manuscript bibliography.

**Verdict: not submittable as supplied.** The central quantitative results largely survive this audit. The most consequential remaining defects are measurement labels that overstate erasure, two incompatible R/T classifications, an overbroad novelty claim, incomplete Methods details, and an unfinished reference/data/supplement package. The registered 8086 failures and untested law verdicts are mostly handled correctly; they should not be rewritten as successes. Existing bounded claims do not require a new experiment merely to fix this draft.

### Proposed abstract A

Reproduction supports Darwinian evolution only if descendants inherit differences on which selection can act. Interacting programs offer a tractable setting for asking what spontaneous replicators transmit. Here we introduce Zilion, an execution substrate for comparing copying mechanisms across processor architectures. Populations founded by a representative early Z80 replicator survived eight generations, yet none of 512 tested variants met the registered carriage criterion. In a recurring forward-copy family, the distance between reading and writing determines how many positions outside the copying core can vary independently. This distance is encoded in an inherited byte. Registered Z80 interventions and eligible evolved 6502 copiers agreed with the conditional geometric prediction. Environmental changes shifted the prevalence of copying designs. Availability did not predict evolutionary arrival: an exact 8086 pusher was available, yet 12 of 14 first copiers fetched no partner instructions in sampled encounters. Registered 8086 copying-law tests remained untested; separate exploratory analysis supported the same overlap rule. These results distinguish independence from partner information from transmission of parental variation. They provide a deliberately strict experimental analogue of one aspect of Woese’s Darwinian threshold, without demonstrating adaptive use of the transmitted material.
