# Replication precedes individuality, and heritable variation follows it, in a digital primordial soup

*Internal review copy, 2026-10-10 (revision 3). Working title, to be replaced: the registered branching-transfer test (X1) showed that the first replicator carries no heritable variation, so the earlier title no longer fits. Nature Article format: summary 199 words, main text 4,568 words, six figures, Methods 3,186 words, ten Extended Data items, and Supplementary Information (Supplementary Methods, figures and tables, theorems and proofs). Every number is from a generated table of the project repository; every experiment reported is complete; the genealogies were settled on 17 worlds and replicated as registered on 20 more, except for one prediction (N6-4).*

---

Organisational theories define a living individual as a system whose future depends on itself rather than on its surroundings^1–4^; somewhere in the origin of life, spreading patterns must have become such individuals. We followed that passage through thousands of worlds of random machine code. On tapes of 16 to 64 bytes with harmless by-products, in the Z80 and an 8080-like subset, the first replicator whose copies copy in turn is almost always a two-byte word that writes itself when executed, runs on into its partner and makes copies that vary with it. Its lineages persist when they branch, but no site keeps a mutation: it replicates a pattern, not a genome. Closure, execution confined to the replicator's own bytes, follows where a closing design is within reach, and seeded closers displace the open form; the evolved closers we tested rely on registers that the harness empties. Heritable variation arrives only with closure, and only in some closers: in a recurring block-copy core one byte decides whether a closer rewrites itself, erasing mutations, or copies its whole tape, keeping them. Whether by-products halt execution sets their balance. In a second machine, adding one self-writing instruction made the first replicator open.

## Main

What distinguishes a living thing from the chemistry around it has two answers: a list of properties, metabolism, compartment and heredity, with the question of which came first^5–10^, or a relation: a living system is one whose operations produce the organisation that produces them, so that its future depends on its own past rather than on its environment^1–4,11,12^. Evolutionary theory places the start of Darwinian evolution proper at a related passage, where vertical descent comes to dominate communal exchange^13^ and replication outcompetes the chemistry that produces replicators^14^. Few experimental systems allow such a transition to be observed as it happens, repeated under controlled variation and taken apart.

Digital organisms usually descend from a designed ancestor^15,16^; random-program soups come closer. When random byte strings for a simple machine execute one another, with no fitness function and no replacement, self-replicating programs arise and take over^17^, as in earlier program soups and artificial chemistries^18–22^. For the Z80 processor the first replicators are load–push programs that give way to a block-copy family^17,23^, a takeover attributed to mutational robustness^23^, and a Z80 soup with an energy budget has been used to study cooperation^24^; whether the successors descend from the first replicators, and how, has not been asked. We treated such a soup as a test of one hypothesis: that the smallest programs that write themselves run on into their surroundings, so that replication begins open (Fig. 1a), and that closure, a path of execution that returns into the organism and one step towards individuality, is a later invention; and we asked when heritable variation, the material of Darwinian evolution, appears. We ran more than 3,600 worlds of random Z80 code, varying the instruction set, program length, execution budget, mutation rate and memory geometry, with confirmatory stages pre-registered with kill criteria (Methods). Heredity is measured by intervention, as in serial-transfer tests of self-replication^25^: a program is lifted out and cultured against fresh random partners. We repeated the central experiment on an 8080-like subset of the Z80 (the 8080 subset) and on a second, minimal machine whose instruction set we could edit, and recorded every encounter of 40 worlds to rebuild their genealogies.

### Order before replicators

The soup holds 20,000 programs of L bytes on a 160 × 125 lattice (Fig. 2a). At each step 8,192 random cells are each paired with a lattice neighbour, the pair is executed as one program for 128 instructions and written back, and a background mutation replaces a fixed number of random bytes; nothing else happens (Methods). A tape is *heritable* if, lifted out and executed against random partners, it makes copies that copy in turn; the heritable fraction of random cells is the population measure.

The first thing to form is order that does not inherit. When the most common pattern first occupies a tenth of the soup, a quarter to a third of all bytes are zero (means of 24–34% at each of seven lengths from 9 to 100 bytes, Stage B; Fig. 2 follows one world). Push, call and reset instructions executed with empty registers write zeros wherever the stack pointer points, and zero is itself an instruction that does nothing, so the pattern spreads by being written and changes nothing when executed. We call this flood *tar*, after the asphalt that prebiotic chemistry makes from the reactions that also make sugars^26^. In the standard soup the tar is *benign*; in some soups we made a fetched zero halt the pair, making the tar *lethal*. The tar is what detectors see: the high-order entropy used to detect the transition^17^ is near chance or inverted in unablated worlds at six of eight lengths from 25 to 100 bytes, and an assembly measure of selection^27,28^, which rises with any duplication, passes ten times its random-soup value before the first heritable replicator in 71 of 80 worlds (Extended Data Fig. 1). Only intervention tells the two apart.

### The first replicator is open

With benign tar and the harness's standard conventions (Methods), the first heritable replicator of every confirmatory world of 16 to 64 bytes is the same program, which we call the *pusher*: the two-byte word `01 c5`, or one of its register variants, repeated to fill the tape (Fig. 1a). It comes first in 170 of 170 such worlds, across five lengths, the Z80 and its 8080 subset, and two pairing rules, at Kaplan–Meier medians of 450–750 steps in Stage G (Supplementary Table 1; Fig. 1b); on shorter tapes block copiers often come first (at 5, 6, 9 and 12 bytes; Extended Data Fig. 2b). It still comes first when every register is drawn at random before each encounter (20 of 20 worlds); with a random stack pointer the first replicator is open in 18 of 20, though in 13 it is the stack move `e1 e3`, which has no operand (Methods). Executed, `01 c5` loads its own next two bytes into a register pair and writes that pair where the stack pointer points, in the partner: its operand bytes pass verbatim into the output, a *literal channel* from code to copy, so it copies itself as a side effect of being run, with no loop, no counter and no test of its surroundings. It is nearly the only thing of its size that replicates: of all 65,536 two-byte words tiled to 16 bytes, exactly five, besides the zero word, copy themselves into an all-zero partner, each pairing a load with a push (Supplementary Information, Proposition 3). Removing the immediate loads or the stack writers delays the first replicator by orders of magnitude or prevents it, and no ablation that keeps them changed its time significantly with ten worlds each (Extended Data Fig. 2).

The first replicator is *open*. Its pointer fetches partner bytes in every one of 256 encounters, for every traced first replicator under benign tar (100 of 100). Against 256 random partners it copies into a median 0.66–0.73 of them and loses a quarter or more of its own bytes in a median 0.06–0.43 of encounters (Fig. 3a,b), because after writing its copy it executes the partner's bytes, which can poison the register pair before the next push. The offspring is an exact copy in a median 58% of encounters at L = 16 and almost never at L = 64, and the information that flows from the partner into it, its entropy over random partners (Supplementary Information, Lemma 7), runs from 3.9 bits at L = 16 to the 8-bit ceiling of the sample at L = 64 (Fig. 3c): a child depends in part on the neighbour it was made against. Against cells drawn from the whole soup at step 5,000 the offspring still carry 3.8 bits of the partner at L = 16 and the pointer enters in every encounter (80 of 80 first replicators; registered), although on the lattice a pusher's neighbour is its own kind in 57% of its encounters, and inflow among lattice neighbours was not measured (Supplementary Methods). The median heritable fraction of random cells stays between 0.09 and 0.19 from step 2,000 to step 20,000 at L = 16 (Fig. 3d). Copied serially into fresh random partners, one offspring per transfer, 96–100% of first-replicator lines end within eight transfers (Fig. 4a), about as fast as a copy success of 0.66 per encounter predicts. Allowed to branch, with two fresh partners per member and generation, 48 of 64 unmutated lineages survive eight generations, yet none of 512 single mutants (2,048 populations) still carries its mutation, with random partners or with cells of open-phase soups (registered; Supplementary Table 2): the pusher replicates a pattern, not a genome.

### Closure, where a closer is within reach

The first replicator does not stay. At L = 16 the dominant tape carries a control-flow instruction in 20 of 20 worlds by 300,000 steps, 17 of them ending on the identical eight-byte sequence whose conditional return sends the pointer back into the organism's own body (Fig. 3e; Extended Data Table 1). Every such successor copies into all 256 random partners without self-damage (Fig. 3a,b,f), and in 63 of 67 loop-bearing final dominants (the most heritable of the three commonest tapes at the end) the offspring is one and the same string in all 256 encounters (Fig. 3c). At the last snapshot every sampled heritable cell is confined in all 20 worlds at L = 16, and most are in 19 of 20 worlds at L = 20, 12 of 20 at L = 32 and 8 of 20 at L = 64 (registered population sample; Supplementary Table 1); the heritable fraction of random cells rises to 0.75–0.94 at L = 16 (Fig. 3d).

We call this property *closure*, and a replicator that has it a *closer*. A loop in the code, confinement of the pointer and independence of the offspring from the partner travel together here, and each was measured: every loop-bearing final dominant fetches no partner byte as an instruction in any traced encounter (89 of 89, including the 20 at L = 50 whose offspring reopen), and confinement and zero inflow agree in 106 of 110 finals (Extended Data Fig. 3). Closure in this sense is a property of execution, not the organisational closure by which theories of autonomy define living systems^1,3,11^, though it is one component of individuality in the information-theoretic sense^4^. It is not a membrane, since the organism's bytes stay exposed, nor a property of place^29^: it also arose with well-mixed pairing, though later (post hoc; Supplementary Methods). The longer the tape, the slower and rarer was closure (Cochran–Armitage test, P = 6 × 10⁻⁵, post hoc; the per-byte mutation rate also falls with length): 20 of 20 worlds by 300,000 steps at L = 16, 12 of 20 by one million at L = 32 and 8 of 20 at L = 64, and given ten million steps every world closed at L = 16 and 20 (Extended Data Fig. 4). At L = 50 a jump spreads but its carriers' offspring reopen, and only 18–39% of heritable cells are confined (registered population sample). Closers rely on conventions of the harness: under random registers the four evolved or evolved-core closers we tested lose their lineages and only a constructed self-initialising closer survives (Supplementary Methods, Parent × environment), and some at L = 20 and 50 use a 16-bit program counter in a memory addressed modulo 2L (Methods).

Closure is selected where it is found. Seeded at 1% into a world filled with pushers, the evolved return closer at L = 16 and the block-copy tiling at L = 32 held half the cells within 400–750 and 260–290 steps, in 5 of 5 soups each; the pusher seeded into a closer's world vanished in 5 of 5 (Extended Data Fig. 5). Where closure did not come, the closer was missing from what evolution had found, not from what the instruction set allows. In the 8080 subset no world closed at L = 32 in a million steps, yet a 20-byte closer of 8080 instructions, seeded at 1%, had its loop in half the cells within 300–320 steps in 5 of 5 soups. With registers drawn at random before each encounter, closure came in 8 of 40 worlds by three million steps, and a self-initialising closer, seeded, displaced the pusher in 5 of 5 soups (Extended Data Fig. 6g; Supplementary Methods). The barrier was discovery, not expressibility, and closers seeded at 1% were not held back by competition.

### The first closers are not point mutants

How the first closer is made cannot be read from snapshots, and the soup cannot be replayed exactly, so we recorded every encounter and mutation of 20 worlds at L = 16 until closure, rebuilt the lines of descent of their final confined populations and checked the record by replaying encounters byte for byte (Methods); once the analysis had been settled on these worlds, we registered it and repeated it unchanged on 20 new ones (Supplementary Table 3). Seventeen of the first 20 worlds closed and all 20 of the second. On every sampled line of every closed world the first copier was open (registered; 37 of 37 worlds). On these lines the founder, the first confined copier after the newest open copier, appeared a median of 79 and 46.5 steps after the newest open copier in the two sets, through non-copiers only in 16 of 17 and 20 of 20 worlds (Extended Data Fig. 7a,b,e; in 2 and 3 worlds a few confined copiers, at most 0.05% of cells, were already present off these lines). No founder came from the tape before it by a point mutation: it differs from that tape in 2 to 11 bytes, and the 54 and 55 recorded mutations on these lines were expected to make 0.002 and 0.006 founders (Extended Data Fig. 7d). Each founder was written in one encounter (Extended Data Fig. 7c) by a tape that does not copy itself whole: in 23 of the 37 worlds by one that copies neither itself nor the code it runs, rarely or never with soup partners (median 0.2% and 0% of encounters, excluding three writers that make it with most partners; never in 12 of the 23), and in 14 by a confined tape that copies the code it runs but not the rest of itself and always makes the founder. Non-copiers fill 84–91% of these soups, so the writers' kind largely reflects their abundance (P = 0.076 and 0.062). In 34 of 37 worlds the founder carries the return closer's motif, built of one-byte variants of the pusher's word `21 e5` (Extended Data Fig. 7). Closers thus descend from the open lineage, as expected in soups the pushers dominate, but the first one is not a point mutant of the tape before it: it is written in one encounter by a tape that does not copy itself whole. The registered replication confirmed how the founder is made and that only non-copiers lie between it and the newest open copier; its prediction of the writer's kind by the code it copies failed (11 of 20 copy neither, against 12 required).

### Closure regenerates or transmits

Heritable variation comes only with closure, and not with every closer. We followed every single-byte mutant of 14 genomes through eight serial transfers into fresh random partners (Fig. 4a–c; Methods). The pusher's mutations reach its first copies, at 16 of 50 and 30 of 64 positions, but they fade as its lines of transfer end, mutated or not (0 and 7 sites after four transfers, none after eight); our registered prediction that its sites persist failed, and they fade at least as fast when its lineages branch (Supplementary Table 2). The evolved closers, the return at L = 16 and the block-copy tilings at L = 20 and 32, lose no unmutated lineage, but they copy the code they run and rebuild the rest of the tape from it: 60–88% of their mutant lineages live on with the mutation erased, and almost none keeps it (at most 0.3%). A third design, a short copying core followed by bytes it copies but never runs, keeps the allele in 46–63% of its mutant lineages, at 9 to 20 positions, 7 to 12 of them never executed (Fig. 4c). Such bytes are copied without being interpreted, half of what Langton's and von Neumann's descriptions do^30,31^; and erasure is not canalisation^32^, since the variation never reaches the offspring.

One byte decides which. A four-byte block-copy core recurs across stages, `XX 5e ed b0`: one instruction, then `LD E,(HL)` and the repeating block move `LDIR`; with its variant `1e NN ed b0` it is carried by 2,347 of the 15,655 confined heritable cells we scanned (Methods). Because the registers start at zero, the first byte, which ref. 23 annotates as the partner offset, is both an instruction and the copy offset: each byte is copied d = XX mod 2L addresses ahead, and the block move tiles the first d bytes around the ring, the organism included. With d < L the closer rewrites its own body from its first d bytes and erases every mutation beyond them; with d = L it copies the tape verbatim and keeps them. A closed lineage can therefore transmit d − 4 sites, set by one byte of its own code, whenever d divides L and the first byte passes control to the core without writing below address d (Proposition 8). Of 2,343 confined block-copy cells with such an offset, sampled in Stages G, I, K and L (846 distinct tapes, 42 worlds), the single-mutant scan finds exactly d − 4 sites in 2,289 (98%).

A world's most common tape hides which setting it holds, since a regenerating population is nearly clonal and a transmitting one a cloud of distinct genomes, so we classified random cells: confined cells with at most two transmissible sites are *regenerators*, those with five or more *transmitters* (Fig. 4d,e; Methods). Under benign tar, regenerators are the majority of heritable cells at the last snapshot in 17 of 20 worlds at L = 16 and 14 of 20 at L = 20, and in 20 of 20 in the 8080 subset at L = 16; longer tapes hold mixtures (at L = 32 regenerators are the majority in 3 of 20 worlds and no class in 9; at L = 64 regenerators and transmitters each in 2). Under lethal tar, transmitters are the majority in 9 of 10 worlds, with a median of 11.5 sites they never execute, while the most common tape holds 0.2–1.5% of the soup.

The mode belongs to the parent, which keeps it in every environment where its lineages survive (Extended Data Fig. 8a), and the halting rule chooses among parents. A matched regenerator and transmitter at L = 16, differing only in the offset byte and in what follows the core, copy alike and survive alike as partners of cells drawn from the soups, to within 0.01 (Extended Data Fig. 6e), yet populations converge from either side to a share that the tar sets: at L = 32 transmitters hold 0.03–0.37 of the core-carrying cells after 300,000 steps with benign tar and 0.55–0.87 with lethal tar, whether they start at 1%, 50% or 99% (Extended Data Fig. 6a,b). Encounter censuses of 20 such soups under each rule show where the two kinds differ (post hoc; Methods). Copies between them balance to within 0.1%; what differs is how each fares against the cells that carry no core, a median 13% of the soup. A core-free executor whose pointer enters a regenerator runs the copies of the copying code that make up its body and destroys it in 0.75–0.89 of such entries; one that enters a transmitter wanders through bytes never run and, under the lethal rule, is often halted by a zero first. These terms account for the drift of the transmitters' share between rules, but not for why regenerators win under benign tar, which remains open (Methods).

Robustness^33^ does not explain the succession: the first replicator is at least as robust to single mutations as its successor (0.74 against 0.59 of single mutants remain heritable at L = 16; Supplementary Fig. 1), and by the criterion of ref. 23, an exact self-copy after mutation, the matched transmitter scores 0.88 and the regenerator 0 (Extended Data Fig. 6f): the criterion measures the transmission of mutations, not survival. A transmitter tolerates a mutation and passes it on, a regenerator survives it by erasing it, and only tolerance builds variation^34^.

### What decides the beginning

If the order of events follows the literal channel, a machine without one should be born loop-bearing, and giving it one should start replication open. BFF, the Brainfuck family of ref. 17, has no literal channel: copying a 64-byte tape into a partner that differs from it everywhere takes at least 64 instructions that change a cell and 64 that move a head, so a copier confined to its own 64 cells must execute some cell twice (Theorem 1). In soups of 2¹⁷ programs run for 16,384 epochs (Fig. 5), BFF as published and with a wrapping pointer produced a heritable replicator in 28 of 48 worlds; every first replicator contains a loop (28 of 28), and none enters its partner in more than 22 of 64 encounters (with harmless brackets, 7 of 12 worlds replicated, every first replicator loop-bearing but only 5 confined (entering the partner in at most 5% of encounters; 4 in none): the missing channel explains a loop, not confinement). Adding one instruction that writes its own two operand bytes below the write head changes the beginning: in 12 of 12 worlds under either pointer rule the first replicator is the one-byte tiling of that instruction, loop-free, with its pointer entering the partner in every encounter (Fig. 5b). It is the `01 c5` of BFF, and without a wrapping pointer its offspring carry 8 bits of the partner, the ceiling of the measurement (Supplementary Fig. 2).

Then it dies. An unmatched bracket ends BFF execution, so the soup fills with tar that halts the pointer, and by epoch 1,024 the open replicator holds under 1% of the soup; no closed class evolves (0 of 12; Supplementary Methods). The open phase is a *window* whose length the tar sets. With harmless brackets the open replicator holds for all 16,384 epochs in 12 of 12 worlds; with brackets that halt with probability p it holds 98% of the soup at 0 < p ≤ 0.1 (18 of 18 soups), and at p = 0.3 it falls below 1% within about 900 epochs in 6 of 6 soups but recovers to 10% or more in 5, against the registered prediction (Fig. 5c). When the literal writes two or three copies of its word per execution, raising the *write ratio*, bytes written per byte read, from 2/3 to 4/3 or 2, so that a copy completes within one pass, the open tiling's offspring carry 0 bits of the partner, information-closed from birth without a loop, and every random tape is heritable at 16,384 epochs in 12 of 12 soups (Fig. 5d).

Lethal tar also removes the open phase in the first machine. When a zero byte halts the Z80, its own tar becomes lethal and the open beginning disappears: in 10 of 10 pre-registered worlds no pusher establishes, contrary to the registered expectation of an open phase followed by extinction, and replication begins closed and late, as a block copier, at a Kaplan–Meier median of 38,000 steps against 450 with benign tar (Extended Data Fig. 9). Between the two rules lies a threshold. When a fetched zero halts the pair with probability p, the first replicator is an open load–push word in 10 of 10 worlds at every p up to 0.1, in 2 of 10 at p = 0.3 and in none at p = 1, and the median share of transmitters among heritable cells at 300,000 steps rises from 0 at p ≤ 0.03 to 0.78 at p = 1 (Spearman ρ = 0.50, permutation P < 5 × 10⁻⁵; Extended Data Fig. 8b,d), though they are the majority in only 6 of 10 worlds at p = 1 (7 registered); closure timing is not monotone in p (Extended Data Fig. 8c).

The beginning does not need the Z80's own extensions. In the 8080 subset, made by turning every Z80-only instruction into a no-op, which keeps the literal word but removes the block copies and relative jumps (Methods), the load–push word comes first in every world, and at L = 16 the Z80's own return closer, pure 8080 code, dominates 20 of 20 worlds by 300,000 steps. Together these fill a map (Fig. 5e): a literal channel suffices for replication to start open, though it is not the only route; the lethality of the tar decides whether an open beginning lasts, or happens at all; and the closed designs within reach decide whether what follows is closure, a late closed beginning, or extinction.

Two counting statements bound this order of events without forcing it, and a third bounds heritable variation (Fig. 6; Methods). A straight-line replicator is open by construction, and a confined one must execute some address twice wherever execution cannot stop inside it before the budget is spent (Fig. 6a). Counting complete self-writers, the usual estimate of how likely a replicator is to arise by chance^35,36^, does not explain which comes first: the five of period two are open, but 558 of the 641 of period four are closed (Fig. 6b). And a closer with the block-copy core transmits d − 4 sites (Fig. 6c).

### Discussion

The order of events is observed, and the counts bound it. The first replicator is open, and its children depend on the partner they were made against; closure, execution confined to the replicator, is found where a closing design is within reach and selected once found; and the first closer was written in one encounter, not made by a point mutation. Replication came first, heritable variation only after closure, and the first closer was written by other tapes before it copied itself, which recalls, at the level of single programs and in an analysis whose founder result was replicated as registered, the passage from communal exchange to vertical descent that Woese placed at the Darwinian threshold^13^. Closure is necessary for heritable variation here but not sufficient. In the terms of ref. 8, the open replicator replicates without heritable variation, and so does a closer that copies only the code it runs, regenerating itself and erasing its mutants, as autocatalytic networks that lack evolvability do^37^; a closer that also copies bytes it never runs has a limited heredity at d − 4 sites. In a recurring block-copy core one byte decides which, and the halting rule shifts the balance between them by a mechanism our encounter censuses only partly account for.

For chemistry these are analogies, not results. Template copying is also a literal channel, a strand specifying its complement by physical fit with no interpreter, and short self-templating oligomers replicate without enzymes^38^; by analogy, whether by-products are inert or inhibitory would decide whether an open phase lasts long enough for control to evolve, processivity, the chemical write ratio, now selectable in ribozyme polymerases^39^, how far copies depend on their surroundings, and if the first self-sufficient replicators are written rather than refined, they would first appear as products of molecules that do not replicate, as replicating RNAs arise de novo in reactions of Qβ replicase^40^. In these soups detection required intervention, since low-complexity order precedes the first replicators and trips compression and assembly measures alike^27,28^.

What limits the claims is plain: one processor, a restriction of it and one toy language; units of fixed size, start address and pairing imposed by the harness; a culture test that is our own ground truth, though the open beginning held under both register conventions, at every confirmatory length from 16 to 64 bytes and under heredity thresholds from 0.2 to 0.4; closure incomplete at the largest lengths within our horizons, and more than a hundred times slower when the harness does not empty the registers, on which the evolved closers depend as scaffolded reproducers^41^; and genealogies at one length, with definitions settled on 17 worlds and confirmed, in part, on 20 more. Within those limits the result stands: replication came first; execution confined to the replicator, with offspring independent of the partner, came later; and heritable variation came only with closure, in closers that one byte of a recurring block-copy core sets to transmit rather than regenerate.

## Methods

**Soup.** The soup holds N = 20,000 tapes of L bytes (L from 3 to 100), initialised with uniformly random bytes, on a 160 × 125 square lattice. At every step 8,192 cells are drawn uniformly at random and each is paired with one of its four lattice neighbours (a step off the edge is reflected; the lattice does not wrap); a pair is active if neither member is already in another pair that step, which leaves ≈ 3,950 active pairs. An active pair is concatenated, organism first, into a pair memory of P bytes (P = 2L except in the padding experiment of Stage F), executed by a Z80 emulator for a fixed budget of 128 instructions (32–2,048 in the budget arms) from byte 0 with all registers zero and the stack pointer at the last byte of the partner, and both tapes are written back. After each step 8,192/2^k bytes chosen uniformly at random over the soup are replaced by random bytes (k = 4 unless stated; the size-axis control arms of Stage E also vary N and the mutation rate, Supplementary Methods); the replacements are applied in parallel without synchronisation, so two falling in the same four-byte word in one step can lose one, at most about 0.3% of mutations at L = 16 and fewer at longer tapes. There is no fitness function, no replacement and no death. Instruction-set ablations replace whole opcode families by no-ops through a prefix-aware fetch hook that sees the operation after `DD`/`FD` and `ED`/`CB` prefix resolution; the ablations and their opcode counts are listed in Supplementary Table 4. The executor was validated against an independent Z80 reference (z80-emulator 2.3.0 by L. Kesteloot), both mapping every 16-bit address modulo the pair size: at pair sizes of 32, 38, 40, 64, 100 and 128 bytes, 100,000 random and 9,000 targeted programs per size in each of two seeded runs (jumps, stack moves and block copies across address 0) left no unexplained mismatch, every difference tracing to a documented defect of the reference or to one of two undocumented details the executor does not model (the F3 and F5 flags after `BIT n,(HL)`, and the refresh register after a wasted prefix); the odd and padded rings of Stage F were not differentially tested, and no claim depends on cycle-exact timing.

**Culture test and partner tests.** A tape runs as the first program against 64 uniformly random partners for the condition's budget; copy success is the fraction of partners that become ≥ 75% copies at the best cyclic shift. The score of a tape x is the mean, over partners j whose best-shift match to x before the encounter, s_j, is below 0.75, of (s′_j − s_j)/(1 − s_j), s′_j being the match after it. The second-generation score, gen2, is the same quantity one generation on: the offspring of encounter j runs as organism against a fresh random partner, whose match to x is measured before and after; gen2 thus approximates the share of encounters whose offspring copies in turn (Supplementary Methods). Heritable means gen2 ≥ 0.3, faithful additionally ≥ 50% of partners copied. Partner-independence tests use 256 random partners and record self-damage (≥ 25% of the organism's bytes changed in one encounter). In BFF the culture test also records whether the instruction pointer entered the partner in each encounter; closed means ≤ 5% of encounters entered and ≥ 95% of partners copied, open means ≥ 50% entered. Information inflow from the partner, the conditional mutual information^42^ I(E; Y | X = x) = H(Y | X = x) − H(Y | X = x, E) between partner E and offspring Y (the partner's bytes after the encounter) with the organism x fixed, equals H(Y | X = x) because the executor is deterministic; it is estimated as the plug-in entropy in bits over 256 random partners, ceiling 8 bits.

**Events and statistics.** The event `t_rep` is the first sampled step at which one of the three most common tapes has a share ≥ 0.5% and is heritable by the culture test (a clonality criterion by design: it dates the first *clone*, not the first heritable material); `t_faith` additionally requires faithfulness; the *final dominant* is the most heritable of the three most common tapes at the horizon. Because the most common tape can misrepresent a diverse population, population claims rest on random cells (Population sampling, below); `tq_10` (occupancy of the quasispecies^43^ of the most common tape ≥ 10%) is the pre-registered occupancy event and fires on zero floods as well as on replicators. Emergence fractions are quoted as worlds reaching the event within the horizon with Wilson 95% intervals; times as Kaplan–Meier medians with censoring at the horizon. Ablation effects are ratios of Kaplan–Meier medians with seed-paired sign tests (the same seeds run under every ablation); confirmatory contrasts use one-sided Fisher exact tests and the Cochran–Mantel–Haenszel test pooled over budgets. All thresholds (0.75 copy, gen2 ≥ 0.3, 0.5% share, 10% occupancy, 95%/5% closure) were fixed before the confirmatory stages. Principal medians are given with 95% bootstrap intervals across worlds (Supplementary Table 1). Same-seed Z80 runs diverge because GPU scheduling makes the pairing order non-deterministic, so every Z80 claim is a population claim over seeds (the spread of ten same-seed repeats is 55–85% of that of ten seeds); the BFF soup is bitwise reproducible.

**Harness conventions.** Two derived soup and executor shaders, byte-identical to the Stage G ones except after the register reset of every encounter (`gen_conv_shader.py`): *random registers* draws A, F, B, C, D, E, H, L, their alternates, IX and IY uniformly (program counter 0, stack pointer as before); *random stack pointer* draws SP uniformly. Twenty worlds each at L = 16 under Stage G conditions for 300,000 steps (seeds 8001–8020), and 40 random-register worlds for 3,000,000 steps (seeds 8101–8120, and 8201–8220 as a registered replication after the first set closed only in odd seeds); every assay, including the culture test and tracing, runs under the world's own convention. Closed: most heritable cells among 64 random cells confined in 16 traced encounters. Registered predictions and outcomes, invasion designs and the constructed 8080 closer are in Supplementary Methods (Harness conventions; Invasions at aligned lengths; The 8080 subset; Invasions of a constructed closer).

**First closure.** For every world of Stages G, H, I, K, L and M (Stage G at L = 50, whose offspring reopen, excluded), the first sample at which one of the three most common tapes holding at least 0.5% of cells is heritable and confined in 16 of 16 traced encounters, censored at the horizon; Kaplan–Meier medians and a log-rank test of benign L = 16 (Stages G and L, 30 worlds) against lethal L = 16 (Stage I, 10 worlds), with the decision rule registered before the analysis (`first_closure.py`).

**The copy-offset switch.** In the block-copy core `XX 5e ed b0` with HL = D = B = C = 0, register E is loaded from the organism's first byte, so `LDIR` copies each byte d = XX mod 2L addresses ahead; for `1e NN ed b0`, d = NN mod 2L. The working values of XX were found by executing the core with every byte value (`offset_census.py`). The copy-period law pools every confined heritable cell of the population scans whose first four bytes are either core and compares d with the number of transmissible sites of its single-mutant scan (`offset_population.py`). The closing-core counts (2,347 of 15,655 confined heritable cells carry `XX 5e ed b0` or `1e NN ed b0`) are from `core_families.py`.

**Encounter censuses (exploratory).** All encounters of 20 final matched-pair soups at L = 32, ten of each tar, were recorded for 2,000 steps under each rule, cells classed by their first four bytes as regenerator (core with d < L), transmitter (d = L) or core-free; copying, destruction, conversion and mutational terms were compared with the 2,000-step drift of the transmitters' share (post hoc). Registered hypotheses that regenerators survive through backup cores (N2) and that inherited zeros carry the lethal rule's protection of transmitters (N4) were rejected; payload zeros (N1) are inherited stack-damage scars, not a selected defence (Supplementary Methods).

**Genealogies (exploratory, then replicated).** Same-seed runs diverge (Events and statistics), so to inspect events 20 new benign-tar worlds at L = 16 (seeds 31001–31020, Stage G dynamics) were recorded encounter by encounter from step 0 (`lod.py`): the pairs drawn, each active pair's memories before and after execution, and the soup after mutation, until the confined share of copy events had exceeded 0.5 for 2,000 steps, then 5,000 steps more (cap 80,000). Each changed cell receives a birth record: a *copy* (within Hamming distance L/4 of the executor at some cyclic shift, closer to it than to its own former tape, and with at least L/4 bytes newly matching it), *damage*, or partial overwrite (within L/4 of its former tape), *copy of the partner* (the executor's own half becomes such a copy of its partner), *rewrite* (none of these, a residual class that does not imply that the bytes of two parents were joined; its functional parent is the tape that supplied the bytes it executes) or *mutation*. Replay on the traced executor reproduced the recorded memories byte for byte in a validation world and all 40 recorded worlds (Supplementary Methods). On the lines of 64 sampled cells per closed world, every tape was classed against 64 random partners as a copier if it made a ≥ 75% copy in at least half of the encounters, confined if its pointer entered the partner in fewer than half, and otherwise a non-copier; the founder is the first confined copier after the newest open copier older than the final stretch in which at least 90% of the line is confined, and a world's first founder is its earliest. On each line the first copier is the earliest record whose tape is an open or confined copier (registered, X3). The copy-gain rule, the founder, the functional parent and the separation of first founders from later conversions by existing closers were defined after inspecting data, as dated in the change log; the frozen pipeline was then registered and run on 20 new worlds (N6, seeds 32001–32020; Supplementary Table 3). Word shares, founding-encounter replays, the executed-bytes copier test, the production test and the reach and abundance nulls are in Supplementary Methods.

**Traced execution.** A derived copy of the culture-test executor records every address fetched as instruction stream (opcodes, prefixes, operands) in a 256-bit map per encounter, with byte-identical execution (checked). Pointer confinement is the fraction of 256 encounters with a seeded random partner in which no partner address is fetched; the executed set is the union of fetched organism positions; a transmissible position of the mutational scan is called unexecuted if it is never fetched. Every first replicator and final dominant of Stages G, K and I (110 of each) was traced. In a registered test of openness among the soup's own cells (X2), each Stage G first replicator was also run against 256 cells drawn uniformly from its world at step 5,000 (L = 16 and 20) or at the snapshot nearest twice its t_rep (L = 50 and 64) (`inflow_insoup.py`; kin encounters on the lattice, Supplementary Methods, Controls). Confinement concerns the instruction pointer only; a confined copier may read its partner's bytes as data.

**Mutational scan.** A mutant is heritable if gen2 ≥ 0.3; its mutation is transmitted if the mutant byte is present at the aligned position in at least half of the offspring that are ≥ 75% copies; a position is a transmissible site if at least half of its tested values are both heritable and transmitted. Transmission is scored in first-generation copies only; serial retention follows alleles further.

**Branching transfer (registered, X1).** As serial retention, except that each generation every member of a population runs against two fresh partners, every offspring that is a ≥ 75% copy of its parent joins the next generation, populations are capped at 32 by random subsampling, and each of 32 seeded values per position founds four populations, against 64 unmutated ones; a population carries the allele if at least half of its members contain the mutant's five-byte window, and a site is transmissible under the serial-retention rule (`branching_transfer.py`; partners uniform or drawn from the step-5,000 and 10,000 snapshots of the 20 Stage G worlds of the same length).

**Serial allele retention.** Fourteen genomes (Fig. 4a) were scanned with every single-byte mutant (all 255 values per position at L ≤ 32, 32 seeded values at L ≥ 50). Each mutant founded 16 lineages and the unmutated tape 256. In each of eight transfers the lineage tape ran as organism against a fresh uniformly random partner under the genome's own rule (8080 subset, lethal tar) and the partner's bytes after the encounter became the next lineage tape. A lineage is alive while every transfer has been a copy (offspring matching its parent at ≥ 75% of positions at the best cyclic shift). The allele is carried if the mutant's five-byte window centred on the mutation occurs anywhere in the lineage tape. A position is serially transmissible after g transfers if, for at least half of its values, the share of alive lineages carrying the allele exceeds by at least 0.5 the share of alive unmutated lineages that contain the same window by chance (scoring details, Supplementary Methods).

**Population sampling.** At the final snapshot of every Stage G, I, K, L and M world, 256 random cells were culture-tested (32 partners) and traced against 16 random partners; a cell is confined if no partner address is fetched as instruction in any of the 16 (registered). At every snapshot of the same worlds, 64 random cells were culture-tested and traced, and up to 16 random heritable cells were given the single-mutant scan (16 values per position, 16 partners) and their executed set (defined before the run and exploratory, since the first sample motivated it). A heritable cell is open if not confined; a confined one is a regenerator (at most two transmissible sites), intermediate (three or four) or a transmitter (five or more); the classes of unscanned confined cells are inferred from the scanned ones. A world is closed at the population level if most of its heritable cells are confined. For confined cells the first-generation scan agrees with serial retention (Fig. 4a,c); for open cells it overstates it, so open cells are reported as open.

**Address mapping and aligned lengths.** Pair memory is a ring of 2L bytes: every memory access uses the 16-bit address modulo 2L, and the stack pointer starts at the largest 16-bit value that aliases the last byte of the partner. The evolved closers tested rely on the empty registers (Extended Data Fig. 8a). The program counter and the stack pointer themselves are 16-bit, so a relative jump or a stack move that crosses address 0 reaches (65,536 + target) mod 2L, which equals the true-ring target only when 2L divides 65,536. Stage K (L = 32, 20 worlds, one million steps, seeds 5001–5020) tested closure at an aligned intermediate length (outcomes, Supplementary Methods).

**The 8080 subset.** The `i8080` suppression makes every Z80-only instruction a no-op: the 256 CB-page opcodes, the 78 defined ED-page opcodes (among them the block copies and their repeats, 16-bit ADC and SBC, IN and OUT (C), NEG, RETN and RETI, IM, LD A,I and A,R, RLD and RRD) and EX AF,AF', DJNZ, the five JR forms and EXX. This is an 8080-like restriction of the same emulator, not an 8080. Stage M: Stage G conditions at L = 16 (300,000 steps) and L = 32 (one million), 20 worlds each, seeds 7001–7020, with the predictions registered before the run.

**Ten-million-step runs.** Stage L: the Stage G conditions at L = 16 and 20, ten worlds each (seeds 6001–6010), run for ten million steps.

**Controls.** Stage H, well-mixed pairing (the partner drawn uniformly from the soup) at L = 16, ten worlds, seeds 3001–3010, against the 20 Stage G worlds at L = 16. Stage I, lethal tar: a zero byte fetched as an opcode halts the pair for the rest of the encounter (a change to the machine's semantics, not only to the fate of its by-product), at L = 16, ten worlds, seeds 4001–4010, with the culture and partner tests under the same rule.

**BFF.** The default semantics of cubff, the code of ref. 17, verified from source: two 64-byte tapes concatenated, pointer and heads at byte 0, ten instructions, every other byte a no-op, unmatched brackets halt, 2¹³ steps per encounter, 2¹⁷ programs, random ordered pairs each epoch, mutation 2⁻¹² per byte per epoch. Switches: `ip_wrap` (pointer wraps modulo 128); `literal` (byte 0x50 = `P a b`: write a at head1−1 and b at head1−2, then head1 −= 2, skip the operands; designed so that its one-byte tiling writes itself, the analogue of the Z80's load–push word); `nohalt` (unmatched brackets are no-ops); `halt_p` (an unmatched bracket halts with probability p, decided by a hash of the pair, the step and the seed); `lit_rep` (the literal instruction writes r copies of its word per execution). Our GPU kernel reproduces cubff's validation replicator and matches a CPU reference on randomised tests (`tests/test_bff.py`, 15 of 15 passed). Seed counts, registered predictions and their outcomes are in Supplementary Methods.

**What is proved.** Theorems 1 and 2 (Supplementary Information; Fig. 6a): a straight-line replicator runs on into its partner, open by construction; a confined one must re-execute an address (return control into code already run) wherever execution cannot stop inside it before the budget ends (benign-tar Z80: 128 executions, ≤ 100 cells; HALT counts as a repeat by convention) or a copy costs more executions than it has cells (BFF). Lethal tar breaks the first proviso (a zero can stop execution inside the organism); the second is unproven for the Z80. Counting complete self-writers, the usual chance-origin estimate, does not explain which comes first: all five of period two are open, 558 of 641 of period four closed (Fig. 6b; Proposition 3); the open word's head start (Kaplan–Meier medians at L = 16, Stage G: first replicator 450 steps, first closer 21,000) is measured, not counted. Proposition 8 bounds heritable variation: a closer with the block-copy core transmits d − 4 sites (Fig. 6c). Perfect fidelity requires zero information inflow (Lemma 7), reached by confinement in Z80 loop-bearing finals and, while entering the partner in every encounter, by the BFF literal writing two or three copies (Figs 3c and 5d). A window model (closure probability ≈ 1 − exp(−q ∫ n dt); q, closed-variant rate; n, open population) fits the lethality dial's ends, not its middle (Supplementary Information, Model 5).

Stages D, F, G, H and I, the BFF cells and every stage added in revision (K, L, M, the mutational scan, the pair invasion, the traced execution and the dials) were pre-registered with predictions and kill criteria before launch. Misses are reported in the text and listed, with the registered tests of the second and third revisions, in Supplementary Methods. ## Supplementary Methods

Each paragraph below extends the Methods paragraph of the same heading; paragraphs whose heading does not appear in Methods are given here in full.

**Soup.** In the padding experiment of Stage F, P ≥ 2L and the padding bytes are never written back. Background mutation (8,192/2ᵏ random bytes per step) uses k ∈ {2, 4, 6} in the mutation arms of Stages A, C and D and k ∈ {1, 2, 3, 5, 8} in the Stage E mutation sweep at L = 100; the per-byte-constant arm of Stage E replaces 32·L bytes per step, the L = 16 rate per byte, at every length.

**Recording.** Stages C–M write a sample at steps 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21 and 34, then every 50 steps to step 5,000, then every 500 steps (300,000-step runs), 1,000 (one-million-step runs) or 5,000 (Stage L), with the ten most common exact tapes and their shares, quasispecies^43^ occupancy (fraction of cells within Hamming distance ⌈L/4⌉ of the dominant tape), byte histogram, zero-byte fraction and byte entropy, brotli-compressed bits per byte and the high-order entropy of ref. 17, species entropy and unique-tape fraction, per-interaction statistics (bytes written into the partner, copy events, destroyed programs), 16 random tapes for the functional census, and full soup snapshots at fixed steps and at emergence. Stages A and B sample every 500 steps from step 500, with the three most common tapes and without the byte histogram, the per-interaction statistics or the random tapes.

**Culture test and partner tests.** A tape is heritable if its copies copy in turn, and gen2 ≥ 0.3 tests this only approximately: gen2 is a normalised gain in best-shift similarity to the original tape, not a count of copies, so offspring that are not copies contribute about zero unless, like the offspring of a word that smears its return addresses into its partners, they raise their own partners' similarity to the word without copying it. Such words pass the test without being heritable: 246 two-byte census words reach gen2 ≥ 0.3 without producing a single 75% copy (Supplementary Fig. 3). Parent, offspring and granddaughter sequences of three lineages of each kind of replicator are tabulated in `results/review_r2/LINEAGES.md` (`lineage_examples.py`). Replicators are therefore also scored by the copies they make (faithfulness and the partner tests), and dating the first replicator by the faithful criterion (`t_faith`, Methods) instead of by heredity alone gives the same tape at the same step in all 80 Stage G worlds.

**Events and statistics.** The final dominant is the most common tape in 57 of the 60 Stage G worlds at L = 16, 20 and 64; at L = 50 it is the second most common in all 20, behind the plain pusher tiling (5.5–6.6% of cells), while the tapes that carry its jump word `20 f0` hold 70–76% of cells. Its offspring is then the parent with one more `20 f0` inserted, the same string in every encounter; that offspring enters the partner in every encounter, the next generation (six jumps) is confined but fails the culture test (gen2 0.19), and the one after (ten jumps) no longer copies (`l50_lineage.py`), so at L = 50 the final dominant is a stage of a lineage rather than a lineage. Between-variant transition rates in BFF use two-sided Fisher tests and Mann–Whitney tests on event times with non-transitioning runs censored at the horizon. Across the 233 unablated worlds of the exploratory stages, 92 gained a control-flow instruction between the first replicator and the final dominant and 3 lost one. In BFF, where replicator populations are quasispecies with small exact-class shares, the first replicator is the most common tape class (a tape and its reverse count as one class) at the first sample at which it is heritable, and the population event is a heritable fraction ≥ 0.5 of 32 random tapes. Re-dating the first replicator from the recorded runs under heredity thresholds from 0.2 to 0.4 leaves the load–push word first in 170 of 170 benign-tar worlds of 16–64 bytes; at 0.5 and 0.6, near the pusher's own gen2, it is first in 155 of 169 and 107 of 164.

**Definitions and theorems.** A *self-writer* is a tape that, executed against the all-zero partner, leaves the partner holding a copy of itself. *Closure* (confinement): the organism's pointer never fetches a partner address as part of an instruction in any encounter with a partner of the sampled class. In the tests a tape is *confined* if no partner byte is fetched in any of a stated number of traced encounters with uniformly random partners, and *open* if one is fetched, for a first replicator in at least half of them and for a sampled cell in any; each use below states the number of encounters and any other criterion. Theorem 1: copying a 64-byte BFF tape into a partner that differs from it in every cell takes at least 128 instructions, 64 that change a cell and 64 head moves, so a program whose pointer stays in its own 64 cells executes some address twice in that encounter, through a backward bracket jump: a loop, a return of control into code already executed, not a repeat of the machine's state. Theorem 2: a pointer that stays in the organism's L cells executes some address twice, a return of control into code already executed (not a repeat of the machine's state), provided a copy costs more instruction executions than the organism has addresses (BFF) or execution cannot stop inside the organism before the budget is spent (the Z80 soups with benign tar, budget 128 > L). By convention a `HALT`, which holds the processor on its own address for the rest of the budget, counts as executing that address again, so a confined program that halts inside the organism meets the conclusion by the convention alone, without returning control into code already executed. The second proviso fails wherever a zero byte can halt the pair, and the first is not established for the Z80, so the theorem does not apply in Stage I, in the lethality dial at p > 0 or in any other run under the lethal rule (the lethal arms of the copy-offset switch and of the parent × environment matrix, the encounter censuses, and the serial transfers and scans of lethal-tar tapes), nor in the Stage E arms with budgets 32 and 64 at L = 100, where the budget ends before a pass over the tape. Theorem 6: the tiling of a self-consistent literal-write instruction is a straight-line, hence open, program the size of the literal that writes its own content at an advancing write pointer, and it is a self-writer wherever the encounter lets those writes cover the partner's region (in the Z80, by Proposition 3); without such an instruction, under the theorem's copy-cost hypothesis, which we do not prove for BFF, and in a machine whose execution ends when the pointer leaves the pair, every self-writer executes some address twice. A literal write is sufficient for an open first replicator, not necessary: open first replicators also arose without one, in BFF with harmless brackets and in the Z80 with a random stack pointer (Supplementary Information). Lemma 7: with the organism x fixed, I(E; Y | X = x) > 0 implies imperfect fidelity across partners, and perfect fidelity implies I(E; Y | X = x) = 0; the converse fails. Proposition 8, an elementary consequence of the overlapping block move: in the block-copy core `XX 5e ed b0`, if the first instruction passes control to the second, writes no memory below the copy offset and leaves HL, D and BC as stated in the Supplementary Information, and the copy offset d = XX mod 2L satisfies 4 ≤ d ≤ L and divides L, every encounter leaves organism and partner holding the d-periodic tiling of the organism's first d bytes, so exactly the d − 4 bytes between the core and the offset are transmitted. Proposition 3 (computer-assisted, `census_period.py`): every word of period two, three and four, tiled to 16 bytes and executed against the zero partner; a self-writer is closed if its pointer fetches no partner byte against the zero partner and 16 random partners. Period two: exactly six self-writers, the trivial `00 00` and the five load–push words, all straight-line and open. Period three: one, `b0 5e ed` tiled, closed, a block copier whose copy offset equals the tape length. Period four: 641 of primitive period four, 558 of them closed, all block copies (455 `LDIR`, 103 `LDDR`), and 83 open. Proposition 4 states what the count of the initial soup does and does not show. Full statements and proofs are in the Supplementary Information.

**Harness conventions.** Draws are seeded by the step's batch seed and the pair index. Registered predictions (pre-registration document, sections R3-C and R4) and outcomes: the first replicator is a load–push word under random registers in at least 18 of 20 worlds (met, 20 of 20) and open under a random stack pointer in at least 15 of 20 (met, 18 of 20; the first replicator is a load–push word in 5, the stack move `e1 e3` in 13 and a closed block copier in 2); closure in at most 5 of 20 random-register worlds by 3,000,000 steps (not met: 6 of 20), and in 2–12 of 20 in the replication (met: 2, both even seeds). Closers of the closed random-register worlds (most heritable cells among 64 random cells confined in 16 traced encounters) were characterised post hoc (32 heritable cells each: whole-tape exact copy over 16 encounters, never-executed bytes by tracing). Invasions under random registers at L = 16: the 13-byte closer `21 00 00 11 10 00 01 10 00 ed b0 18 fe` with a random 3-byte tail per cell, seeded at 1% into a pusher-filled world, and the reverse, five seeds each and three unseeded controls, 50,000 steps; under the standard convention, the same closer against the 4-byte regenerator `44 5e ed b0` × 4, 100,000 steps. The registered motif-share endpoints failed on definition (neutral operand bytes drift in a transmitter); classes of 64 random cells at the end (culture test, and confinement in 16 traced encounters, under the world's convention) are reported (`r4_analyse.py`). Under random registers the seeded closer's descendants held the soup closed in 5 of 5 pusher-filled worlds: at 50,000 steps 0.81–0.91 of random cells were heritable and 0.93–0.98 of those confined, against 0.03–0.08 heritable and none confined in the three unseeded pusher worlds; with the pusher seeded at 1% into the closer's world, the soup stayed 0.84–0.89 heritable and 0.93–0.98 confined (5 of 5). Under the standard convention neither design persisted as such, and every soup stayed closed (0.88–0.97 of random cells heritable, 0.98–1.00 of those confined), held by a diverse block-copy family.

**Invasions at aligned lengths.** Stage G dynamics at L = 16: the return closer `ad e3 21 e3 21 c0 ad c0` × 2 against the pusher `01 c5` × 8; Stage K dynamics at L = 32: the block-copy tiling `04 5e ed b0` × 8 against `01 c5` × 16. Each seeded at 1% into a world filled with the other, five seeds each way, three unseeded controls per resident, 20,000 steps; class share (within Hamming distance L/4 of the design at any shift) every 10 steps to 500 and every 250 after, and classes of 64 random cells at 1,000, 5,000 and 20,000 steps. Registered: the closer holds most cells and most heritable cells are confined (no partner byte fetched in 16 traced encounters) at 20,000 steps in at least 4 of 5 soups at each length (met, 5 of 5 at both), and the pusher stays below 5% in a closer world (met, 5 of 5).

**The copy-offset switch.** The matched pair (registered in the revision as test E, unrelated to Stage E): at L = 16, R = `44 5e ed b0` × 4 and T = `b0 5e ed b0` followed by 12 random bytes drawn for every cell; at L = 32, R = `04 5e ed b0` × 8 and T = `a0 5e ed b0` + 28 random bytes. Conditions L = 16 with benign tar, L = 16 with lethal tar and L = 32 with benign tar; starts 50:50 (ten worlds without mutation for 100,000 steps and ten with the standard mutation for 300,000 steps) and 1% each way with mutation (five each); every cell classified by its first four bytes every 50 steps to 5,000 and every 500 after (`offset_switch.py`). L = 32 with lethal tar was registered separately, after these results, to separate the tar from the length (E-L32L): ten 50:50 worlds and five 1% starts each way, standard mutation, 300,000 steps; its prediction, a transmitter share among core-carrying cells above 0.5 at 300,000 steps in at least 7 of 10 mixtures, was met (10 of 10; median 0.77, range 0.55–0.87). The registered predictions of a mutation-bias equilibrium (the switch's E1 and E2) were not met; without mutation the share drifts. Encounter components of the pristine pair were measured with 4,096 encounters each against cells of the final soups (`offset_encounter.py`). Mutational flows (`offset_flow.py`, registered as E-mech): every single-byte mutant of R and T, four random partners each, classified by whether its offspring copies itself exactly and with what period; the registered prediction (EM1) failed and the flows of isolated mutants are the same under benign and lethal tar, so the environment dependence is reported without a mechanism.

**Encounter censuses (exploratory).** Lattice-neighbour pairs (200,000 per soup set) from the final soups of the ten 50:50 L = 32 worlds of each tar, executed under each rule (composition crossed with rule), with every cell classed by its first four bytes before and after (`offset_census_enc.py`); and core-free executors from the same soups against three partners, R, T with a random body and T with a tiled body (`a0 5e ed b0` × 8), traced to see whether the executor's pointer entered the partner (`offset_intruder.py`, 16,384 encounters per combination). Both were run after the matched-pair results and are reported as descriptive. The in-situ demography (`n2_demography.py`, `n2_background.py`) summed per-capita copying between classes (`n2_mutual.py`), destruction by core-free executors, conversion of intruders and mutational terms. The inherited-zero test (N4) replaced a transmitter's zeros by random non-halting bytes (`n4_scars.py`); the outcomes of N2 and N4 are given under Pre-registration and stages. Payload zeros (`payload_toxin.py`, N1) rise towards the end of the payload, are about as common under benign as under lethal tar at L = 32 (median 0.067 against 0.051; at L = 16, 0.038 against 0.060) and are absent in most mutation-free worlds.

**Genealogies (exploratory, then replicated).** Same-seed runs diverge because the pair draw resolves collisions by atomic claims whose order varies. Records from which no living cell descends are freed. Validation: before any registered world, replaying every active pair of the first 200 steps of a validation world on the traced executor reproduced the recorded memories byte for byte (795,934 encounters), cells changed outside active pairs never exceeded the mutation count, and in a world seeded with the return closer at 1% among pushers all 64 sampled confined copiers traced to a seeded closer; the replay check held in all 40 recorded worlds. Lines, founders and first founders are computed by `lod_chain.py` and `lod_founders2.py`, the first copier on each line by `lod_openfirst.py`; every first founder and confined writer entered the partner in none of 64 encounters. Word shares are measured in the last soup snapshot (every 2,000 steps) before each founder against a random-tape baseline of about 2.4 × 10⁻⁴ per two-byte word (`lod_parts_first.py`) and, post hoc, against the shares of all 255 second-byte variants of `21 e5` in the same snapshot (`lod_neighbours.py`). The founding encounter of each first founder was replayed from the record to find which tape executed and on which side the founder was written; the executor and every earlier record on the chain were then run against 64 random partners to measure how often each writes the founder into the partner or turns itself into it, and whether its pointer enters the partner (`lod_founding.py`). Because the copier test asks for a copy of the whole tape, each founding executor was also tested by its executed bytes only (a tape copies its executed code if, in at least half of 64 random encounters, at least 75% of the organism positions it fetches reappear in the partner), and its production of the founder was measured with 512 partners drawn from the last soup snapshot before the founder (the executed-bytes test and this production test are also in `lod_founding.py`). Every single-byte mutant of every distinct tape on the chains was classed against 64 random partners to measure the reach of point mutation, and the composition of the soup before each founder gives the abundance null for the writers' kind (`lod_reach.py`). The registered descent prediction was met but uninformative, since by then nearly every cell present had an open copier among its ancestors of the previous 2,000 steps. Separating first founders from later conversions by existing closers identifies 15 later founders in 10 worlds, 11 of them with bytes from a confined copier, and 14 in 10 worlds of the replication, 11 of them with such bytes.

**Parent × environment.** Six parents at L = 16 (the pusher; the evolved return closer; R and T of the copy-offset switch, T with a fixed random tail; the evolved lethal-tar closer of world 4009; the self-initialising closer `2e 00 1e 10 ed b0` with a fixed random tail) were followed through eight serial transfers, with every single-byte mutant in 16 lineages and 256 unmutated lineages, as in the serial-retention test, in six environments: benign or lethal rule with uniform random partners; partners drawn from the final soup of Stage G world 2001 (closed: every sampled heritable cell confined) or from its snapshot at 5,000 steps (open phase, before closure), benign rule; partners from the final soup of Stage I world 4001, lethal rule; random registers with random partners (`pe_matrix.py`; registered as PE). Registered: wherever a parent's lineages survive, regenerators carry at most 2 sites and transmitters at least 8 after eight transfers (met, 24 of 24); the pusher's lineages are lost everywhere (not met: 0.36 alive with partners from the closed world, with 0 sites).

**Lethality dial.** Derived soup and executor shaders (`gen_dial_shader.py`) in which a zero byte fetched as the first byte of an instruction halts the pair with probability p, a fresh draw from a per-encounter generator for every such fetch; p ∈ {0, 0.01, 0.03, 0.1, 0.3, 1}. Before any soup ran, the p = 0 executor reproduced the standard executor and the p = 1 executor the Stage I executor exactly on 65,536 random pairs with 25% zero bytes. Ten worlds per p at L = 16 under Stage G conditions, the same seeds (9001–9010) at every p, 300,000 steps; top-3 tapes every 50 steps to 2,000, every 250 to 20,000 and every 2,500 after; every assay under the world's own p (`dial_soups.py`; registered as DZ). Registered: the first replicator is open (its pointer in the partner in at least half of 16 traced encounters) in at least 8 of 10 worlds at p = 0 and at most 2 at p = 1, with a falling trend (met: 10 of 10, and 0 of the 9 worlds with a heritable common tape; one-sided Cochran–Armitage z = −6.1); first closure (a top-3 tape holding at least 0.5% of cells, heritable and confined in 16 of 16 traced encounters) later at higher p, monotone up to one reversal and Spearman-positive (Spearman met, ρ = 0.25, one-sided permutation P = 0.023; monotone part not met: Kaplan–Meier medians of 18,000, 42,500, 60,000, 75,000, 57,500 and 52,500 steps from p = 0 to p = 1, so that closure came later at p = 0.1, where the first replicator was open in 10 of 10 worlds, than at p = 1, where there was no open phase, which contradicts the monotone window reading of Model 5); the transmitter share of heritable cells rising with p (met, ρ = 0.50, one-sided permutation P < 5 × 10⁻⁵, no value as large in 20,000 permutations) with transmitter-majority worlds at most 3 of 10 at p = 0 and at least 7 at p = 1 (1 and 6: the second not met). The registered closure endpoint (a top-3 tape holding 0.5% of cells) misses closed transmitter populations, whose genomes all differ: two worlds counted as unclosed (p = 0.03 and 1) are closed at the population level (most heritable cells confined) and transmitter-dominated at 300,000 steps, which biases the timing test against closure at high p.

**Ref. 23's robustness criterion.** Every Stage K first replicator and final dominant (L = 32): 1,000 trials each of 1, 4 and 8 successive random single-byte mutations; a trial counts if the mutant, run for 512 instructions against the all-zero partner, leaves the partner equal to the mutant. Registered: block-copy finals more robust than pushers at every k (not met: medians 0.000–0.002 for both). The matched pair of the copy-offset switch was scored the same way post hoc (`robust_ref22.py`).

**Mutational scan.** For each Stage G first replicator and final dominant, every single-byte mutant (all 255 alternative values per position at L = 16 and 20; 32 seeded values per position at L = 50 and 64) was run as organism A against 32 shared random partners for 128 instructions and its offspring against 32 fresh partners, with the culture test's thresholds. The single-mutant variation score is Σ_i log₂(1 + 255 f_i) over positions, f_i being the fraction of transmissible values at position i, a summary of how many single mutants are heritable and reach first-generation copies, not a channel capacity (it ignores epistasis and later generations). Three predictions were written before the run (the successor more robust to mutation than the first replicator; the closed form with more transmissible sites; the matched pusher-with-jump of L = 50 with more sites than its pusher); all three failed and are reported as such.

**Pair invasion.** Soups of 20,000 cells at L = 50 under the Stage G dynamics, every cell set to one tape of the matched pair (the pusher tiling, or the same tiling with `20 f0` inserted three times, the final dominant of 14 Stage G worlds, confined in all 256 traced encounters although its offspring reopen) and 1% of cells to the other at step 0; five seeds per direction, three seeds per resident with no invader; 20,000 steps. The pre-registered outcome, the invader's nearest-class share passing 50%, could not be met at this length because the quasispecies cloud splits between the two classes (a closed tape that has lost two jumps is nearer the pusher); the share of cells carrying the jump word, reported instead, was defined after the data were seen. In single-tape invasion assays the pusher tiling `01 c5` × 8 seeded into 1% of the cells of a random soup at L = 16 (128 instructions, mutation 1/16, five soups) peaked at a median occupancy of 0.32 at step 250. The jump-word marker was checked against traced confinement before use (registered): in three re-runs of the closed-into-ancestor invasion, 512 random cells at each of eight steps were traced against 16 random partners. The marker share reached 0.73–0.75 while the share of cells confined in all 16 encounters stayed at 0.19–0.25, and only 26–33% of marker-carrying cells were confined; the registered agreement failed, so the L = 50 invasion is reported as the spread of the jump, not of closure (Extended Data Fig. 5a).

**Serial allele retention.** The fourteen genomes are four open pushers, the most common closed final dominant (confined in all 256 traced encounters) at each of L = 16, 20 and 32, the L = 50 final dominant, the lethal-tar final dominant with the most first-generation sites, the most common tape of three Stage L worlds at the first snapshot at which a genome-bearing block copier was most common, and the 20-byte 8080 closer with two random 12-byte payloads. Lineage r met the same partners for every mutant. The window is sought cyclically, which needs no alignment, so inserted jumps and shifts do not hide it. Every lineage is counted as lost (not alive), erased (alive, allele absent) or carrying the allele. Mutants whose window occurs in the unmutated tape would be excluded (none did). Design and predictions were registered before the run: the pusher's sites were predicted to persist (failed: its alleles reach first-generation copies only, and fade as fast when its lineages branch and persist, X1); the evolved closers to erase, the transient genomes to keep at least five sites and the constructed closer its payload (met). A post hoc variant drew partners from cells of the organism's own world (Stage G, seed 2001) instead of random bytes.

**Invasions of a constructed closer.** Stage M dynamics at L = 32. Against the pusher (registered as A1 in the revision, unrelated to Stage A): the 20-byte 8080 closer with payload 1 seeded at 1% into a world filled with the 8080 pusher, and the reverse, under the 8080 subset, five seeds each and three unseeded controls per resident, 20,000 steps. Against a block copier (A2): the same closer against the evolved block-copy tiling `04 5e ed b0` × 8 under the full Z80, 50,000 steps. The registered outcome, the share of cells carrying the closer's exact 20-byte core, failed on definition: the core mutates within a few hundred steps while its descendants hold the soup. We report the share carrying the closer's loop body (post hoc) and the classes of 64 random cells per soup at the last step (Extended Data Fig. 5).

**Address mapping and aligned lengths.** The conventions, addresses taken modulo the pair length, the stack pointer at the end of the pair and every register empty at the start of an encounter, follow ref. 17, whose tapes were 16 bytes long, an aligned length. Of the 67 final dominants of Stage G that are closed (loop-bearing, and fetching no partner byte in any of 256 traced encounters), 25 carry a relative jump (JR or DJNZ) at a length where the 16-bit program counter does not fold consistently onto the ring: 20 at L = 50 and 5 at L = 20, one of which (seed 2003) is classed by its block repeat in Extended Data Table 1 (we did not check that every one of these jumps crosses address 0); the pair-invasion and mutational-scan results at L = 50 are results about the machine so defined. Stage K was pre-registered: first replicator a load–push word in ≥ 18 of 20, a closed successor in ≥ 15 of 20, kill below 10. Outcomes: the first replicator was a load–push word in 20 of 20 worlds, and the final dominant was closed in 12 of 20, all block copies.

**The 8080 subset.** Three deviations from a real 8080 are kept: the IX and IY prefixes `DD` and `FD` still select the indexed form of an 8080 instruction, where the 8080 executes them as CALL; the 8080's other alternate opcodes are no-ops here (`CB`, JMP on the 8080, and `ED`, CALL, become two-byte no-ops with the byte that follows, and `D9`, RET, a one-byte no-op); and the core keeps Z80 flag behaviour, so P/V holds overflow after arithmetic where the 8080 sets parity, and conditional jumps, calls and returns on PE or PO can differ. The registered reading that the subset expresses no closer on a 32-byte ring was wrong and is withdrawn: a reviewer's 20-byte closer of 8080 instructions (`21 20 00 31 40 00 16 10 2b 46 2b 4e c5 15 c2 08 00 c3 11 00`, a counted push loop closed by a conditional jump, then a jump to itself) copies itself and any 12 following bytes exactly into 256 of 256 random partners without entering them, in our executor under the subset (`check_8080_closer.py`).

**Ten-million-step runs.** Stage L samples every 5,000 steps, with full snapshots at the Stage G steps and at 2, 3, 5, 7.5 and 10 million. At every snapshot of the Stage L worlds and of the Stage G, K and I worlds, the most common exact tape was scanned (32 values per position, 32 partners) and traced (64 partners), giving its transmissible sites, single-mutant variation score, executed set and pointer confinement against step (a modal-tape analysis; population classes are in Fig. 4d,e). Predictions registered before the run: L1, in at least 5 of 10 worlds at each length the dominant at ten million steps has more transmissible sites than the closed dominant at 300,000 (L = 16) or one million (L = 20) steps; L2, every such recovery is in a block-copy lineage and its sites are copied-but-unexecuted positions; L3, no return-based or push-based dominant with more than one transmissible site at any snapshot. Outcomes: L1 not met (1 of 10 at L = 16, 0 of 10 at L = 20); L2 not met at L = 16 (the one recovery, from 0 to 4 sites, is a block-copy lineage with one of its four sites unexecuted) and not applicable at L = 20; L3 not met as written, at L = 16 (the open pusher of seed 6003 holds two transmissible sites at eight snapshots from 2,000 to 30,000 steps) and at L = 20 (the open pusher holds two to three sites and the jump-based closer two); the kill criterion fired (final dominant with at most one site in 9 of 10 and 8 of 10 worlds). Every world closed (the most common tape heritable and entering the partner in fewer than half of 64 traced encounters), first at 10,000–75,000 steps (L = 16) and 20,000–500,000 (L = 20); the heritable fraction of random cells at ten million steps is a median 0.89 and 0.95.

**Controls.** Pre-registered: H1, the open phase less heritable under mixing (killed: the median heritable fraction of 16 random cells over steps 2,000–20,000 is 0.125 in both, one-sided P = 0.78); H2, closure sooner under mixing, closure here meaning t_close70, the first sampled step at which the heritable fraction of 16 random cells reaches 0.7, not pointer confinement (killed: median 50,000 against 30,000 steps, reached by 300,000 steps in 7 of 10 worlds against 20 of 20, one-sided P = 0.77). Post hoc, after H2 was killed, the share of worlds reaching t_close70 by 300,000 steps was compared by a two-sided Fisher exact test (7 of 10 against 20 of 20, P = 0.030): closure by this heritability proxy was later and rarer under mixing, the direction opposite to the registered prediction. Also post hoc, the trend of population closure (most sampled heritable cells confined) with tape length at the aligned lengths L = 16, 32 and 64 (20 of 20 by 300,000 steps; 12 of 20 and 8 of 20 by one million) was tested by a two-sided Cochran–Armitage test on log₂ L (z = −4.02, P = 5.7 × 10⁻⁵); the shorter horizon at L = 16 makes the test conservative, and the per-byte mutation rate also falls with length. Descriptive: the first replicator met its own kind (Hamming distance at most 2) in 57% of its open-phase encounters on the lattice and 11% when mixed (`kin_census.py`). Under lethal tar the heritable fraction of random cells rose from 0 at 20,000 steps to 0.41 at 30,000 and 0.88 at 50,000 (medians over worlds). *Heritable material* is dated by the first sampled step at which at least 10% of the 16 random cells of the census are heritable (post hoc, `timing_heritable.py`): a median of 40,000 steps under lethal tar against 1,250 under benign tar (Stage G, L = 16), a 32-fold delay, against Kaplan–Meier medians of 38,000 and 450 steps for the first clone, t_rep.

**BFF.** 24 seeds each for the published and wrapping variants, 12 for each literal variant, 12 for the published rule with harmless brackets (run after review; pre-registered: every first replicator loop-bearing, met 7 of 7; a transition in at least 9 of 12, not met at 7; pointer-closed first replicators (entering the partner in at most 5% of encounters) in every world, not met in two, one intermediate (23% of encounters) and one open (every encounter)), 6 for each of p = 0.01, 0.03, 0.1 and 0.3 under wrap + literal push, and 6 for each of r = 2 and 3 under the literal push without a wrapping pointer; events every 64 epochs; high-order entropy with brotli quality 2 as in ref. 17. With the literal and halting brackets no closed class evolved: a closed top class (entering the partner in at most 5% of encounters and copying at least 95% of partners) appeared in none of the 12 soups with a wrapping pointer, where at least 6 had been registered (prediction (e3) of the theory note), and in none of the 12 without one (`bff_analysis.py`). Dial predictions, registered before the run as D1 and D2 of the revision (unrelated to Stage D): D1-1, the open population persists (share ≥ 10% at 16,384 epochs) in at least 4 of 6 soups at p = 0.01 (met, 6 of 6) and in at most 1 of 6 at p = 0.3 (not met: the share falls below 1% at 832–896 epochs in 6 of 6 and recovers to 0.10–0.11 in 5 of 6, with 0.31–0.44 of random tapes heritable); D1-2, the epoch of collapse falls monotonically with p within a factor of three of 1/p (not met: no collapse at 0 < p ≤ 0.1, 896 at p = 0.3, 192 at p = 1); D1-3, no closure by control flow at any p (met); D2-1, inflow at most 1 bit at r ≥ 2 (met: 0.00 bits in 12 of 12 soups at r = 2 and 3, against 8.00 in 12 of 12 at r = 1); D2-2, the first replicator still the loop-free, pointer-open tiling (met, 12 of 12); D2-3, collapse slower or absent at r ≥ 2 (met: absent, with an all-`P` share at epoch 1,024 of 22–31% at r = 2 and of 3.6–7.3% in 5 of 6 soups and 0 in one at r = 3, against 0–0.24% at r = 1, and every random tape heritable at 16,384 epochs).

**Searches.** Two-byte census: all 65,536 words tiled to 16 bytes, culture test (32 partners) and partner test (256), plus the deterministic fixed-point test against the zero partner. ## Supplementary Information: theorems and proofs

*What kind of statement each item is: a **Theorem** has a pen-and-paper proof, and where a part of one is applied to a machine through a computed fact, as the Z80 case of Theorem 6 (ii) is through Proposition 3, this is marked; a **Proposition** is an exact statement that is either elementary (Propositions 4 and 8) or decided by finite computation, with the computation described (Proposition 3); a **Lemma** is a short general statement used to read a measurement (Lemma 7); a **Model** is a result about a stylised model with stated assumptions, not about the simulator. Empirical premises are marked as such, and every number quoted is from the generated tables behind the main text.*

**Notation.** An encounter executes a program held in a shared memory. The organism A occupies addresses 0 to L − 1 and the partner B the next L addresses; in the Z80 soups the 2L addresses form a ring, in BFF the 128 addresses do not wrap. Execution starts at address 0 with a fixed initial machine state (registers and flags zero; the Z80 stack pointer at the 16-bit address that aliases the last byte of B) and lasts at most T instruction executions (T = 128 in the Z80 soups, 2¹³ in BFF). A *control transfer* is a jump, call, return, repeat-in-place or halt; a *straight-line program* executes none, so that under default flow its pointer advances past each executed instruction. An address is *executed* when the first byte of an instruction is fetched from it; an instruction that repeats in place (`LDIR`) fetches its own address again at each repetition, and each repetition counts as one execution. An address is *repeated* if it is executed twice in one encounter, that is, if control returns into code that has already been executed. A repeated address is not a repeated machine state: registers, flags, data heads and memory may differ between the two visits. The *no-op context* is the partner all of whose bytes are no-ops (zero in the Z80, where byte 0 is NOP; zero in BFF, where every byte outside the ten instruction characters is a no-op). A tape x is a *self-writer* if its encounter with the no-op context ends with B holding a copy of x (up to a cyclic shift, as in the culture test), and a *full self-replicator in context e* if the encounter (x, e) ends with B holding a copy of x. It is *closed* (confined) if its pointer fetches no byte of B as part of an instruction, opcode or operand, in any context, and *open* if it does in some context. Confinement is a property of execution, measured by tracing the pointer; it is not closure in the sense of organisational theories of life, where closure is the mutual maintenance of a set of constraints^45^, and a confined organism still writes into its partner and relies on the harness for its initial registers and its turn to run. A value is *prescribed* if it is determined by x alone, the same for every partner.

**Theorem 1 (the cost of a copy in BFF).** *Setting.* BFF as published: a 128-byte pair; the instruction pointer p and the two data heads h0 and h1 start at address 0; p advances by one after every instruction unless a bracket jumps; `<` `>` and `{` `}` move h0 or h1 by one cell; `+` `−` change the byte under h0 by one; `.` copies the byte under h0 into the cell under h1 and `,` the byte under h1 into the cell under h0; every other byte is a no-op; execution ends when p leaves the pair, on an unmatched bracket, or after 2¹³ steps. *Statement.* Let A be the organism's 64-byte tape, at addresses 0–63, fix a cyclic shift s, and let B be a partner whose byte differs in every cell from the copy it is to receive (A rotated by s). Any execution of the pair that leaves B holding that copy runs at least 128 instructions: at least 64 that change a cell and at least 64 head moves. If p stays among A's 64 addresses throughout, some address is therefore executed at least twice, and the execution takes a backward bracket jump: control returns into code it has already executed. *Proof.* Every cell of B must change, and an instruction changes at most one cell (`+`, `−` and `,` the cell under h0, `.` the cell under h1), so at least 64 changing instructions run, and each cell of B is under a head when it changes. Both heads start at address 0, which lies in A, and each move changes one head's position by one, so after v moves the heads have occupied at most v positions besides 0; occupying all 64 cells of B needs v ≥ 64. Moves change no cell, so the two sets of instructions are disjoint, and at least 128 instructions run. An execution of at least 128 instructions at 64 addresses executes some address twice. Without a bracket jump p only increases, and `[` jumps forward, so p can return to an executed address only through a taken backward jump at `]`. ∎ *Corollary 1.1.* A BFF program that copies its tape, at some shift, into a partner differing in every cell from that copy while its pointer stays in its own cells takes a backward bracket jump in that encounter, that is, runs a loop. The loop is a repeated address, not necessarily a repeated state: between two visits to an address the heads and the tape may differ. *Scope.* The count is per encounter: it needs neither the same instruction sequence in every encounter nor a no-op partner, and it says nothing about a program whose pointer enters the partner, which then fetches the partner's bytes. An earlier version stated two further parts, a bound uniform over partners for programs whose pointer stays out of B and the claim that no straight-line execution writes a copy of a tape with more than 32 non-zero bytes into a zero partner. Both proofs were wrong, the first because brackets test the byte under h0 and `,` copies B's bytes into A, so that a confined program's control flow can depend on B, the second because bytes written into B ahead of the pointer are executed when the pointer arrives; both parts are withdrawn. That every observed BFF first replicator carries a loop (35 of 35 under the published rule, the wrapping pointer and harmless brackets) is an observation explained, not forced, by the count; whether the loop also keeps the pointer out of B is a separate, measured fact (22 of the 28 first replicators under the published rule and the wrapping pointer never entered the partner in 256 encounters).

**Theorem 2 (a confined execution repeats an address).** *Statement.* Consider one encounter of an organism of L bytes whose pointer executes only addresses of A. Some address of A is executed at least twice, so that control returns into code already executed, provided either (i) the encounter completes a copy that costs more than L instruction executions, or (ii) execution cannot end inside A before a budget of T > L executions is spent. *Convention.* In the Z80 a `HALT` holds the processor on its own address for the rest of the budget; the executor stops there, which leaves memory as holding would, and a held `HALT` counts as executing its address again, so that execution does not end at a `HALT` in the sense of (ii). A confined program that halts inside A therefore meets the conclusion by this convention alone, without returning control into code already executed. A closed full self-replicator is confined in every encounter, so the conclusion holds in each of its encounters that meets (i) or (ii). *Proof.* Every executed address lies among the L addresses of A. If none is executed twice, the encounter comprises at most L executions. Under (i) these are too few to complete the copy; under (ii) the budget requires more than L. In either case two executions share an address. ∎ *What the theorem does not say.* It gives a repeated address, not a repeated state. At the two visits the registers, flags, heads and memory may differ, and in a copy loop they do, since each pass moves a different byte; nothing here says that the machine's state is periodic. A periodic orbit would follow from the pigeonhole principle only over the whole state, every register and flag and all 2L bytes of memory, which takes far more values than the budget has steps. *Hypothesis (H1), default flow.* In the absence of an executed control transfer the pointer advances past each instruction, so that a straight-line program runs from the organism's region into the partner's. The theorem does not use (H1); Corollary 2.1 and Theorem 6 do. Under (H1), a confined execution that repeats an address has executed a control transfer, since without one its address rises from 0 until it leaves A. *The machines.* BFF satisfies (i) in every encounter with a partner that differs in every cell from the copy it is to receive: by Theorem 1 the copy costs at least 128 executions, more than the organism's 64 addresses. The Z80 soups with benign tar satisfy (ii): the budget is 128 executions for tapes of at most 100 bytes, and no instruction ends execution (a `HALT` is held, under the convention above). *Where the provisos fail.* Under lethal tar a zero byte fetched as an opcode ends execution, possibly inside the organism: in Stage I, in the lethality dial at every p > 0 (with probability p) and in every other run under the lethal rule (the lethal arms of the copy-offset switch and of the parent × environment matrix, the encounter censuses, and the serial transfers and scans of lethal-tar tapes). In the Stage E arms with budgets of 32 and 64 instructions at L = 100 the budget ends before a pass over the tape. In all of these (ii) does not hold, (i) is not established for the Z80, and the theorem does not apply. *The write-ratio reading.* A variant of (i) uses the write ratio. Suppose the partner differs from the copy in every cell, so that a copy writes at least L bytes, and the organism writes at most β < 1 bytes per byte of code it fetches. An execution confined to A in which no byte of A is fetched twice as part of an instruction fetches at most L bytes of code and so writes at most βL < L bytes, too few; hence some byte of A is fetched twice. This counts fetched bytes rather than instruction addresses, because an instruction can begin inside an earlier one. The ratio describes the replicators observed: the first replicator writes two bytes per four bytes of code (the executor trace shows eight bytes written when the pointer leaves its sixteen cells), BFF's copy instructions move one byte per instruction and need a head move per cell, and the literal push of modified BFF writes two bytes per three. It is not a machine-wide property of the Z80, in which a chain of pushes writes two bytes per byte of code; for the Z80 the theorem rests on (ii). *Corollary 2.1.* Under (H1) every straight-line program is open: with T > L its pointer reaches the partner's region. A closed self-writer therefore contains an executed control transfer, which may be a repeating instruction such as `LDIR`, besides the instructions that write its content. For the Z80 the census of Proposition 3 makes this exact at small periods: the self-writers of period two are five straight-line load–push words, all open, and the smallest closed self-writer has period three (`b0 5e ed` tiled, whose tiling contains the block copy `b0 5e ed b0`). The corollary does not make closed self-writers rare: at period four they outnumber the open ones.

**Proposition 3 (the short self-writers of the Z80; computer-assisted).** Tile a word of period k to 16 bytes and execute it for 128 instructions as A against the all-zero partner; it is a self-writer if B ends holding the tiling at some cyclic shift. By exhaustive execution of every word: at period two, of 65,536 words exactly six leave B holding a tiling of the word, the trivial `00 00`, which B already holds, and the five load–push words `01 c5`, `11 d5`, `21 e5` (`LD rr,nn ; PUSH rr`) and `2a e5`, `e5 2a` (`LD HL,(nn) ; PUSH HL`); none contains a control transfer, so each runs on into the partner, and every two-byte self-writer of this machine is open. At period three, of 16,777,216 words exactly one is a self-writer, `b0 5e ed`, which is closed: tiled, it contains the block copy `b0 5e ed b0` (`OR B ; LD E,(HL) ; LDIR`), whose copy offset is the tape length. At period four, of 4,294,967,296 words, 641 are self-writers of primitive period four: 558 closed, every one of them a block copy (455 with `LDIR`, 103 with `LDDR`), and 83 open. Closure is tested by tracing the self-writer against the zero partner and 16 random partners (closed if the pointer fetches no partner byte in any). The stochastic census of period two agrees: the same five words copy into 0.66–0.81 of 256 random partners, none into 0.95 or more, and the 246 words that pass the heredity threshold by smearing return addresses produce no 75% copy. *What it shows.* The smallest self-writers are open, and a closed self-writer needs only one period more; but closed self-writers are the majority at periods three and four, the largest the census covers, because a single repeating block move closes a copy. Counting self-writers does not, therefore, explain why the open word comes first.

**Proposition 4 (a count on the initial soup).** In a soup of N tapes of L uniformly random bytes, the expected number of occurrences of a given k-byte word, at any of the L − k + 1 positions of a tape, is N (L − k + 1) / 256ᵏ: for N = 20,000 and L = 16, 4.6 for a given two-byte word, 0.017 for a given three-byte word and 6.1 × 10⁻⁵ for a given four-byte word. *What the count does and does not show.* The load–push word works only in phase, as the four bytes `01 c5 01 c5` (`LD BC,nn` reading `c5 01`, then `PUSH BC`), the same length as the shortest evolved closers (`1e a4 ed b0`, `44 5e ed b0`), so a given open and a given closed working unit have the same expected count at step 0, 6.1 × 10⁻⁵. Proposition 3 counts more closed than open four-byte self-writers. The count therefore gives the open form no head start; the head start is measured (Kaplan–Meier medians over the 20 Stage G worlds at L = 16 of 450 steps to the first replicator and 21,000 to the first closed replicator, a top-3 tape heritable and confined in 16 of 16 traced encounters; 38,000 to the first closed replicator in the 10 Stage I worlds under lethal tar, where the first replicator is closed). An earlier version compared a two-byte word with a four-byte closer and concluded that the open form is exponentially likelier; that comparison was wrong and is withdrawn. Why the open word is found first, perhaps because partial load–push words write useful bytes from any position along an executed path while a closer works only from the start of execution, is not modelled.

**Model 5 (the closure window).** *Assumptions.* An open replicator population of n(t) tapes; closed functional variants arise at a rate q per open tape and step, by point mutation or written by programs that carry parts of the open population (for point mutation alone, which must place the right m bytes at the right phase, q is of order sites × μ × 256⁻ᵐ; writing from parts already present can make q much larger); a closed variant, once present, grows faster than the open form and fixes with probability of order one. *Statement.* The expected number of closed variants produced while the open population lasts is q ∫ n dt, and closure occurs with probability ≈ 1 − exp(−q ∫ n dt); the integral is the window. If the open form's own copies are viable in the soup it creates, n stays near the carrying capacity, the window grows without bound and closure is a matter of waiting; if the soup becomes lethal to the open form before q ∫ n dt reaches order one, the open form dies without closing. The rate q need not be constant: where the parts are spread by open copiers, q may itself grow with n, and q ∫ n dt is then to be read as ∫ q n dt, which rises faster than linearly with the open population. *What the experiments found.* How closers arise: in exact genealogies at L = 16, settled on 17 closed worlds and replicated as registered on 20 more (Methods, Genealogies), the founder (the first confined copier after the newest open copier: a ≥ 75% copy in at least half of 64 random encounters, the pointer entering the partner in fewer than half; every one entered in none) was out of reach of a single point mutation of the tape before it and was written in one encounter, by a program that copies neither its tape nor the code it runs in 23 of 37 worlds and by a confined tape that copies only the code it runs in 14, and in 34 of 37 worlds its parts were one-byte variants of the pusher's own word. Z80, benign zeros: closure of the population (most sampled heritable cells confined) in 20 of 20 worlds by 300,000 steps at L = 16, in 19 of 20 by one million at L = 20 and in 8 of 20 at L = 64, where the open form is least damaged and the motif must land in the longest tape (smallest q); at L = 50 a confined final dominant (no partner byte fetched in 256 traced encounters) arises in 20 of 20 worlds but its offspring reopens, so the model's assumption that a closed variant breeds true fails there. BFF with the literal push and lethal brackets: under a wrapping pointer the open form held 0.58–0.63 of N = 2¹⁷ tapes at epoch 64 and 2–3 × 10⁻⁴ N at epoch 256, an integrated window ∫ n dt of 8.2–8.7 × 10⁶ tape-epochs over 16,384 epochs, 5.0–5.5 × 10⁶ of them before epoch 256; without a wrapping pointer the window was 2.5–4.1 × 10⁵; a closed top class (entering the partner in at most 5% of encounters and copying at least 95% of partners) in 0 of 12 soups in each. BFF with the literal push and benign brackets: the open form persists for 16,384 epochs, holding a median 0.98 of the soup at the end, yet there is no closure by control flow in 12 of 12. The model does not explain this by q = 0. The search that found no closed heritable tiling up to period 10 covered only the four symbols {`P`, `[`, `]`, no-op} (Supplementary Methods), and the full language makes closed, loop-bearing replicators under related rules: they are most of the first replicators of BFF without the literal, 23 of 28 under halting brackets and 5 of 7 with harmless ones closed (entering the partner in at most 5% of encounters). In the model's terms q ∫ n dt stayed below order one over a long window, so q was small there, for reasons the model does not give. Z80 with lethal zeros (Stage I): the open form never establishes, so the window is empty from the start, and the first replicator is closed (no partner byte fetched in 256 traced encounters) and late (Kaplan–Meier median t_rep of 38,000 steps in the 10 Stage I worlds against 450 in the 20 Stage G worlds at L = 16; 10 of 10 worlds). The registered lethality dial (zeros halt with probability p; ten worlds per p, the same seeds at every p) tested the model's monotone prediction and refuted it: the first closed replicator (a top-3 tape heritable and confined in 16 of 16 traced encounters) comes at Kaplan–Meier medians of 18,000, 42,500, 60,000, 75,000, 57,500 and 52,500 steps at p = 0, 0.01, 0.03, 0.1, 0.3 and 1. Closure came later at p = 0.1, where the first replicator was open in 10 of 10 worlds, than at p = 1, where there was no open phase: the open phase was not needed for closure, and a damaged one delayed it. The dial therefore contradicts the window reading between its ends; the reading describes at most the contrast between benign and lethal tar, and that contrast changes lethality as well as the open phase. At the aligned length L = 32, 12 of 20 worlds closed by one million steps, and given ten million steps 20 of 20 closed at L = 16 and 20 (population closure, as above). In a world filled with the open form from step 0 at L = 50, n at carrying capacity throughout, the jump arose by mutation and spread to half the cells within 5,000–13,250 steps in 3 of 3, though its carriers are mostly not confined. The model's qualitative prediction, that an open phase feeds closure, is consistent with the first closed replicator appearing at a Kaplan–Meier median of 22,500 steps under benign tar (the 30 worlds of Stages G and L at L = 16) against 38,000 under lethal tar (the 10 Stage I worlds), where there is no open phase (log-rank P = 0.007), but that comparison changes lethality as well, the dial contradicts the reading between the extremes, and the open phase's part in closure is not settled; in exact genealogies the founders' lines run back to open copiers, as expected under any account (37 of 37 worlds; Methods, Genealogies). When an unmatched bracket halts with probability p, the open form held 98% of the soup at 0 < p ≤ 0.1 (median at 16,384 epochs) and fell below 1% at p = 0.3 (by epochs 832–896, in 6 of 6 soups) and at p = 1 (by epoch 192, median, in 12 of 12); at p = 0.3 it recovered to about a tenth of the soup by 16,384 epochs (at least 0.10 in 5 of 6), at p = 1 it did not (median 0.002). The registered expectation that the collapse time would scale as 1/p was not met, the lethality acting as a threshold between 0.1 and 0.3. When the literal writes two or three copies of its word, so that a copy completes in one pass, no partial copies are made, no lethal tar forms and the open form persists for 16,384 epochs in 12 of 12 soups, with zero inflow from birth. The model does not predict which regime a machine is in; that depends on how the open replicator's own by-products act on it, which is what the experiments measure.

**Theorem 6 (a literal write and the loop-free self-writer).** *Hypotheses.* (H1) Default flow, as in Theorem 2, with execution ending when the pointer leaves the pair (as in BFF) or continuing around a ring (as in the Z80 soups). (H2) *Copy cost:* in the no-op context, an execution that runs each address at most once cannot write a copy of the tape. (H2) is a hypothesis to be checked per machine. We do not prove it for BFF, where writes can land ahead of the pointer and be executed, and we have no direct test of it: the nearest evidence, a search of 16,447,860 straight-line BFF programs, was run under a wrapping pointer against two random partners, not in one pass against the no-op context, and found none that copies a tape of period two or more, only one-symbol fills (Supplementary Methods). (H3) *Self-consistent literal write:* an instruction that writes its own operand bytes verbatim at a write pointer that starts in, or advances into, the partner's region, and whose operand can be its own encoding in the phase executed (`LD BC,nn ; PUSH BC` with nn = `c5 01`; `P a b` with a = b = `P`). *Statement.* (i) Under either proviso of Theorem 2, every closed self-replicator executes some address at least twice in each encounter that meets the proviso. (ii) Under (H1) and (H3), the tiling of the literal instruction is a straight-line program, hence open, that writes its own content at the advancing write pointer; it is a self-writer wherever the encounter lets the written bytes cover the partner's region, which holds in the Z80 at L = 16 by the computer-assisted Proposition 3 (eight pushes over two passes of the 2L-byte ring); in BFF with `P` the tiling is observed to arise first (below), which the theorem does not decide. (iii) Under (H1) and (H2) without (H3), in a machine whose execution ends when the pointer leaves the pair, no straight-line program is a self-writer, and every self-writer executes some address at least twice in its encounter with the no-op context: born loop-bearing (whether its pointer also stays out of the partner is a separate, measured property). (iv) Under uniform random initialisation over an alphabet of size σ, the expected number of occurrences of a fixed k-symbol pattern among N regions of length L is N (L − k + 1) σ⁻ᵏ, so the expected count of a minimal open pattern shorter than every closed one exceeds that of any closed pattern by a factor σ raised to the length difference. This is a statement about expected counts, not about the probability that a pattern is present, which saturates once patterns are common (a given two-byte word is expected 4.6 times in the initial soup at L = 16; Proposition 4); it concerns the presence of the pattern, not of a replicating tiling; and nucleation is not modelled. In the Z80 at L = 16 the minimal working units, open and closed, are both four bytes long, so (iv) gives the open form no advantage there (Proposition 4); this does not hold at every length (at L = 9 the replicator is the three-byte block copier `1d ed b0`, DEC E; LDIR, tiled). *Proof.* (i) is Theorem 2. (ii) The tiling executes no control transfer, so by (H1) its pointer runs into the partner's region: it is open; its writes are its operands, by (H3) the tiling itself; completion of the copy is the per-machine fact cited. (iii) In the no-op context a straight-line program runs each address at most once before the pointer leaves the pair, and by (H2) such an execution cannot complete a copy; so a self-writer executes some address twice. (iv) is linearity of expectation. ∎ *What it shows.* In a machine that meets (H1) and (H2) and whose execution ends when the pointer leaves the pair, a self-consistent literal write is what allows a straight-line, and hence open, self-writer: without one, every self-writer repeats an address in its encounter with the no-op context. The theorem does not make a literal necessary for an open first replicator, and the data show that it is not: in BFF as published with harmless brackets, which has no literal, one of the 7 first replicators, loop-bearing, entered the partner in every encounter (and one more in 23%), and in the Z80 with the stack pointer drawn at random before each encounter the first replicator was open (its pointer in the partner in at least half of 16 traced encounters) in 18 of 20 worlds but a load–push word in only 5; in 13 it was `e1 e3` (POP HL; EX (SP),HL), a stack move that copies memory rather than writing a literal (Methods, Harness conventions). A literal channel is therefore sufficient for an open first replicator, not necessary. Whether the first replicator is open in practice is observed. The Z80 has a self-consistent literal write, and its first replicator is open (in the partner in at least half of 16 traced encounters) in 170 of 170 benign-tar worlds of 16–64 bytes (Stages G, H, K, L and M; Supplementary Table 1). BFF has none, since its instructions increment in place or copy from memory, and its first replicators are loop-bearing in 35 of 35 worlds with a replicator under the published rule, the wrapping pointer and harmless brackets: none of the 28 under the first two rules enters the partner in more than 34% of encounters (22 never), and of the 7 with harmless brackets five enter in at most 3%, one in 23% and one in every encounter. BFF with one literal-write instruction added is open first, loop-free and in the partner in every encounter, in 12 of 12 soups under each pointer rule. The theorem says nothing about how long the open phase lasts (Model 5). *Scope.* (H1) is a property of a von Neumann architecture in which a program's code lies in a shared memory and execution runs on past it: it holds in the Z80 soups, BFF, Core War^46^ and Tierra^15^, and not in Avida^16^, whose genomes are circular, so that a straight-line Avida program returns to its own start. The theorem also inherits the provisos of Theorem 2, which fail under lethal tar and in budget arms shorter than the tape. (H2) is a property of the copy instructions and must be checked per machine; Theorem 1 does not imply it (one pass over 128 addresses meets Theorem 1's bound), and we do not prove it for BFF. In the ring Z80 the premise that execution ends when the pointer leaves the pair fails, so (iii) does not apply there: the two-byte self-writer completes its copy on a second pass of the ring. For the Z80 the open-first conclusion rests on observation; the census (Proposition 3) shows that the smallest self-writers are open, but not why they come first. For a chemistry the hypotheses are a reading, not a theorem: (H1) would be a copier that runs off its template into adjacent material by default, (H3) a template whose content specifies its own complement without an interpreter.

**Lemma 7 (perfect fidelity requires zero information inflow).** *Setting.* Let X be the organism's tape, E the partner, drawn from a context distribution independently of X, and Y = F(X, E) the offspring, the partner's L bytes after the encounter. Fix an organism x. The *information inflow* into its offspring is the conditional mutual information λ(x) = I(E; Y | X = x) = H(Y | X = x) − H(Y | X = x, E), the information the offspring carries about the partner when the organism is held fixed. Execution is deterministic given (x, E), so H(Y | X = x, E) = 0 and λ(x) = H(Y | X = x): the entropy of the offspring over partners, with the organism fixed, which the partner test measures, equals the information that flows from the partner into the offspring. *Statement.* (i) If λ(x) > 0 then P(Y = x | X = x) < 1: a replicator with inflow cannot be perfectly faithful across contexts. (ii) If P(Y = x | X = x) = 1 then λ(x) = 0: perfect fidelity across contexts requires zero inflow. (iii) P(Y ≠ x | X = x) ≥ (H(Y | X = x) − 1) / log₂|𝒪| ≥ (λ(x) − 1) / log₂|𝒪|, where 𝒪 is the set of possible offspring. (iv) The converse of (ii) is false: λ(x) = 0 says that the offspring does not depend on the partner, not that it is x. *Proof.* (ii) If Y = x with probability one given X = x, then H(Y | X = x) = 0; a conditional mutual information is at most the corresponding conditional entropy, so 0 ≤ λ(x) ≤ H(Y | X = x) = 0. (i) is the contrapositive of (ii). (iii) Fano's inequality, applied to the guess Y = x, gives H(Y | X = x) ≤ 1 + P(Y ≠ x | X = x) log₂|𝒪|, and λ(x) ≤ H(Y | X = x). (iv) A regenerator of Proposition 8 whose tape carries a mutation at a position i ≥ d has the same offspring in every context, so λ = 0, and that offspring lacks the mutation, so Y ≠ x. ∎ Parts (i)–(iii) do not use determinism; it is used only to identify λ(x) with the measured entropy. *Measurement.* The partner test estimates λ(x) by the plug-in entropy of the offspring over 256 uniformly random partners; the estimate cannot exceed log₂ 256 = 8 bits and is biased downwards when the offspring takes many values. *Context.* For one generation and a fixed organism, λ(x) = 0 is the condition that the environment adds no information about the system's next state given its present one, which Bertschinger et al.^42^ use, with related conditional mutual informations, to express autonomy, and which Krakauer et al.^4^ develop into an information theory of individuality. *Reading, with the measured qualification.* Confinement (execution never enters the partner) is neither necessary nor sufficient for λ = 0: it is not sufficient because offspring positions the organism never writes keep the partner's bytes (7 of the 22 BFF first replicators whose pointer never entered the partner have H(Y | X = x) > 0, three of them ≈ 7 bits from a single unwritten byte), and it is not necessary because a copier that overwrites everything it touches has λ = 0 although its pointer enters the partner (the BFF literal that writes two or three copies of its word, whose offspring carry 0 bits of the partner while its pointer enters the partner in every encounter; Fig. 5d). Inflow comes from partial copying; openness of control flow is the mechanism that makes the Z80 first replicator's copies partial. Measured: the first replicator has λ > 0 in 80 of 80 Stage G worlds (medians 3.9, 6.2, 7.8 and 8.0 bits at L = 16, 20, 50 and 64, the last at the ceiling) and the loop-bearing successor λ = 0 in 63 of 67 (one offspring string in all 256 contexts), the four exceptions copying all but one to three bytes (Fig. 3c).

**Proposition 8 (the copy period).** The proposition is elementary. It rests on a property of the forward overlapping block move that programmers use on purpose: a move whose destination lies d bytes ahead of its source fills memory with its first d bytes repeated (with d = 1 this is the usual way to fill or clear memory with `LDIR`; the same overlap is why `memmove` and `memcpy` differ). Its interest is the consequence for heredity: in a recurring evolved core the displacement d is one byte of the genome, and it sets how many sites a lineage can transmit. The core is not new: the appendix of the second version of Cicala et al.^23^ lists the `XX 5e … ed b0` core with byte 0 annotated as the “partner offset”; what is added here is the count and its reading. *Overlap lemma.* Let M be a memory of P cells with addresses taken mod P, and let a forward move with displacement d, 1 ≤ d < P, perform M[s + d + k] ← M[s + k] for k = 0, 1, …, K − 1 in this order. Then the byte written at step k is the initial byte at s + (k mod d). If moreover d divides P and K ≥ P − d, the move leaves every cell s + j holding the initial byte at s + (j mod d). *Proof.* By induction on k. For k < d, cell s + k has not been written before step k, since the writes to it come at the steps congruent to k − d mod P, the first of which is P − d + k > k; it still holds its initial byte. For k ≥ d, its last write before step k came at step k − d (writes to one cell are P steps apart, and d < P) and carried, by the induction hypothesis, the initial byte at s + ((k − d) mod d) = s + (k mod d). For the second part, the write at step k lands on the cell whose offset from s is congruent to d + k mod P, hence, when d divides P, to k mod d: every cell written receives the initial byte of its own residue class, cells s to s + d − 1 keep their bytes, and the steps k < P − d reach every cell from s + d to s + P − 1. ∎ *Setting.* The Z80 soup at tape length L ≤ 64: the pair memory is a ring of 2L bytes, addresses are taken mod 2L, every register and flag except the stack pointer is zero at the start of an encounter (the stack pointer aliases the last byte of B), and an encounter executes 128 instructions, each repetition of `LDIR` counting as one. Let the organism's tape A begin with `XX 5e ed b0`, and let d = XX mod 2L satisfy 4 ≤ d ≤ L and d | L (hence d | 2L). Assume that, in every encounter, the one-byte instruction XX (a) passes control to address 1, (b) writes memory, if at all, only at addresses d to 2L − 1, and (c) leaves HL ≡ 0 and 256·D ≡ 0 (mod 2L) and BC either 0 or at least 126. Hypothesis (b) admits, for example, `f5` (PUSH AF), a working value at L = 20, which writes two bytes at the end of the partner. *Statement.* Whatever the partner holds, (1) the encounter leaves the whole ring, organism and partner, equal to the d-periodic tiling of A's first d bytes, with the partner in phase with the organism; (2) a mutation at a position i with 4 ≤ i < d, to any byte value, is carried by the offspring and, in every later encounter in which a descendant runs as the organism, by that descendant's offspring; (3) the byte at a position i ≥ d has no effect on either tape after the encounter, so a mutation there is erased from the offspring and from the parent; (4) outside the four-byte core, the positions at which every mutant value is transmitted are therefore exactly the d − 4 positions 4 to d − 1. *Proof.* By (a) and (b), XX hands control to address 1 and leaves addresses 0 to d − 1, the core among them, unchanged. `LD E,(HL)` reads address HL mod 2L = 0, which holds XX, so E = XX and, by (c), DE ≡ XX ≡ d (mod 2L). Neither HL nor DE passes 0xFFFF in the next 125 increments (after a one-byte XX that meets (a)–(c), HL is at most 0xFF00, reached with `DEC H`, and DE at most 0xFF1B, reached with `DEC DE`), so the k-th repetition of `LDIR` performs M[(d + k) mod 2L] ← M[k mod 2L]. By (c), BC is 0 (65,536 repetitions) or at least 126, so `LDIR` repeats for all 126 executions left, provided its own bytes at addresses 2 and 3 do not change. They do not: apply the overlap lemma with s = 0 and P = 2L to the memory at the start of the move, which agrees with the encounter's initial memory at addresses 0 to d − 1 (a byte XX wrote at address d or above is overwritten by the move before it is read, since the move reads address k ≥ d only after writing it at step k − d); every write therefore gives its cell A's byte of the cell's residue class mod d, and addresses 0 to 3 lie below d, so they receive their own bytes. Execution therefore stays at addresses 0 to 3, and the lemma, with K = 126 ≥ 2L − d (because L ≤ 64 and d ≥ 4), leaves every address a holding A's original byte at a mod d. Because d | L, the partner's position j, at address L + j, holds A's byte at j mod d, which is (1). Bytes 4 to d − 1 are never executed, and they are read only by the block move, as data (XX reads at most addresses 0 and 2L − 1, and `LD E,(HL)` address 0); so their values do not change the execution, and each is copied to every position congruent to it mod d. The offspring therefore begins with the same core and the same d − 4 bytes, meets (a)–(c), and the argument repeats in the next generation, which is (2). Positions d and beyond are overwritten in both tapes with copies of earlier bytes, whatever they held, which is (3); (4) follows from (2) and (3). ∎ *Why the hypotheses are needed.* Without (b) the statement is false: at L = 16, `70` (LD (HL),B) passes control to address 1 and has d = 16, but it writes 0 over address 0, so that E = 0 and nothing is copied. Without (a) it is false too: with every flag clear, the returns `RET NZ`, `RET NC`, `RET PO` and `RET P` (`c0`, `d0`, `e0`, `f0`) are taken, write no memory and leave HL, D and BC at zero, yet send control to address 256·XX + b (mod 2L), b being the partner's last byte, so that the outcome depends on the partner; their offsets divide L at L = 16 (`d0`, `f0`), 32 (`d0`, `e0`), 48 (`d0`, `f0`) and 64 (`c0`). For d ≤ 3, one of the first two repetitions overwrites a byte of the block move with an earlier byte of the core, and the next fetch is another instruction. The bound L ≤ 64 lets the 126 repetitions reach the end of the ring. *Offsets that do not divide L.* If d divides 2L but not L, the proof holds up to its last step: the ring holds the d-periodic tiling of A's first d bytes, but the partner holds it in phase r = L mod d ≠ 0, so the offspring begins with A's bytes r, r + 1, … (mod d), starts with the core only if the parent repeats the core at those positions, and the count d − 4 does not follow. If d does not divide 2L, the tiling does not close around the ring: the writes that wrap past address 2L − 1 bring bytes of other residue classes onto the core, in general before the budget is spent, and the proposition says nothing. *Remarks.* The variant `1e NN ed b0` (`LD E,n ; LDIR`) has offset d = NN mod 2L and the same count; its block move has 127 repetitions. The 4 in d − 4 counts the core, the four bytes that are executed: XX, `LD E,(HL)` and the two bytes of `LDIR`. A change there alters what is executed: at position 0 another working value XX′ ≡ XX (mod 2L) keeps the offset and is carried, `00` sets d = 0 and nothing is copied, and `HALT` (`76`) at position 1 or 2 stops execution before the copy; the single-mutant scan counts a site only if most of its values are carried, so the core contributes none. In the soups (Fig. 6c), 2,289 of the 2,343 confined block-copy cells whose offset divides L have exactly d − 4 transmissible sites in the single-mutant scan, the median equals d − 4 in all 15 (L, d) groups, and the three cells whose offset does not divide L have 0, 3 and 0 sites. A transmitter is the case d = L: it copies itself verbatim and carries L − 4 sites; every d < L regenerates, rewriting the organism's own body from its first d bytes.
