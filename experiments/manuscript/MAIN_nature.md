# Heredity before individuality in a digital primordial soup

*Nature Article draft v3 (summary, introduction and discussion rewritten 2026-10-08 night to lead with the explanation; results sections to be condensed next). Title 58 characters; alternative: "Open replicators evolve closure in a digital primordial soup". Summary ≤ 200 words. Every number is from a generated table named in `manuscript/NUMBERS_INDEX.md`.*

---

Theories of the origin of life disagree about what came first but agree about what the transition was: a pattern that merely spread became an individual, whose future depends on itself rather than on its surroundings^1–4^. That step has never been watched. We watched it in thousands of worlds of random machine code executing one another, with nothing selected. The order of events is forced by arithmetic: a copier that writes fewer symbols than it reads cannot finish a copy of itself in one pass, so its execution must either run on into its surroundings or come back on itself. The first replicator takes the first way: a two-byte word that writes itself when executed; its offspring take seven of eight bits from the neighbour. Evolution's first act is the second way: one jump that returns execution inside the organism, after which the offspring take nothing from the neighbour. Individuality is a return in control flow, not a boundary in space. In a second machine, one instruction decides whether life begins open and the lethality of the sterile order preceding life decides how the open phase ends. The first living things are the simplest objects their chemistry makes, dependent on their surroundings and no more complex than the order around them.

## Main

What distinguishes a living thing from the chemistry around it has been answered in two ways. One tradition lists properties, metabolism, compartment and heredity, and organises the origin of life around which came first^8–11^. The other locates life in a relation: a living system is one whose operations produce the organisation that produces them, so that its future depends on its own past rather than on its environment, in Maturana and Varela's operational closure^1^, Rosen's closure to efficient causation^2^, Fontana and Buss's organisational closure^3^ and, made quantitative, the information-theoretic individuality of Krakauer and colleagues^4^. The second view coincides with the moment population genetics identifies as the start of Darwinian evolution proper, when vertical descent comes to dominate the communal exchange of an early world^14,17^. Whatever the first replicating molecules were, this is the transition that made them organisms, and no experimental system has yet allowed it to be observed as it happens, repeated under controlled variation, and taken apart.

Soups of random programs come close. When random byte strings for a simple machine are made to execute one another, with no fitness function and no replacement, self-replicating programs arise and take over^5^, as they did earlier in the Coreworld^6^ and among random Lisp expressions^7^, and as they do for the Z80 processor^12^. The phenomenon is robust, and it reproduces the gross signature of a transition to life, a collapse of diversity and a rise in compressibility. What has not been asked is what the first replicator is, why that program and not another, what becomes of it after it fills the soup, and which properties of the machine decide these outcomes; nor has heredity been measured directly, the transition having been inferred from compression statistics of the whole soup. We treated such a soup as an experiment and as a test of one explanation. The explanation is arithmetic: a copier that writes fewer symbols than it reads cannot complete a copy of itself in one pass over its own cells, so its first pass must end either by running on into whatever lies beyond it or by returning to a cell it has already executed. If life in a soup begins with the smallest thing that writes itself, it must begin open, and individuality, in the sense of the theories above, must be a later invention that closes the path of execution. We ran 3,560 worlds of random Z80 code on graphics processors across thirteen deletions of instruction families, program lengths from 3 to 100 bytes, execution budgets, mutation rates and memory geometries, with confirmatory stages pre-registered with predictions and kill criteria (Methods). Heredity is measured by intervention: a program is lifted out of the soup, executed against fresh random partners, and its offspring are tested in turn, so that a pattern that merely spreads is not mistaken for one that inherits; the information an offspring carries about its partner is measured the same way. We then repeated the central experiment on a second, minimal machine whose instruction set we could edit, and proved what could be proved.

Here we show that life in these worlds begins in a fixed order. A sterile order of self-written zero bytes forms first. Then a two-byte word that loads itself into a register and pushes the register into memory becomes the first heritable replicator in almost every world; it needs no loop and never reads its surroundings, but its execution runs on from its last byte into its partner, and its offspring are mostly the partner's. Within a few hundred thousand interactions it is displaced, convergently and down to the byte, by a descendant carrying one control-flow cycle whose offspring are entirely its own. A theorem says why closure requires a cycle and why an instruction that writes its own operand forces the first replicator to be open; a second machine confirms that adding such an instruction switches the open beginning on, and that the lethality of the order preceding life decides how the open phase ends. The first living thing is the simplest object its chemistry makes, no more complex than the sterile order around it, and structure-reading detectors fire on that order before life exists.

### A soup with nothing to select for

The soup holds 20,000 programs ("tapes") of L bytes, randomly initialised (Fig. 1a). At each step 8,192 random cells are drawn, each paired with one of its four neighbours on a 160 × 125 lattice, and about 3,980 of these pairs are active; each pair is concatenated into a 2L-byte ring, executed by a Z80 core for 128 instructions starting at the first byte of the first tape with the stack pointer at the last byte of the second, and written back. A background mutation replaces a fixed number of random bytes per step. There is no fitness function and no replacement: a tape changes only by being executed or written into. The sweeps ran on 50 parallel GPUs (Methods).

Replication is measured by a *culture test* (Fig. 1b): a tape is executed against 64 random partners; the fraction of partners that become ≥ 75% copies of it is its copy success, and the tape is *heritable* if its offspring, run against fresh partners, produce copies in turn (gen2 ≥ 0.3). The test matters because the most common byte pattern in a soup is often not a replicator: a program may write an inert pattern into its neighbours, and a return-address smear can occupy 0.5% of the soup without inheriting anything. We date emergence by the first heritable exemplar among the three most common tapes with a share of at least 0.5%, and follow the heritable fraction of random cells as the population measure.

### Tar, then an open replicator

In every unablated world that produces life the same sequence follows (Fig. 1c,d). First comes *tar*: at the moment the first pattern reaches 10% occupancy, 24–34% of all bytes are zero at every tape length from 9 to 100. The zeros are self-written — `PUSH`, `CALL` and `RST` with zero registers and return addresses write zeros wherever the stack pointer points, and zero is a no-op — compressible, spreading and sterile: its carriers have gen2 ≈ 0. It is the digital analogue of Benner's tar^13^, order without heredity made by the same chemistry that will make life.

Then the first heritable replicator appears, and it is the same program almost everywhere: the two-byte word `01 c5`, `LD BC,nn ; PUSH BC`, tiled across the tape. In the pre-registered confirmatory stage it is the modal first tape in 19/20, 12/20, 14/20 and 18/20 worlds at L = 16, 20, 50 and 64, the rest being its register variants, at a Kaplan–Meier median of 450–750 steps regardless of L (Fig. 1d). Its mechanism is a tautology (Fig. 1b): the literal `01 c5` is both the instruction `LD BC,$01c5` and the data it loads; `PUSH BC` writes those two bytes onto the stack, which lies in the partner, and the pointer advances to the next copy of the same literal. Code equals data equals literal. It needs no loop, because the ring brings the pointer back; no counter, because every byte of the tape is the same word; and no test of its surroundings, because it never reads them. It is also nearly the only thing of its size that can replicate: of all 65,536 two-byte words tiled to 16 bytes, exactly five leave an all-zero partner holding a copy, all load–push pairs (`01 c5`, `11 d5`, `21 e5`, and the memory-load variants `2a e5`/`e5 2a`), and none copies into more than 81% of random partners. A further 246 words pass the heredity threshold without producing a single 75% copy — `CALL` instructions smearing return addresses — which is why we require faithfulness as well as heredity before calling anything a replicator.

### Closure evolves without a membrane

The pusher does not stay. It is *open*: executed against 256 random partners it copies into a median 0.66–0.73 of them (range 0.56–0.84 over 80 worlds and four lengths) and loses a quarter or more of its own bytes in a median 0.06–0.43 of encounters (Fig. 2a,b). The failures have one cause. After writing its copy the pointer runs on into the partner and executes the partner's bytes, which can poison the register pair before the next push. Against all-zero partners the pusher copies perfectly; its success falls as the density of active bytes in the partner rises, and no execution budget rescues it.

By 300,000 steps the dominant tape at L = 16 carries a control-flow instruction in 20/20 worlds and 17 of them end on the identical sequence `ad e3 21 e3 21 c0 ad c0` — `XOR L ; EX (SP),HL ; LD HL,nn ; RET NZ` — whose `RET NZ` returns the pointer into the organism's own body; at L = 50 all 20 worlds carry a `JR NZ` inserted into the pusher tiling, 14 of them byte-identical (Fig. 2e). Across the 233 unablated worlds of the exploratory stages, 92 gained a control-flow instruction between the first replicator and the final dominant and 3 lost one (exact McNemar P = 7 × 10⁻²⁴). Every such successor copies into 1.00 of 256 random partners with 0.00 self-damage, whatever instruction implements the cycle: `RET NZ`, `JR NZ`, `DJNZ`, `JP`, `JP (HL)`, `RET`, or the hardware loops `LDIR` and `LDDR` (Fig. 2a,b). The population tells the same story (Fig. 2c): at L = 16 the heritable fraction of random cells stays between 0.07 and 0.19 for the first 20,000 steps after emergence — the pusher's reign is a world of damaged copies — and rises to 0.75–0.94 when the closed successor takes over.

We call this property *closure*: the organism's bytes are a forward-invariant set under execution for every context (Methods). It is Maturana and Varela's operational closure made countable, and Krakauer's individuality made measurable by intervention: the closed organism's future depends on its own past alone. Note what closure is not. It is not a membrane; the closed organism's bytes are as exposed to writes as before. It is not a test of the environment; `RET NZ` tests a flag the organism set itself. It is a cycle in the organism's control flow that never leaves the organism (Fig. 2d). Closure takes longer the larger the organism: at L = 20, 19/20 worlds are closed by one million steps, at L = 64 only 8/20, with 12 still dominated by the open pusher, which there is also the least damaged open form in the atlas (self-damage 0.06) and so under the weakest pressure to close. The pre-registered expectation of ≥ 10/20 closures at both lengths was met at L = 20 only behaviourally — that family closes with `LDIR`, which our syntactic criterion did not count — and not at L = 64 (Methods, Pre-registration).

### Three load-bearing instructions

Which instructions does this need? The seed-paired ablation atlas (Fig. 3a) finds three load-bearing parts and nothing else. Removing *immediate loads* leaves 2/10 worlds alive at 300,000 steps; removing *stack writers* delays emergence 160- to 992-fold in median and hands the soup to a replicator built on the block-copy instruction `LDIR`; removing *all copying* (loads, pushes and block copy, 112 opcodes) or leaving only read-modify-write instructions produces no replicator in 40 worlds at one million steps. Every other ablation — block copy, calls and returns, memory loads, I/O, exchanges — is within seed-paired noise of the unablated soup. The mutation rate chooses the family that holds the soup, and the execution budget decides whether an organism can be faithful: at L = 100 the `LDIR` regime is faithful in 1/10 worlds at 128 steps per encounter and 10/10 at 256, while at 64 steps no exemplar ever reaches 0.5% of the soup yet 6/10 final soups are heritable populations with no clone — heredity carried by partial copies, the communal regime Woese placed before the Darwinian threshold^14^.

At L = 9 removing the stack writers makes life *more* likely (12/20 vs 6/20 worlds at 128 steps and 19/20 vs 5/20 at 512, Cochran–Mantel–Haenszel P = 3.9 × 10⁻⁶; Fig. 3d), because the tar they produce destroys nascent `LDIR` copiers before they assemble: an `LDIR` tape reaches the ten most common tapes in 5/20 worlds at a median 118,500 steps with stack writers present, against 20/20 within 3,400 steps without, and programs are destroyed 6.8 against 1.3 times per 1,000 interactions. Tar is not a nursery; it is a hazard that only an open literal-writer, which cannot be poisoned by zeros, gets through.

### No size floor, a geometry switch

Emergence is certain from L = 16 to L = 100 (10/10 worlds at every length under four control arms) and its median time rises only eightfold over that range, and not at all when the execution budget grows with the tape (Fig. 1d): the pusher costs two bytes however long the tape, because tiling makes a large organism of a small word. Below 16 there is no monotone floor but a dead zone (Fig. 3b): life at L = 5 and 6 (whole-tape and period-3 copiers, slowly), at L = 8 and from L = 16 up; none at L = 3, 4 and 7; and 4/10, 4/10 and 6/10 worlds at L = 9, 10 and 12, exactly where the pusher's own isolated heritability dips to 0.20–0.32. The dead zone is geometric: the stack pointer descends two bytes per push while the instruction pointer ascends, and on a 2L-byte ring they race; when a push lands on the literal the pointer is about to read, the register pair is poisoned. Padding the pair memory to a longer ring without changing the organism moves the collision and switches the dead zone off (Fig. 3c): at L = 12, rings of 28 or 36 bytes give 10/10 worlds alive at 350–600 steps where the native 24-byte ring gives 6/10 at a median 245,500. Closure, too, has a geometric channel: the `RET NZ` closer returns to an absolute address and copies 0.00 of partners on any ring but its own, whereas a relative-jump closer keeps control closed on every ring. Control can be made coordinate-free; this machine's reproductive channel cannot.

### Tar fools compression detectors

The transition of Agüera y Arcas et al. is detected by a drop in high-order entropy, byte entropy minus compressed size^5^. In our unablated soups that detector is anti-correlated with life at L = 25, 49, 64 and 81 (area under the curve 0.07–0.34 against the culture-test ground truth), because the zero flood compresses better than the replicators that replace it; the fraction of non-unique tapes and the raw bits per byte are robust (≥ 0.84 at every length but one), and occupancy of the dominant genotype is a poor proxy for a heritable population (0.61–0.95 by length). Tar is more orderly than life, and a one-byte organism, as we show below, is as compressible as tar. Any agnostic biosignature that scores order without testing propagation will be fooled by the chemistry that makes life possible.

### A literal channel starts life open

Why is the first replicator open, and must it be? Two counting arguments answer (Methods, Theorems). A straight-line program's pointer runs on into its partner, so a straight-line replicator is open by construction; and a closed replicator must execute more bytes of code than it has, because each byte of code writes fewer than one byte, so its pointer must revisit an address: closure requires a cycle. A straight-line replicator exists only if the instruction set can write a *literal* — content independent of the memory being overwritten — at a self-advancing pointer. The Z80 can (`LD rr,nn ; PUSH rr`). BFF, the Brainfuck family of Agüera y Arcas et al., cannot: `+` and `−` increment in place, `.` and `,` copy from memory, and a one-pass execution of 128 instructions cannot copy a 64-byte tape with three or more distinct bytes (it needs 64 writes, 63 head moves and 2 source changes). An exhaustive search over 16.4 million structured straight-line BFF programs under a wrapping pointer found only one-symbol fills, the BFF analogue of tar.

Eighty-four soups of 2¹⁷ programs run for 16,384 epochs test the consequences (Fig. 4). In BFF as published, 9 of 24 worlds produced a heritable replicator; every first replicator contains a loop (9/9), none is open (0/9), and seven of nine are closed by our strict criterion from the first sample in which they appear. A wrapping pointer produces more transitions (19/24; Fisher P = 0.008) but the same birth: 19/19 loop-bearing, 0/19 open, 16/19 closed. Adding one instruction that writes its own two operand bytes below the write head — the literal channel — changes the beginning completely: in 12/12 worlds the first replicator is the one-byte tiling of that instruction, whose operands are two more copies of itself, open (its pointer enters the partner in 100% of encounters), loop-free, and present at the first sample, epoch 64, with 53–60% of the soup. It is the `01 c5` of BFF. And then it dies: within about 200 epochs each of the 12 soups turns into tar that halts a pointer within a few dozen steps (an unmatched `]` ends BFF execution), copies into such partners are cut short and sterile, and the open replicator persists only as a 0.2% minority, heritable in isolation and unable to spread. No closed class evolves, and none can: no closed heritable replicator exists among 1.3 million periodic programs of this language up to period 10 (Methods). The open phase is therefore a *window*, not a stage, and one more switch shows what sets its length. Making an unmatched bracket a no-op instead of a halt — benign tar — leaves the same open replicator in place in 12/12 worlds for all 16,384 epochs, with every random tape heritable at the end and no collapse; and since no closed design exists in this language (none among 1.3 million periodic programs up to period 10, Methods), none evolves: a substrate in which life is open forever. Open here means that execution enters the partner; by the information criterion this organism is already closed, because under a wrapping pointer its pushes lap the ring and overwrite everything, so its offspring is the same string in nearly every context (information inflow 0.04 bits, Methods), which is why it never needs to close by control flow. Keeping the literal but removing the wrapping pointer still starts life open in 12/12 worlds, but the one-pass bandwidth makes the wave weak (it reaches half the soup in 5/12 worlds) and it dies out in all. Together these complete a classification (Fig. 4d): a self-consistent literal channel decides whether life starts open or closed; the lethality of the tar decides whether an open beginning lasts; and the instruction set's supply of copyable closers decides whether a lasting open phase ends in closure, as in the Z80, or never does.

### Closure as a theorem

The architectural statement holds for any sequential shared-memory machine whose pointer runs on into adjacent memory by default and whose code writes less than one byte per byte executed (Methods, Theorem 6): every closed replicator contains an executed cycle; a self-consistent literal-write instruction yields a straight-line, hence open, replicator the size of the literal; and so the smallest open replicator is strictly smaller than the smallest closed one and is present in a random soup with exponentially higher probability. In information terms (Lemma 7) closure is zero inflow of information from context to offspring, I(o; e | x) = 0, the limit that perfect fidelity across contexts requires; selection for fidelity is selection against listening to the environment. Measured by intervention, the first replicator's offspring carry a median 7.0 bits about the partner (80 worlds; 3.9 bits at L = 16, 8.0 at L = 64) and its loop-bearing successor's carry none in 63 of 67 worlds; the four exceptions copy all but one to three bytes, and those bytes remain the partner's. What the theorems do not decide is the dynamics after the open beginning: closure arrives with probability ≈ 1 − exp(−q∫n dt), where ∫n dt is the integral of the open population over time and q the rate of closing mutations. Benign tar (Z80 zeros are no-ops) lets the integral grow until closure is found; lethal tar (BFF brackets halt the pointer) closes the window first (Fig. 5).

### Discussion

The order of events is not a contingency of one machine. It follows from counting: whatever writes fewer symbols than it reads must, on its first pass over itself, either leave itself or return, and the smallest thing that writes itself has no room for a return. So the first replicator is open, and openness has a measurable meaning, the information its offspring carry about the context it copied into, which here is seven of eight bits. Closure is the return, and it too has a measurable meaning, zero bits, which four different instructions achieve in different worlds. The explanation has no adjustable part: the dichotomy is arithmetic, the literal channel is a property one reads off a substrate, and the lethality of by-products is a measurement. It reaches beyond its origin, to any sequential copying process with overhead, and it answers questions it was not built for: why the first replicators are sloppy and only partly heritable; why individuality can precede compartments; why a cycle, not a membrane, is the first thing selection builds; why complexity-based signatures of life must fail at the origin, the first living thing being no more complex than the sterile order that precedes it; and why the first innovations after replication should concern control, where copying starts, stops and returns, rather than function or code. The boundary of the rule is itself a prediction, and the second machine confirmed it: a copier that writes at least as much as it reads can close by saturation, overwriting everything it touches, and needs no return; the literal pusher of BFF under a wrapping pointer does exactly this, entering its partner in every encounter yet importing nothing from it.

These are predictions for chemistry, not results about it, and they can be tested. Template copying is a literal channel: a strand specifies its complement by physical fit, with no interpreter, so the first replicators in such a chemistry should be the shortest self-templating repeats, dependent on their surroundings and productive mainly of partial copies; the experiment is to measure heritable propagation among sloppy copies by transfer into fresh material, and openness as the variation of the product with its context, rather than to search for a clean self-replicating species. By-products are a variable, not a nuisance: whether a chemistry's tar is inert or inhibitory to the copying process should decide whether an open phase lasts long enough for control to evolve, which a screen of nonenzymatic systems with matched inert and inhibitory additives can test^13^. Processivity is the chemical write ratio: vertical lineages should appear exactly when one copying event spans the genome, and below that heredity should be carried by the population without clones, as our 64-step soups show and as polymerase ribozymes of increasing processivity could test^45^. In laboratory evolution of RNA replicators the earliest fixed changes should be of the control kind, circularisation, terminators, primers^42^. For the search for life elsewhere the lesson is methodological: abundant low-complexity order precedes and surrounds the first replicators and trips compression and assembly measures alike^19,44^, so detection should be by intervention, adding fresh substrate and looking for propagation that depends on the inoculum, and order without heredity should be sought as a precursor signature. What limits the claims is plain. One real instruction set and one toy language were studied; the culture test is our own ground truth, though the order of events is reproduced under every setting we tried; closure at the largest tape lengths is in progress rather than complete within our horizons; and interactions in our soup are local, so the open phase may owe its persistence to neighbourhoods of kin, which a well-mixed control now under way will decide^41^. Within those limits the result stands: heredity came first, individuality was a return in control flow, and the step that made a replicator an organism was the step that made it stop listening to its environment.

## Methods

**Soup.** The soup holds N = 20,000 tapes of L bytes (L from 3 to 100), initialised with uniformly random bytes, on a 160 × 125 square lattice with local interaction. At every step 8,192 cells are drawn uniformly at random and each is paired with one of its four lattice neighbours (a step off the edge is reflected; the lattice does not wrap); a pair is active if neither member is already in another pair that step, which leaves ≈ 3,980 active pairs (0.395 active interactions per cell per step; the count varies by a few per cent with concurrent GPU load and does not affect results). An active pair is concatenated, organism first, into a pair memory of P bytes (P = 2L except in the padding experiment of Stage F, where P ≥ 2L and the padding bytes are never written back), executed by the Zilion Z80 core (`@neovand/zilion` 0.2.0, WGSL) for a fixed budget of 128 instructions (32, 64, 256, 512, 1,024 or 2,048 in the budget arms) from byte 0 with all registers zero and the stack pointer at the last byte of the partner, and both tapes are written back. After each step 8,192/2^k bytes chosen uniformly at random over the soup are replaced by random bytes (k = 4 unless stated; k = 1–6 in the mutation arms). There is no fitness function, no replacement and no death. Instruction-set ablations replace whole opcode families by no-ops through a prefix-aware fetch hook that sees the operation after `DD`/`FD` and `ED`/`CB` prefix resolution; the thirteen ablations and their opcode counts are listed in Extended Data Table 2 (`none`, `block-copy` (4 opcodes), `stack-writes` (46), `stack-write-only` (22), `stack-read-only` (24), `push-only`, `ex-sp-only`, `call-rst`, `call-rst-write`, `ld-mem`, `ld-imm`, `ld-reg`, `all-ld`, `ed-loads`, `cb-page`, `no-copy` (112), `rmw-only`). The emulator was validated against an independent Z80 reference with zero genuine divergences in randomised 5,000-pair tests with and without ablation; it is not treated as ground truth for the Z80 itself, and no claim depends on cycle-exact timing.

**Recording.** Every run writes a sample at fixed steps (every step for the first 30, then a geometric schedule, then every 500 or 1,000) with: the ten most common exact tapes and their shares; quasispecies occupancy (fraction of cells within Hamming distance ⌈L/4⌉ of the dominant tape); byte histogram, zero-byte fraction and byte entropy; brotli-compressed bits per byte (quality 2) and the high-order entropy of ref. 5; species entropy and unique-tape fraction; per-interaction statistics (bytes written into the partner, copy events, destroyed programs); 16 random tapes for the functional census; and full soup snapshots at fixed steps and at emergence.

**Culture test and partner tests.** A tape runs as the first program against 64 uniformly random partners for the condition's budget; copy success is the fraction of partners that become ≥ 75% copies at the best cyclic shift; gen2 is the copy success of the produced copies against fresh partners, weighted by the copy fraction; heritable means gen2 ≥ 0.3, faithful additionally ≥ 50% of partners copied. Partner-independence tests use 256 random partners and record self-damage (≥ 25% of the organism's bytes changed in one encounter). In BFF the culture test also records whether the instruction pointer entered the partner in each encounter; closed means ≤ 5% of encounters entered and ≥ 95% of partners copied, open means ≥ 50% entered. Information inflow from the environment, I(o; e | x) = H(o | x) for a deterministic executor, is the plug-in entropy in bits of the offspring (the partner's bytes after the encounter) over 256 random partners, ceiling 8 bits (`results/biology/individuality/`).

**Events and statistics.** `t_rep` is the first sampled step at which one of the three most common tapes has a share ≥ 0.5% and is heritable by the culture test (a clonality criterion by design: it dates the first *clone*, not the first heritable material); `t_faith` additionally requires faithfulness; `tq_10` (quasispecies occupancy ≥ 10%) is the pre-registered occupancy event and is reported because it fires on zero floods as well as on replicators. Emergence fractions are quoted as worlds reaching the event within the horizon with Wilson 95% intervals; times as Kaplan–Meier medians with censoring at the horizon. Ablation effects are ratios of Kaplan–Meier medians with seed-paired sign tests (the same seeds run under every ablation); confirmatory contrasts use one-sided Fisher exact tests and the Cochran–Mantel–Haenszel test pooled over budgets; the paired gain of control flow uses the exact McNemar test; between-variant transition rates in BFF use two-sided Fisher tests and Mann–Whitney tests on event times with non-transitioning runs censored at the horizon. In BFF, where replicator populations are quasispecies with small exact-class shares, the "first replicator" is the most common tape class (a tape and its reverse count as one class) at the first sample at which it is heritable, and the population event is a heritable fraction ≥ 0.5 of 32 random tapes; `t_rep` is reported alongside. All thresholds (0.75 copy, gen2 ≥ 0.3, 0.5% share, 10% occupancy, 95%/5% closure) were fixed before the confirmatory stages. Same-seed Z80 runs diverge because GPU scheduling makes the pairing order non-deterministic, so every Z80 claim is a population claim over seeds (ten same-seed repeats spread as widely as ten seeds); the BFF soup is bitwise reproducible across machines (identical epoch statistics on an Apple GPU and an NVIDIA L40S for six seeds over 16,384 epochs).

**Definitions and theorems.** A *self-writer* is a tape fixed under its own execution in some context. *Closure*: the organism's bytes are forward-invariant under execution for every context in the class of uniformly random partners. Theorem 1: no straight-line execution in one-pass BFF copies a 64-byte tape with three or more distinct byte values (writes + writer-head moves + source changes exceed 128). Theorem 2: if bytes written per byte of code executed is below one, a closed full replicator executes some address twice. Theorem 6: for any machine in which the pointer runs on into adjacent memory absent a control transfer and Theorem 2's bandwidth condition holds, a self-consistent literal-write instruction implies a straight-line (open) replicator the size of the literal, and its absence implies every replicator is loop-bearing. Lemma 7: I(o; e | x) > 0 implies imperfect fidelity across contexts; perfect fidelity implies I = 0. Proposition 3 (computer-assisted): the two-byte fixed points of the Z80 against the zero partner are exactly the five load–push words. Full statements and proofs in Supplementary Information.

**BFF.** cubff default semantics verified from source: two 64-byte tapes concatenated, pointer and heads at byte 0, ten instructions, every other byte a no-op, unmatched brackets halt, 2¹³ steps per encounter, 2¹⁷ programs, random ordered pairs each epoch, mutation 2⁻¹² per byte per epoch. Switches: `ip_wrap` (pointer wraps modulo 128); `literal` (byte 0x50 = `P a b`: write a at head1−1 and b at head1−2, then head1 −= 2, skip the operands); `nohalt` (unmatched brackets are no-ops). Our GPU kernel reproduces cubff's validation replicator and matches a CPU reference on randomised tests. 24 seeds each for the published and wrapping variants, 12 for each literal variant; events every 64 epochs; high-order entropy with brotli quality 2 as in ref. 5.

**Searches.** Two-byte census: all 65,536 words tiled to 16 bytes, culture test (32 partners) and partner test (256), plus the deterministic fixed-point test against the zero partner. BFF straight-line search: 16,447,860 programs (prefix ≤ 3 instructions + tiling unit of period 2–5 with a copy instruction), 2 random partners, wrapping pointer, alphabet prefilter then exact similarity, full culture test for survivors. Closed-replicator search: all 1,309,528 periodic tilings of {`P`, `[`, `]`, no-op} up to period 10 under the literal, wrapping, no-halt rules, three partners each.

**Pre-registration.** Stages D, F and G and the BFF cells were pre-registered with predictions and kill criteria before launch; every amendment is dated in the change log. Misses reported: the heritable-fraction thresholds of G1(c) failed at L = 50; the syntactic loop criterion of G2 omitted hardware loops (6/20 and 3/20 syntactic against 19/20 and 8/20 behavioural); the mechanism first claimed for an open BFF replicator under a wrapping pointer was falsified by the harness test before any soup ran and the prediction restated with both readings; a held prediction for the non-wrapping literal cell was corrected by the counting argument before it was run.

**Experimental stages.** Stage A (630 runs, L = 16): seven ablations × budgets {32, 128, 512} × mutation {1/4, 1/16, 1/64}, ten seeds, 300,000 steps. Stage B (640): tape lengths 4–100 under two ablations and three budgets. Stage C (490): thirteen ablations at L = 16, seeds 101–110, with one-million-step nulls. Stage D (280, confirmatory): the L = 9 reversal with seeds 1001–1020. Stage E (1,230): the size axis under four control arms, budget and mutation sweeps at L = 100, variance and exception cells. Stage F (210, confirmatory): padded pair memories. Stage G (80, confirmatory): closure with seeds 2001–2020 at L = 16 and 50 (300,000 steps) and L = 20 and 64 (1,000,000 steps). BFF: 24 seeds each for the published and wrapping variants, 12 for each of the three literal variants. Post hoc analyses (closure, two-byte census, detectors, invasion, census dynamics, ring sweep) used recorded data and local GPU time only.

**Compute, code and data.** 3,560 Z80 runs and 84 BFF soups on Modal L40S GPUs plus local runs on an Apple GPU; ≈ $400 at list price. The source code (simulator, assays, analysis and figure scripts), every condition file, every raw run summary, every generated table and the pre-registration documents with their dated change log are in the project repository (to be archived with a DOI on acceptance); each figure is produced from the generated tables by one script, and each number in the text is traceable to a named table.
