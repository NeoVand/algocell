# Heredity can precede individuality in a digital primordial soup

*Review copy, 2026-10-09 (revision 2). Nature Article format: summary ≤ 200 words, main text ≈ 3,600 words, six figures, Methods, Extended Data legends and a Supplementary Information with the theorems and proofs. Every number is from a generated table of the project repository; every experiment reported is complete.*

---

Theories of the origin of life disagree about what came first but agree on the transition: a spreading pattern became an individual whose future depends on itself, not on its surroundings^1–4^. We watched, repeated and dissected it in thousands of worlds of random machine code. On tapes of 16 to 64 bytes with harmless by-products, in the Z80 and an 8080-like subset, the first replicator is a two-byte word that writes itself when executed, runs on into its neighbour and makes damaged copies that vary with the neighbour; its lineages soon stop copying. Closure, a return of control flow into the organism, follows where a closing design is within reach, and seeded closers displace the open form. Closed copies no longer depend on the partner, but closers differ in heredity, and in the commonest closing core one byte decides how: the byte that sets where the copy lands sets how many sites a lineage can transmit. Closers that rewrite themselves from their first bytes erase mutations; closers that copy the whole tape keep them. The environment chooses: regenerators prevail where by-products are harmless, transmitters where they are lethal. In a second machine one instruction decides whether life begins open.

## Main

What distinguishes a living thing from the chemistry around it has two answers: a list of properties, metabolism, compartment and heredity, with the question of which came first^5–9^, or a relation: a living system is one whose operations produce the organisation that produces them, so that its future depends on its own past rather than on its environment^1–4,10,11^. The relation is also where population genetics places the start of Darwinian evolution proper, when vertical descent comes to dominate communal exchange^12,13^. No experimental system has allowed this transition to be observed as it happens, repeated under controlled variation and taken apart.

Digital organisms usually descend from a designed ancestor^14,15^; random-program soups come closer. When random byte strings for a simple machine execute one another, with no fitness function and no replacement, self-replicating programs arise and take over^16–21^. For the Z80 processor the first replicators are load–push programs that give way to a block-copy family^16,22^, a takeover attributed to mutational robustness^22^; whether the successors descend from the first replicators, and how, has not been asked. We treated such a soup as a test of one hypothesis: that the smallest programs that write themselves run on into their surroundings, so that life begins open, and that individuality, a path of execution that returns into the organism, is a later invention. We ran more than 3,600 worlds of random Z80 code, varying the instruction set, program length, execution budget, mutation rate and memory geometry, with confirmatory stages pre-registered with kill criteria (Methods). Heredity is measured by intervention, as in serial-transfer tests of self-replication^23^: a program is lifted out and cultured against fresh random partners. We repeated the central experiment on an 8080-like subset of the Z80 and on a second, minimal machine whose instruction set we could edit, and recorded every encounter of 20 worlds to rebuild their genealogies.

### Order before life

The soup holds 20,000 programs of L bytes on a 160 × 125 lattice (Fig. 1a). At each step 8,192 random cells are each paired with a lattice neighbour, the pair is executed as one program for 128 instructions and written back, and a background mutation replaces a fixed number of random bytes; nothing else happens (Methods). A tape is *heritable* if, lifted out and executed against random partners, it makes copies that copy in turn; the heritable fraction of random cells is the population measure.

The first thing to form is order that does not inherit. When the most common pattern first occupies a tenth of the soup, 24–34% of all bytes are zero at every tape length from 9 to 100 (one world in Fig. 2). Push, call and reset instructions executed with empty registers write zeros wherever the stack pointer points, and zero is itself an instruction that does nothing, so the pattern spreads by being written and changes nothing when executed. We call this flood *tar*, after the asphalt that prebiotic chemistry makes from the reactions that also make sugars^24^. The tar is what detectors see: the high-order entropy used to detect the transition^16^ is near chance or inverted at six of eight lengths from 25 to 100 bytes, an assembly measure of selection^25,26^ passes ten times its random-soup value before the first heritable replicator in 71 of 80 worlds, and the first replicator's assembly index is the minimum for its length, shared with the tar (Extended Data Fig. 1). Only intervention tells the two apart.

### The first replicator is open

The first heritable replicator is the same program everywhere: the two-byte word `01 c5`, or one of its register variants, repeated to fill the tape (Fig. 1a). On tapes of 16 to 64 bytes with benign tar it comes first in 170 of 170 worlds, across five lengths, the Z80 and its 8080-like subset, and two pairing rules, at Kaplan–Meier medians of 300–750 steps; on shorter tapes block copiers come first (Fig. 1b), and the order survives randomised registers and stack pointer (Methods). Executed, the word loads its own next two bytes into a register pair and writes that pair where the stack pointer points, in the partner: the program is its own data, and it copies itself as a side effect of being run, with no loop, no counter and no test of its surroundings. It is nearly the only thing of its size that replicates: of all 65,536 two-byte words tiled to 16 bytes, exactly five, besides the zero word, copy themselves into an all-zero partner, all of them load-and-push pairs (Supplementary Information, Proposition 3). Without immediate loads 2 of 10 worlds produce a replicator by 300,000 steps; without the stack writers life arrives 160- to 992-fold later, as a block copier; no other ablation changed the time to the first replicator significantly (seed-paired sign tests, P ≥ 0.18; Extended Data Fig. 2).

The first replicator is *open*. Its pointer fetches partner bytes in every one of 256 encounters, for every traced first replicator under benign tar (100 of 100). Against 256 random partners it copies into a median 0.66–0.73 of them and loses a quarter or more of its own bytes in a median 0.06–0.43 of encounters (Fig. 3a,b), because after writing its copy it executes the partner's bytes, which can poison the register pair before the next push. The offspring is an exact copy in a median 58% of encounters at L = 16 and almost never at L = 64, and the information that flows from the partner into it, its entropy over random partners (Supplementary Information, Lemma 7), runs from 3.9 bits at L = 16 to the 8-bit ceiling of the sample at L = 64 (Fig. 3c): what a child looks like depends in part on the neighbour it was made against. In the population the median heritable fraction of random cells stays between 0.09 and 0.19 from step 2,000 to step 20,000 at L = 16 (Fig. 3d), and copied serially into fresh random partners, 96–100% of first-replicator lineages stop copying within eight transfers (Fig. 4a).

### Closure, where a closer is within reach

The first replicator does not stay. At L = 16 the dominant tape carries a control-flow instruction in 20 of 20 worlds by 300,000 steps, 17 of them ending on the identical eight-byte sequence whose conditional return sends the pointer back into the organism's own body (Fig. 3e; Extended Data Table 1). Every such successor copies into all 256 random partners without self-damage (Fig. 3a,b,f), and in 63 of 67 loop-bearing final dominants the offspring is one and the same string in all 256 encounters (Fig. 3c). At the last snapshot every sampled heritable cell is confined in all 20 worlds at L = 16, and most are in 19 of 20 worlds at L = 20, 12 of 20 at L = 32 and 8 of 20 at L = 64 (Fig. 4d); the heritable fraction of random cells rises to 0.75–0.94 at L = 16 (Fig. 3d).

We call this property *closure*. A loop in the code, confinement of the pointer to the organism's cells and independence of the offspring from the partner travel together here, and each was measured: every loop-bearing final dominant fetches no partner byte as an instruction in any traced encounter (89 of 89), and confinement and zero inflow agree in 106 of 110 finals (Extended Data Fig. 3). Closure in this sense is a property of execution, not the closure of constraints by which organisational theories define autonomy^1,3,10^, though it is one component of individuality in the information-theoretic sense^4^. It is not a membrane, since the organism's bytes are as exposed as before, nor a property of place^27^: well-mixed pairing delayed it (7 of 10 worlds closed against 20 of 20, Fisher P = 0.03). The longer the tape, the slower and rarer closure (trend test P = 6 × 10⁻⁵): 20 of 20 worlds by 300,000 steps at L = 16, 12 of 20 by one million at L = 32 and 8 of 20 at L = 64 (Extended Data Fig. 4). At L = 50 a jump spreads but its carriers' offspring reopen, and only 18–39% of heritable cells are confined (Fig. 4d). Some closers rely on conventions of the harness: the empty registers and, at L = 20 and 50, a 16-bit program counter in a memory addressed modulo 2L (Methods).

Closure is selected where it is found. Seeded at 1% into a world filled with the pusher, the evolved return closer at L = 16 and the block-copy tiling at L = 32 held half the cells within 400–750 and 260–290 steps, in 5 of 5 soups each; the pusher seeded into a closer's world vanished in 5 of 5 (Extended Data Fig. 5). Where closure did not come, the closer was missing from what evolution had found, not from what the instruction set allows. In the 8080-like subset no world closed at L = 32 in a million steps, yet a 20-byte closer of 8080 instructions, seeded at 1%, had its loop in half the cells within 300–320 steps in 5 of 5 soups. With registers drawn at random before each encounter, closure came in 8 of 40 worlds by three million steps, and a self-initialising closer, seeded, displaced the pusher in 5 of 5 soups (Extended Data Figs 5 and 6; Methods). The barrier was discovery, not expressibility or competition.

### The first closer is assembled

Whether closers descend from the open form or displace it, and how the first one is made, cannot be read from snapshots, and the soup cannot be replayed exactly (Methods). We therefore recorded every encounter and every mutation of 20 new worlds at L = 16, from random bytes to closure, so that every cell's line of descent can be rebuilt, and checked the record by replaying encounters byte for byte (Methods). Seventeen worlds closed; on the line of descent of each, the founder is the first self-confined copier after the newest open copier (Extended Data Fig. 7a). Every founder's line runs back to open pushers, which by then was close to certain, since nearly every living cell had an open copier among its ancestors of the previous 2,000 steps. How the founder was made was not certain, and it was the same in every world: the encounter that completed it was a recombination of two tapes in 16 and an imperfect copy of a neighbour in 1, never a point mutation of a copier. It appeared a median of 79 steps (1–360) after its newest open-copier ancestor, through intermediates that could not copy themselves in 16 of 17 worlds, from bytes of a median of 3 separate events (Extended Data Fig. 7b,c). Its parts are the pusher's own words with one byte changed, `21 e3` and `21 e0`, an exchange with the stack or a return in place of the push, carried before each founder by a median 0.21% of cells, about eight times the share expected in random tapes; in 15 of 17 worlds they were joined into the motif of the return closer. In three of four exhaustive hypercubes of genotypes between a pusher and a return closer, no single-byte path through heritable genotypes reaches a heritable closer (Methods). Point mutation made the parts; recombination, a by-product of how open copiers overwrite one another, joined them. These analyses are exploratory: their definitions were settled after the data were inspected, and a replication on new worlds with the frozen pipeline is pre-registered (Methods).

### Closure regenerates or transmits

Closure need not cost a lineage its variation, but in most worlds it did. We followed every single-byte mutant of 14 genomes through eight serial transfers into fresh random partners (Fig. 4a–c; Methods). The pusher's mutations reach its first copies, at 16 of 50 and 30 of 64 positions, but not their descendants, because its lineages stop copying, mutated or not; our registered prediction that its sites persist failed. The evolved closers, the return at L = 16 and the block-copy tilings at L = 20 and 32, lose no unmutated lineage, but they copy the code they run and rebuild the rest of the tape from it: 60–88% of their mutant lineages live on with the mutation erased, and none keeps it. A third design, a short copying core followed by bytes it copies but never runs, keeps the allele in 46–63% of its mutant lineages, at 9 to 20 positions, 7 to 12 of them never executed (Fig. 4c). Such bytes are copied without being interpreted, half of what von Neumann's and Langton's descriptions do^28,29^; and erasure is not canalisation^30^, since the variation never reaches the offspring.

One byte decides which. A four-byte block-copy core recurs across stages, `XX 5e ed b0`: one instruction, then `LD E,(HL)` and the repeating block move `LDIR`; with its variant `1e NN ed b0` it is carried by 2,347 of the 15,655 confined heritable cells we scanned (Methods). Because the registers start at zero, the first byte, which ref. 22 annotates as the partner offset, is both an instruction and the copy offset: each byte is copied d = XX mod 2L addresses ahead, and the block move tiles the first d bytes around the ring, the organism included (Fig. 6c). With d < L the closer rewrites its own body from its first d bytes and erases every mutation beyond them; with d = L it copies the tape verbatim and keeps them. A closed lineage can therefore transmit d − 4 sites, set by one byte of its own code, whenever d divides L (Proposition 8, an elementary consequence of the overlapping block move). Of 2,343 confined block-copy cells sampled from every stage with such an offset, the single-mutant scan finds exactly d − 4 sites in 2,289 (98%; Fig. 6c).

A world's most common tape hides which setting it holds, since a regenerating population is nearly clonal and a transmitting one a cloud of distinct genomes, so we classified random cells (Fig. 4d,e). Under benign tar, regenerators are the majority of heritable cells at the last snapshot in 17 of 20 worlds at L = 16 and 14 of 20 at L = 20, and in 20 of 20 in the 8080 subset at L = 16. Under lethal tar, transmitters are the majority in 9 of 10 worlds, with a median of 11.5 sites they never execute, while the most common tape holds 0.2–1.5% of the soup: life that begins closed under lethal tar begins with a genome. So does most closure under random registers.

The mode belongs to the parent, which keeps it in every environment where its lineages survive (Extended Data Fig. 8a), and the environment chooses among parents. A matched regenerator and transmitter, differing only in the offset byte and in what follows the core, cannot be told apart in single encounters (Extended Data Fig. 6e), yet populations converge from either side to a share that the tar sets: at L = 32 transmitters hold 0.03–0.37 of the core-carrying cells after 300,000 steps with benign tar and 0.55–0.87 with lethal tar, whether they start at 1%, 50% or 99% (Extended Data Fig. 6a,b). Recording every encounter of 20 such soups under each rule locates the choice, post hoc (Methods). Regenerators and transmitters copy each other at rates equal to within 0.3%; what differs is how each fares against the 11–16% of cells that carry no core. A core-free executor whose pointer enters a regenerator runs the copies of the copying code that make up its body and destroys it in 0.75–0.89 of such entries, occasionally becoming a regenerator itself; one that enters a transmitter wanders through bytes never run and, under the lethal rule, is often halted by a zero first. Summed, these terms account for the drift of the transmitters' share when a soup is moved from one rule to the other, but not for why regenerators win under benign tar, which remains open. The zeros that halt intruders are not a defence: they are inherited scars of past intrusions, as common under benign as under lethal tar, and removing them keeps 88–94% of the transmitters' protection (Methods).

Robustness^31^ does not explain the succession: the first replicator is at least as robust to single mutations as its successor (0.74 against 0.59 of single mutants remain heritable at L = 16; Supplementary Fig. 2), and by the criterion of ref. 22, an exact self-copy after mutation, the matched transmitter scores 0.88 and the regenerator 0.000 (Extended Data Fig. 6f): the criterion measures the transmission of mutations, not survival. A transmitter tolerates a mutation and passes it on, a regenerator survives it by erasing it, and only tolerance builds variation^32^.

### Two properties of the substrate decide the beginning

If the order of events follows the literal channel, a machine without one should be born loop-bearing, and giving it one should start life open. BFF, the Brainfuck family of ref. 16, has no literal channel: its instructions increment in place or copy from memory, and copying a 64-byte tape into a partner that differs from it in every cell takes at least 128 instructions, 64 that change a cell and 64 that move a head, so a copier whose pointer stays in its own 64 cells must execute some cell twice (Theorem 1). In soups of 2¹⁷ programs run for 16,384 epochs (Fig. 5), BFF as published and with a wrapping pointer produced a heritable replicator in 28 of 48 worlds; every first replicator contains a loop (28 of 28), and none enters its partner in more than 34% of encounters. The absence of a literal channel predicts a loop, not confinement: with harmless brackets 7 of 12 worlds produced replicators, all loop-bearing but not all confined. Adding one instruction that writes its own two operand bytes below the write head changes the beginning: in 12 of 12 worlds under either pointer rule the first replicator is the one-byte tiling of that instruction, loop-free, with its pointer entering the partner in every encounter (Fig. 5b). It is the `01 c5` of BFF, and without a wrapping pointer its offspring carry 8 bits of the partner, the ceiling of the measurement (Supplementary Fig. 3).

Then it dies. An unmatched bracket ends BFF execution, so the soup fills with tar that halts the pointer, and by epoch 1,024 the open replicator holds under 1% of the soup; no closed class evolves (Methods). The open phase is a *window* whose length the tar sets. With harmless brackets the open replicator holds for all 16,384 epochs in 12 of 12 worlds; with brackets that halt with probability p it holds 98% of the soup at 0 < p ≤ 0.1 (18 of 18 soups) and collapses within about 900 epochs at p = 0.3 (6 of 6; Fig. 5c). When the literal writes two or three copies of its word, so that a copy completes within one pass, the open tiling's offspring carry 0 bits of the partner, information-closed from birth without a loop, and every random tape is heritable at 16,384 epochs in 12 of 12 soups (Fig. 5d).

The lethal column holds in the first machine too. When a zero byte halts the Z80, its own tar becomes lethal and the open beginning disappears: in 10 of 10 pre-registered worlds no literal pusher establishes, and life begins closed and late, as a block copier, at a Kaplan–Meier median of 38,000 steps against 450 with benign tar (Extended Data Fig. 9), mostly as transmitters. Between the two rules lies a threshold. When a fetched zero halts the pair with probability p, the first replicator is an open load–push word in 10 of 10 worlds at every p up to 0.1, in 2 of 10 at p = 0.3 and in none at p = 1, and the median share of transmitters among heritable cells at 300,000 steps rises from 0 at p ≤ 0.03 to 0.26, 0.56 and 0.78 at p = 0.1, 0.3 and 1 (Spearman ρ = 0.50, permutation P < 10⁻⁴; Extended Data Fig. 8b,d); closure timing is not monotone in p (Extended Data Fig. 8c).

The beginning is not the Z80's alone. In the 8080-like subset, made by turning every Z80-only instruction into a no-op, which keeps the literal word but removes the block copies and relative jumps (Methods), the load–push word comes first in every world, and at L = 16 the Z80's own return closer, pure 8080 code, dominates 20 of 20 worlds by 300,000 steps. Together these fill a map (Fig. 5e): a literal channel decides whether life starts open; the lethality of the tar decides whether an open beginning lasts, or happens at all; and the closed designs within reach decide whether what follows is closure, a late closed beginning, or extinction.

### What is proved

Two counting statements bound the order of events without forcing it (Fig. 6; Supplementary Information). A straight-line replicator's pointer runs on into its partner, so it is open by construction. A confined replicator, whose pointer never leaves its own cells, must execute some address at least twice, a return of control into code it has already run, wherever execution cannot stop inside it before the budget is spent, as in the Z80 soups with benign tar (128 executions, at most 100 cells), or wherever a copy costs more executions than the organism has cells, as in BFF (Theorems 1 and 2; Fig. 6a). Neither proviso holds under lethal tar, where a zero byte can stop execution inside the organism. Counting complete self-writers, the usual estimate of how likely a replicator is to arise by chance^33^, does not explain which comes first: the five of period two are open, but 558 of the 641 of period four are closed (Fig. 6b; Proposition 3). The open word's head start, Kaplan–Meier medians of 450 steps to the first replicator and 21,000 to the first closed one at L = 16, is measured, not counted. Confinement is also not the only road to independence. For a deterministic encounter, the information that flows from the partner into the offspring is the conditional mutual information between partner and offspring given the organism, which equals the offspring's entropy over partners, and perfect fidelity requires it to be zero (Lemma 7); it falls to zero in the Z80's loop-bearing finals (Fig. 3c) and in the BFF literal that writes two or three copies of its word, which enters its partner in every encounter (Fig. 5d). The dynamics lie outside the theorems: closure arrives with probability about 1 − exp(−q ∫ n dt), q being the rate at which closing variants arise and n the open population, so benign tar lets the window run until a closer is assembled and lethal tar shuts it first, a reading that the lethality dial supports only at its two ends (Supplementary Information, Model 5).

### Discussion

The order of events is observed, and the counts bound it. The first replicator is open, and its children depend on the neighbour they were made against; closure, a return of control flow into the organism, is found where a closing design is within reach and selected once found; and in the worlds we followed encounter by encounter, the first closer was not refined from an open copier by point mutation but assembled by recombination from one-byte variants of the open copier's own words, which sloppy copying keeps in circulation. Heredity came first, and the individual was built from a communal pool of parts, an observable instance of the passage from communal exchange to vertical descent that Woese placed at the origin of cells^12^. Closure is not one thing for heredity. A closer that copies only the code it runs regenerates itself and erases its mutants; one that also copies bytes it never runs keeps them; in a recurring block-copy core one byte decides which, and the environment chooses through how each body fares against the debris around it.

These are predictions for chemistry, not results about it. Template copying is a literal channel, a strand specifying its complement by physical fit with no interpreter, so the first replicators of such a chemistry should be the shortest self-templating repeats, dependent on their surroundings and productive mainly of partial copies. Whether a chemistry's by-products are inert or inhibitory should decide whether an open phase lasts long enough for control to evolve, which matched additives in nonenzymatic systems can test^24^; processivity, the chemical write ratio, should decide when vertical lineages appear^34^; and if the first self-sufficient replicators are assembled rather than refined, they should be found among the recombinants of sloppier ones, as new replicators arise by recombination in laboratory RNA evolution^35,36^. The copy offset has a chemical counterpart, the point at which a copy restarts on its own template. Detection of life at its origin should be by intervention, since low-complexity order precedes the first replicators and trips compression and assembly measures alike^25,26^.

What limits the claims is plain: the Z80, an 8080-like restriction of it and one toy language; units of fixed size, start address and pairing imposed by the harness; a culture test that is our own ground truth, though the open beginning held under both register conventions we tested, at every length from 16 to 64 bytes and under heredity thresholds from 0.2 to 0.4; closure at the largest lengths in progress rather than complete within our horizons, and a hundred times slower when the harness does not empty the registers; genealogies from 17 worlds at one length, with definitions settled after inspection; and an environmental choice between the two kinds of closer that our censuses only partly account for. Within those limits the result stands: heredity came first; independence from context came later, as a return of control flow assembled from parts the open copiers spread; and whether that return cost the organism its variation depended on one byte of its genome and on the environment that selected it.

## Methods

**Soup.** The soup holds N = 20,000 tapes of L bytes (L from 3 to 100), initialised with uniformly random bytes, on a 160 × 125 square lattice. At every step 8,192 cells are drawn uniformly at random and each is paired with one of its four lattice neighbours (a step off the edge is reflected; the lattice does not wrap); a pair is active if neither member is already in another pair that step, which leaves ≈ 3,980 active pairs. An active pair is concatenated, organism first, into a pair memory of P bytes (P = 2L except in the padding experiment of Stage F, where P ≥ 2L and the padding bytes are never written back), executed by a Z80 emulator for a fixed budget of 128 instructions (32–2,048 in the budget arms) from byte 0 with all registers zero and the stack pointer at the last byte of the partner, and both tapes are written back. After each step 8,192/2^k bytes chosen uniformly at random over the soup are replaced by random bytes (k = 4 unless stated; k ∈ {2, 4, 6} in the mutation arms of Stages A, C and D and k ∈ {1, 2, 3, 5, 8} in the Stage E mutation sweep at L = 100; the per-byte-constant arm of Stage E replaces 32·L bytes per step, the L = 16 rate per byte, at every length). There is no fitness function, no replacement and no death. Instruction-set ablations replace whole opcode families by no-ops through a prefix-aware fetch hook that sees the operation after `DD`/`FD` and `ED`/`CB` prefix resolution; the ablations and their opcode counts are listed in Supplementary Table 1. The emulator was validated against an independent Z80 reference, with and without ablation, by a differential harness that runs random programs through both (`src/lib/dev/z80difftest.ts`); no claim depends on cycle-exact timing.

**Recording.** Stages C–M write a sample at steps 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 21 and 34, then every 50 steps to step 5,000, then every 500 steps (300,000-step runs), 1,000 (one-million-step runs) or 5,000 (Stage L), with the ten most common exact tapes and their shares, quasispecies^37^ occupancy (fraction of cells within Hamming distance ⌈L/4⌉ of the dominant tape), byte histogram, zero-byte fraction and byte entropy, brotli-compressed bits per byte and the high-order entropy of ref. 16, species entropy and unique-tape fraction, per-interaction statistics (bytes written into the partner, copy events, destroyed programs), 16 random tapes for the functional census, and full soup snapshots at fixed steps and at emergence. Stages A and B sample every 500 steps from step 500, with the three most common tapes and without the byte histogram, the per-interaction statistics or the random tapes.

**Culture test and partner tests.** A tape runs as the first program against 64 uniformly random partners for the condition's budget; copy success is the fraction of partners that become ≥ 75% copies at the best cyclic shift. The score of a tape x is the mean, over partners j whose best-shift match to x before the encounter, s_j, is below 0.75, of (s′_j − s_j)/(1 − s_j), s′_j being the match after it. gen2 is the same quantity one generation on: the offspring of encounter j runs as organism against a fresh random partner, whose match to x is measured before and after, so offspring that are not copies contribute about zero and gen2 approximates the share of encounters whose offspring copies in turn. Heritable means gen2 ≥ 0.3, faithful additionally ≥ 50% of partners copied. gen2 is a normalised gain in best-shift similarity to the original tape, not a count of copies, so a word that smears its return addresses into its partners raises their similarity to itself without copying: 246 two-byte census words pass gen2 ≥ 0.3 without producing a single 75% copy (Supplementary Fig. 1). Replicators are therefore also scored by the copies they make (faithfulness and the partner tests), and dating the first replicator by the faithful criterion (`t_faith`, below) instead of by heredity alone gives the same tape at the same step in all 80 Stage G worlds. Partner-independence tests use 256 random partners and record self-damage (≥ 25% of the organism's bytes changed in one encounter). In BFF the culture test also records whether the instruction pointer entered the partner in each encounter; closed means ≤ 5% of encounters entered and ≥ 95% of partners copied, open means ≥ 50% entered. Information inflow from the environment, I(o; e | x) = H(o | x) for a deterministic executor, is the plug-in entropy in bits of the offspring (the partner's bytes after the encounter) over 256 random partners, ceiling 8 bits.

**Events and statistics.** `t_rep` is the first sampled step at which one of the three most common tapes has a share ≥ 0.5% and is heritable by the culture test (a clonality criterion by design: it dates the first *clone*, not the first heritable material); `t_faith` additionally requires faithfulness; the *final dominant* is the most heritable of the three most common tapes at the horizon. It is the most common tape in 57 of the 60 Stage G worlds at L = 16, 20 and 64; at L = 50 it is the second most common in all 20, behind the plain pusher tiling (5.5–6.6% of cells), while the tapes that carry its jump word `20 f0` hold 70–76% of cells. Its offspring is then the parent with one more `20 f0` inserted, the same string in every encounter; that offspring enters the partner in every encounter, the next generation (six jumps) is confined but fails the culture test (gen2 0.19), and the one after (ten jumps) no longer copies (`l50_lineage.py`), so at L = 50 the final dominant is a stage of a lineage rather than a lineage. Because the most common tape can misrepresent a diverse population, population claims rest on random cells (Population sampling, below); `tq_10` (quasispecies occupancy ≥ 10%) is the pre-registered occupancy event and fires on zero floods as well as on replicators. Emergence fractions are quoted as worlds reaching the event within the horizon with Wilson 95% intervals; times as Kaplan–Meier medians with censoring at the horizon. Ablation effects are ratios of Kaplan–Meier medians with seed-paired sign tests (the same seeds run under every ablation); confirmatory contrasts use one-sided Fisher exact tests and the Cochran–Mantel–Haenszel test pooled over budgets; between-variant transition rates in BFF use two-sided Fisher tests and Mann–Whitney tests on event times with non-transitioning runs censored at the horizon. Across the 233 unablated worlds of the exploratory stages, 92 gained a control-flow instruction between the first replicator and the final dominant and 3 lost one. In BFF, where replicator populations are quasispecies with small exact-class shares, the first replicator is the most common tape class (a tape and its reverse count as one class) at the first sample at which it is heritable, and the population event is a heritable fraction ≥ 0.5 of 32 random tapes. All thresholds (0.75 copy, gen2 ≥ 0.3, 0.5% share, 10% occupancy, 95%/5% closure) were fixed before the confirmatory stages. Re-dating the first replicator from the recorded runs under heredity thresholds from 0.2 to 0.4 leaves the load–push word first in 170 of 170 benign-tar worlds of 16–64 bytes; at 0.5 and 0.6, near the pusher's own gen2, it is first in 155 of 169 and 107 of 164. Principal medians are given with 95% bootstrap intervals across worlds (Supplementary Table 2). **Definitions and theorems.** A *self-writer* is a tape that, executed against the all-zero partner, leaves the partner holding a copy of itself. *Closure* (confinement): the organism's pointer never fetches a partner address as instruction in any encounter with a partner of the sampled class (uniformly random partners). Theorem 1: copying a 64-byte BFF tape into a partner that differs from it in every cell takes at least 128 instructions, 64 that change a cell and 64 head moves, so a program whose pointer stays in its own 64 cells executes some address twice in that encounter, that is, runs a loop. Theorem 2: in any machine whose pointer advances by default, a closed full replicator executes some address twice, provided a copy costs more instruction executions than the organism has addresses (BFF) or execution cannot stop inside the organism before the budget is spent (the Z80 soups with benign tar, budget 128 > L; not Stage I, where a zero byte halts, nor the Stage E arms with budgets 32 and 64 at L = 100). Theorem 6: a self-consistent literal-write instruction gives a straight-line, hence open, replicator the size of the literal; without one, under the copy-cost hypothesis (H2), which we do not prove for BFF, and in a machine whose execution ends when the pointer leaves the pair, every self-writer executes some address twice. Lemma 7: I(o; e | x) > 0 implies imperfect fidelity across contexts; perfect fidelity implies I = 0. Proposition 8: in the block-copy core `XX 5e ed b0` with copy offset d = XX mod 2L dividing L, every encounter leaves organism and partner holding the d-periodic tiling of the organism's first d bytes, so exactly the d − 4 bytes between the core and the offset are transmitted. Proposition 3 (computer-assisted, `census_period.py`): every word of period two, three and four, tiled to 16 bytes and executed against the zero partner. Period two: exactly six self-writers, the trivial `00 00` and the five load–push words, all straight-line and open. Period three: one, `b0 5e ed` tiled, closed, a block copier whose copy offset equals the tape length. Period four: 641 of primitive period four, 558 of them closed, all block copies (455 `LDIR`, 103 `LDDR`), and 83 open. A self-writer is closed if its pointer fetches no partner byte against the zero partner and 16 random partners. Proposition 4 states what the count of the initial soup does and does not show. Full statements and proofs are in the Supplementary Information.

**The copy-offset switch.** In the block-copy core `XX 5e ed b0` with HL = D = B = C = 0, register E is loaded from the organism's first byte, so `LDIR` copies each byte d = XX mod 2L addresses ahead; for `1e NN ed b0`, d = NN mod 2L. The working values of XX were found by executing the core with every byte value (`offset_census.py`). The copy-period law pools every confined heritable cell of the population scans whose first four bytes are either core and compares d with the number of transmissible sites of its single-mutant scan (`offset_population.py`). The matched pair (REVISION_PREREG E): at L = 16, R = `44 5e ed b0` × 4 and T = `b0 5e ed b0` followed by 12 random bytes drawn for every cell; at L = 32, R = `04 5e ed b0` × 8 and T = `a0 5e ed b0` + 28 random bytes. Conditions L = 16 with benign tar, L = 16 with lethal tar and L = 32 with benign tar; starts 50:50 (ten worlds without mutation for 100,000 steps and ten with the standard mutation for 300,000 steps) and 1% each way with mutation (five each); every cell classified by its first four bytes every 50 steps to 5,000 and every 500 after (`offset_switch.py`, Modal). The registered predictions of a mutation-bias equilibrium were not met (E1, E2); without mutation the share drifts. Encounter components of the pristine pair were measured with 4,096 encounters each against cells of the final soups (`offset_encounter.py`). Mutational flows (`offset_flow.py`, registered as E-mech): every single-byte mutant of R and T, four random partners each, classified by whether its offspring copies itself exactly and with what period; the registered prediction (EM1) failed and the flows of isolated mutants are the same under benign and lethal tar, so the environment dependence is reported without a mechanism.

**Encounter censuses (exploratory).** Lattice-neighbour pairs (200,000 per soup set) from the final soups of the ten 50:50 L = 32 worlds of each tar, executed under each rule (composition crossed with rule), with every cell classed by its first four bytes before and after (`offset_census_enc.py`); and core-free executors from the same soups against three partners, R, T with a random body and T with a tiled body (`a0 5e ed b0` × 8), traced to see whether the executor's pointer entered the partner (`offset_intruder.py`, 16,384 encounters per combination). The registered hypotheses that a regenerator survives through backup cores and that inherited zeros carry the lethal rule's protection of transmitters were both rejected (N2, N4): a regenerator whose first core is damaged never copies itself (0 of 4,096), and replacing a transmitter's zeros by random non-halting bytes keeps 88–94% of the protection (`n4_scars.py`). **Genealogies (exploratory).** The soup is not bitwise reproducible on graphics processors: the pair draw resolves collisions by atomic claims whose order varies, so two runs from one seed diverge, and a world cannot be replayed to inspect an event. We therefore recorded 20 new benign-tar worlds at L = 16 (seeds 31001–31020, Stage G dynamics) encounter by encounter from step 0 (`lod.py`): the pairs drawn, each active pair's memories before and after execution, and the soup after mutation, until the confined share of copy events had exceeded 0.5 for 2,000 steps, then 5,000 steps more (cap 80,000). Each changed cell receives a birth record: a *copy* (within Hamming distance L/4 of the executor at some cyclic shift, closer to it than to its own former tape, and with at least L/4 bytes newly matching it), *damage* (within L/4 of its former tape), *recombination* (neither; its functional parent is the tape that supplied the bytes it executes) or *mutation*; records from which no living cell descends are freed. Before any registered world, replaying every active pair of the first 200 steps of a validation world on the traced executor reproduced the recorded memories byte for byte (795,934 encounters), cells changed outside active pairs never exceeded the mutation count, and in a world seeded with the return closer at 1% among pushers all 64 sampled confined copiers traced to a seeded closer; the replay check held in all 20 registered worlds. On the lines of 64 sampled cells per closed world, every tape was classed as open copier, confined copier or non-copier against 64 random partners; the founder is the first confined copier after the newest open copier older than the final stretch in which at least 90% of the line is confined, and a world's first founder is its earliest (`lod_chain.py`, `lod_founders2.py`). The registered descent prediction was met but uninformative, since by then nearly every living cell had an open copier among its ancestors of the previous 2,000 steps. The copy-gain rule, the founder, the functional parent and the separation of first founders from later conversions by existing closers (15 later founders in 10 worlds, 11 of them with bytes from a confined copier) were defined after inspecting data, as dated in the change log; a replication on 20 new worlds with the pipeline frozen is pre-registered (N6). Word shares are measured in the last soup snapshot (every 2,000 steps) before each founder against a random-tape baseline of about 2.4 × 10⁻⁴ per two-byte word (`lod_parts_first.py`). Hypercubes: for a pusher and a return closer differing at k = 12 or 16 positions, all 2^k intermediates were tested by the culture test (second-generation score ≥ 0.3) and searched for single-byte paths through heritable genotypes (`landscape.py`); a six-step path exists in one of the four cubes.

**Ref. 22's robustness criterion.** Every Stage K first replicator and final dominant (L = 32): 1,000 trials each of 1, 4 and 8 successive random single-byte mutations; a trial counts if the mutant, run for 512 instructions against the all-zero partner, leaves the partner equal to the mutant. **Traced execution.** A derived copy of the culture-test executor records every address fetched as instruction stream (opcodes, prefixes, operands) in a 256-bit map per encounter, with byte-identical execution (checked). Pointer confinement is the fraction of 256 encounters with a seeded random partner in which no partner address is fetched; the executed set is the union of fetched organism positions; a transmissible position of the mutational scan is called unexecuted if it is never fetched. Every first replicator and final dominant of Stages G, K and I (110 of each) was traced. Confinement concerns the instruction pointer only; a confined copier may read its partner's bytes as data.

**Mutational scan.** For each Stage G first replicator and final dominant, every single-byte mutant (all 255 alternative values per position at L = 16 and 20; 32 seeded values per position at L = 50 and 64) was run as organism A against 32 shared random partners for 128 instructions and its offspring against 32 fresh partners, with the culture test's thresholds. A mutant is heritable if gen2 ≥ 0.3; its mutation is transmitted if the mutant byte is present at the aligned position in at least half of the offspring that are ≥ 75% copies; a position is a transmissible site if at least half of its tested values are both heritable and transmitted; the single-mutant variation score is Σ_i log₂(1 + 255 f_i) over positions, f_i being the fraction of transmissible values at position i, a summary of how many single mutants are heritable and reach first-generation copies, not a channel capacity (it ignores epistasis and later generations). Transmission is scored in first-generation copies only; serial retention follows alleles further. Three predictions were written before the run (the successor more robust to mutation than the first replicator; the closed form with more transmissible sites; the matched pusher-with-jump of L = 50 with more sites than its pusher); all three failed and are reported as such.

**Pair invasion.** Soups of 20,000 cells at L = 50 under the Stage G dynamics, every cell set to one tape of the matched pair (the pusher tiling, or the same tiling with `20 f0` inserted three times, the final dominant of 14 Stage G worlds) and 1% of cells to the other at step 0; five seeds per direction, three seeds per resident with no invader; 20,000 steps. The pre-registered outcome, the invader's nearest-class share passing 50%, could not be met at this length because the quasispecies cloud splits between the two classes (a closed tape that has lost two jumps is nearer the pusher); the share of cells carrying the jump word, reported instead, was defined after the data were seen. In single-tape invasion assays the pusher tiling `01 c5` × 8 seeded into 1% of the cells of a random soup at L = 16 (128 instructions, mutation 1/16, five soups) peaked at a median occupancy of 0.32 at step 250. The jump-word marker was checked against traced confinement before use (registered): in three re-runs of the closed-into-ancestor invasion, 512 random cells at each of eight steps were traced against 16 random partners. The marker share reached 0.73–0.75 while the share of cells confined in all 16 encounters stayed at 0.19–0.25, and only 26–33% of marker-carrying cells were confined; the registered agreement failed, so the L = 50 invasion is reported as the spread of the jump, not of closure (Extended Data Fig. 5a).

**Serial allele retention.** Fourteen genomes (Fig. 4a) were scanned with every single-byte mutant (all 255 values per position at L ≤ 32, 32 seeded values at L ≥ 50): four open pushers, the most common closed final dominant at each of L = 16, 20 and 32, the L = 50 final dominant, the lethal-tar final dominant with the most first-generation sites, the most common tape of three Stage L worlds at the first snapshot at which a genome-bearing block copier was most common, and the 20-byte 8080 closer with two random 12-byte payloads. Each mutant founded 16 lineages and the unmutated tape 256. In each of eight transfers the lineage tape ran as organism against a fresh uniformly random partner under the genome's own rule (8080 subset, lethal tar) and the partner's bytes after the encounter became the next lineage tape; lineage r met the same partners for every mutant. A transfer is a copy if the offspring matches its parent at ≥ 75% of positions at the best cyclic shift, and a lineage is alive while every transfer has been a copy. The allele is carried if the mutant's five-byte window centred on the mutation occurs anywhere in the lineage tape, cyclically, which needs no alignment, so inserted jumps and shifts do not hide it; mutants whose window occurs in the unmutated tape would be excluded (none did). Every lineage is counted as lost (not alive), erased (alive, allele absent) or carrying the allele. A position is serially transmissible after g transfers if, for at least half of its values, the share of alive lineages carrying the allele exceeds by at least 0.5 the share of alive unmutated lineages that contain the same window by chance. Design and predictions were registered before the run: the pusher's sites were predicted to persist (failed: its lineages are lost); the evolved closers to erase, the transient genomes to keep at least five sites and the constructed closer its payload (met). A post hoc variant drew partners from cells of the organism's own world (Stage G, seed 2001) instead of random bytes.

**Population sampling.** At the final snapshot of every Stage G, I, K, L and M world, 256 random cells were culture-tested (32 partners) and traced against 16 random partners; a cell is confined if no partner address is fetched as instruction in any of the 16 (registered). At every snapshot of the same worlds, 64 random cells were culture-tested and traced, and up to 16 random heritable cells were given the single-mutant scan (16 values per position, 16 partners) and their executed set (defined before the run and exploratory, since the first sample motivated it). A heritable cell is open if not confined; a confined one is a regenerator (at most two transmissible sites), intermediate (three or four) or a transmitter (five or more); the classes of unscanned confined cells are inferred from the scanned ones. A world is closed at the population level if most of its heritable cells are confined. For confined cells the first-generation scan agrees with serial retention (Fig. 4a,c); for open cells it overstates it, so open cells are reported as open.

**Address mapping and aligned lengths.** Pair memory is a ring of 2L bytes: every memory access uses the 16-bit address modulo 2L, and the stack pointer starts at the largest 16-bit value that aliases the last byte of the partner. These conventions, addresses taken modulo the pair length, the stack pointer at the end of the pair and every register empty at the start of an encounter, follow ref. 16, whose tapes were 16 bytes long, an aligned length; the evolved closers rely on the empty registers (Harness conventions). The program counter and the stack pointer themselves are 16-bit, so a relative jump or a stack move that crosses address 0 reaches (65,536 + target) mod 2L, which equals the true-ring target only when 2L divides 65,536. Of the 67 closed final dominants of Stage G, 25 carry a relative jump (JR or DJNZ) at a length where the 16-bit program counter does not fold consistently onto the ring: 20 at L = 50 and 5 at L = 20, one of which (seed 2003) is classed by its block repeat in Extended Data Table 1 (we did not check that every one of these jumps crosses address 0); the pair-invasion and mutational-scan results at L = 50 are results about the machine so defined. Stage K (L = 32, 20 worlds, one million steps, seeds 5001–5020; pre-registered: first replicator a load–push word in ≥ 18 of 20, a closed successor in ≥ 15 of 20, kill below 10) tested closure at an aligned intermediate length: 20 of 20 and 12 of 20, all closers block copies.

**The 8080 subset.** The `i8080` suppression makes every Z80-only instruction a no-op: the 256 CB-page opcodes, the 78 defined ED-page opcodes (block copies and their repeats, 16-bit ADC and SBC, IN and OUT (C), NEG, RETN and RETI, IM, LD A,I and A,R, RLD and RRD) and EX AF,AF', DJNZ, the five JR forms and EXX. This is an 8080-like restriction of the same emulator, not an 8080. Three deviations from a real 8080 are kept: the IX and IY prefixes `DD` and `FD` still select the indexed form of an 8080 instruction, where the 8080 executes them as CALL; the 8080's other alternate opcodes are no-ops here (`CB`, JMP on the 8080, and `ED`, CALL, become two-byte no-ops with the byte that follows, and `D9`, RET, a one-byte no-op); and the core keeps Z80 flag behaviour, so P/V holds overflow after arithmetic where the 8080 sets parity, and conditional jumps, calls and returns on PE or PO can differ. Stage M: Stage G conditions at L = 16 (300,000 steps) and L = 32 (one million), 20 worlds each, seeds 7001–7020, with the predictions registered before the run. The registered reading that the subset expresses no closer on a 32-byte ring was wrong and is withdrawn: a reviewer's 20-byte closer of 8080 instructions (`21 20 00 31 40 00 16 10 2b 46 2b 4e c5 15 c2 08 00 c3 11 00`, a counted push loop closed by a conditional jump, then a jump to itself) copies itself and any 12 following bytes exactly into 256 of 256 random partners without entering them, in our executor under the subset (`check_8080_closer.py`).

**BFF.** cubff default semantics verified from source: two 64-byte tapes concatenated, pointer and heads at byte 0, ten instructions, every other byte a no-op, unmatched brackets halt, 2¹³ steps per encounter, 2¹⁷ programs, random ordered pairs each epoch, mutation 2⁻¹² per byte per epoch. Switches: `ip_wrap` (pointer wraps modulo 128); `literal` (byte 0x50 = `P a b`: write a at head1−1 and b at head1−2, then head1 −= 2, skip the operands; designed so that its one-byte tiling writes itself, the analogue of the Z80's load–push word); `nohalt` (unmatched brackets are no-ops); `halt_p` (an unmatched bracket halts with probability p, decided by a hash of the pair, the step and the seed); `lit_rep` (the literal instruction writes r copies of its word per execution). Our GPU kernel reproduces cubff's validation replicator and matches a CPU reference on randomised tests. 24 seeds each for the published and wrapping variants, 12 for each literal variant, 12 for the published rule with no-op brackets (run after review; pre-registered: every first replicator loop-bearing, met 7 of 7; a transition in at least 9 of 12, not met at 7; pointer-closed first replicators in every world, not met in two, one intermediate (pointer enters in 23% of encounters) and one open), 6 for each of p = 0.01, 0.03, 0.1 and 0.3 under wrap + literal push, and 6 for each of r = 2 and 3 under the literal push without a wrapping pointer; events every 64 epochs; high-order entropy with brotli quality 2 as in ref. 16. Dial predictions, registered before the run: D1-1, the open population persists (share ≥ 10% at 16,384 epochs) in at least 4 of 6 soups at p = 0.01 (met, 6 of 6) and in at most 1 of 6 at p = 0.3 (not met: the share falls below 1% at 832–896 epochs in 6 of 6 and recovers to 0.10–0.11 in 5 of 6, with 0.31–0.44 of random tapes heritable); D1-2, the epoch of collapse falls monotonically with p within a factor of three of 1/p (not met: no collapse at 0 < p ≤ 0.1, 896 at p = 0.3, 192 at p = 1); D1-3, no closure by control flow at any p (met); D2-1, inflow at most 1 bit at r ≥ 2 (met: 0.00 bits in 12 of 12 soups at r = 2 and 3, against 8.00 in 12 of 12 at r = 1); D2-2, the first replicator still the loop-free, pointer-open tiling (met, 12 of 12); D2-3, collapse slower or absent at r ≥ 2 (met: absent, with an all-`P` share at epoch 1,024 of 22–31% at r = 2 and of 3.6–7.3% in 5 of 6 soups and 0 in one at r = 3, against 0–0.24% at r = 1, and every random tape heritable at 16,384 epochs).

Closed-replicator search: all 1,309,528 periodic tilings of {`P`, `[`, `]`, no-op} up to period 10 under the literal, wrapping, no-halt rules, three partners each.

**Pre-registration and stages.** Supplementary Table 3 lists every run of the numbered stages. ## Supplementary Information: theorems and proofs

*What kind of statement each item is: a **Theorem** has a pen-and-paper proof and uses no simulation; a **Proposition** is an exact statement decided by finite computation, with the computation described; a **Model** is a result about a stylised model with stated assumptions, not about the simulator. Empirical premises are marked as such, and every number quoted is from the generated tables behind the main text.*

**Notation.** An encounter executes a program held in a shared memory. The organism A occupies addresses 0 to L − 1 and the partner B the next L addresses; in the Z80 soups the 2L addresses form a ring, in BFF the 128 addresses do not wrap. Execution starts at address 0 with a fixed initial machine state (registers zero; the Z80 stack pointer at the last byte of B) and lasts at most T instruction executions (T = 128 in the Z80 soups, 2¹³ in BFF). A *control transfer* is a jump, call, return, repeat-in-place or halt; a *straight-line program* executes none, so that under default flow its pointer advances past each executed instruction. The *no-op context* is the partner all of whose bytes are no-ops (zero in the Z80, where byte 0 is NOP; zero in BFF, where every byte outside the ten instruction characters is a no-op). A tape x is a *self-writer* if its encounter with the no-op context ends with B holding a copy of x (up to a cyclic shift, as in the culture test), and a *full self-replicator in context e* if the encounter (x, e) ends with B holding a copy of x. It is *closed* if its pointer executes no address of B in any context and *open* if it does in some context. A value is *prescribed* if it is determined by x alone, the same for every partner.

**Theorem 1 (the cost of a copy in BFF).** *Setting.* BFF as published: a 128-byte pair; the instruction pointer p and the two data heads h0 and h1 start at address 0; p advances by one after every instruction unless a bracket jumps; `<` `>` and `{` `}` move h0 or h1 by one cell; `+` `−` change the byte under h0 by one; `.` copies the byte under h0 into the cell under h1 and `,` the byte under h1 into the cell under h0; every other byte is a no-op; execution ends when p leaves the pair, on an unmatched bracket, or after 2¹³ steps. *Statement.* Let A be the organism's 64-byte tape, at addresses 0–63, fix a cyclic shift s, and let B be a partner whose byte differs in every cell from the copy it is to receive (A rotated by s). Any execution of the pair that leaves B holding that copy runs at least 128 instructions: at least 64 that change a cell and at least 64 head moves. If p stays among A's 64 addresses throughout, some address is therefore executed at least twice. *Proof.* Every cell of B must change, and an instruction changes at most one cell (`+`, `−` and `,` the cell under h0, `.` the cell under h1), so at least 64 changing instructions run, and each cell of B is under a head when it changes. Both heads start at address 0, which lies in A, and each move changes one head's position by one, so after v moves the heads have occupied at most v positions besides 0; occupying all 64 cells of B needs v ≥ 64. Moves change no cell, so the two sets of instructions are disjoint, and at least 128 instructions run. An execution of at least 128 instructions at 64 addresses executes some address twice. ∎ *Corollary 1.1.* A BFF program that copies its tape, at some shift, into a partner differing in every cell from that copy while its pointer stays in its own cells executes a loop in that encounter. *Scope.* The count is per encounter: it needs neither the same instruction sequence in every encounter nor a no-op partner, and it says nothing about a program whose pointer enters the partner, which then fetches the partner's bytes. An earlier version stated two further parts, a bound uniform over partners for programs whose pointer stays out of B and the claim that no straight-line execution writes a copy of a tape with more than 32 non-zero bytes into a zero partner. Both proofs were wrong, the first because brackets test the byte under h0 and `,` copies B's bytes into A, so that a confined program's control flow can depend on B, the second because bytes written into B ahead of the pointer are executed when the pointer arrives; both parts are withdrawn. That every observed BFF first replicator carries a loop (35 of 35 under the published rule, the wrapping pointer and harmless brackets) is an observation explained, not forced, by the count; whether the loop also keeps the pointer out of B is a separate, measured fact (22 of the 28 first replicators under the published rule and the wrapping pointer never entered the partner in 256 encounters).

**Theorem 2 (closure requires a cycle).** *Hypothesis (H1), default flow.* In the absence of an executed control transfer the pointer advances past each instruction, so that a straight-line program runs from the organism's region into the partner's. *Statement.* Under (H1), a closed full self-replicator of L bytes executes some address at least twice, provided either (i) a copy of it costs more than L instruction executions, or (ii) execution cannot end inside the organism's region before a budget T > L is spent. *Proof.* A closed execution visits only the L addresses of A. If no address repeats, it comprises at most L instruction executions. Under (i) these cannot complete the copy; under (ii) the budget forces a further execution, which can only be at an address already executed. ∎ *The machines.* BFF satisfies (i) in every encounter with a partner that differs in every cell from the copy it is to receive: by Theorem 1 the copy costs at least 128 executions, more than the organism's 64 addresses. The Z80 soups with benign tar satisfy (ii): the budget is 128 executions for tapes of at most 100 bytes, there is no exit instruction, and HALT holds the processor at its own address, which we count as a one-state cycle. *Where the provisos fail.* Under lethal tar (Stage I) a zero byte fetched as an opcode ends execution, possibly inside the organism, and in the Stage E arms with budgets of 32 and 64 instructions at L = 100 the budget ends before a pass over the tape; there (ii) does not hold and the theorem does not apply. *The bandwidth reading.* Condition (i) holds in any machine whose straight-line code writes fewer bytes than the addresses it consumes, since a pass over L addresses then writes fewer than L bytes. This is the form used in the main text, and it describes the replicators observed: the first replicator writes two bytes per four bytes of code (the executor trace shows eight bytes written when the pointer leaves its sixteen cells), BFF's copy instructions move one byte per instruction and need a head move per cell, and the literal push of modified BFF writes two bytes per three. It is not a machine-wide property of the Z80, in which a chain of pushes writes two bytes per byte of code; for the Z80 the theorem rests on (ii). *Corollary 2.1.* Under (H1) every straight-line program is open: with T > L its pointer reaches the partner's region. A closed self-writer therefore contains an executed control transfer, which may be a repeating instruction such as `LDIR`, besides the instructions that write its content. For the Z80 the census of Proposition 3 makes this exact at small periods: the self-writers of period two are five straight-line load–push words, all open, and the smallest closed self-writer has period three (`b0 5e ed` tiled, whose tiling contains the block copy `b0 5e ed b0`). The corollary does not make closed self-writers rare: at period four they outnumber the open ones.

**Proposition 3 (the short self-writers of the Z80; computer-assisted).** Tile a word of period k to 16 bytes and execute it for 128 instructions as A against the all-zero partner; it is a self-writer if B ends holding the tiling at some cyclic shift. By exhaustive execution of every word: at period two, of 65,536 words exactly six leave B holding a tiling of the word, the trivial `00 00`, which B already holds, and the five load–push words `01 c5`, `11 d5`, `21 e5` (`LD rr,nn ; PUSH rr`) and `2a e5`, `e5 2a` (`LD HL,(nn) ; PUSH HL`); none contains a control transfer, so each runs on into the partner, and every two-byte self-writer of this machine is open. At period three, of 16,777,216 words exactly one is a self-writer, `b0 5e ed`, which is closed: tiled, it contains the block copy `b0 5e ed b0` (`OR B ; LD E,(HL) ; LDIR`), whose copy offset is the tape length. At period four, of 4,294,967,296 words, 641 are self-writers of primitive period four: 558 closed, every one of them a block copy (455 with `LDIR`, 103 with `LDDR`), and 83 open. Closure is tested by tracing the self-writer against the zero partner and 16 random partners (closed if the pointer fetches no partner byte in any). The stochastic census of period two agrees: the same five words copy into 0.66–0.81 of 256 random partners, none into 0.95 or more, and the 246 words that pass the heredity threshold by smearing return addresses produce no 75% copy. *What it shows.* The smallest self-writers are open, and a closed self-writer needs only one period more; but closed self-writers are the majority at periods three and four, the largest the census covers, because a single repeating block move closes a copy. Counting self-writers does not, therefore, explain why the open word comes first.

**Proposition 4 (a count on the initial soup).** In a soup of N tapes of L uniformly random bytes, the expected number of occurrences of a given k-byte word, at any of the L − k + 1 positions of a tape, is N (L − k + 1) / 256^k^: for N = 20,000 and L = 16, 4.6 for a given two-byte word, 0.017 for a given three-byte word and 6.1 × 10^−5^ for a given four-byte word. *What the count does and does not show.* The load–push word works only in phase, as the four bytes `01 c5 01 c5` (`LD BC,nn` reading `c5 01`, then `PUSH BC`), the same length as the shortest evolved closers (`1e a4 ed b0`, `44 5e ed b0`), so a given open and a given closed working unit have the same expected count at step 0, 6.1 × 10^−5^. Proposition 3 counts more closed than open four-byte self-writers. The count therefore gives the open form no head start; the head start is measured (Kaplan–Meier medians of 450 steps to the first replicator and 21,000 to the first closed replicator in Stage G at L = 16; 38,000 under lethal tar, where the first replicator is closed). An earlier version compared a two-byte word with a four-byte closer and concluded that the open form is exponentially likelier; that comparison was wrong and is withdrawn. Why the open word is found first, perhaps because partial load–push words write useful bytes from any position along an executed path while a closer works only from the start of execution, is not modelled.

**Model 5 (the closure window).** *Assumptions.* An open replicator population of n(t) tapes; each tape-step produces a closed functional variant with probability q (a mutation placing the right bytes at the right phase: q is of order sites × μ × 256^−m^ for an m-byte closing motif); a closed variant, once present, grows faster than the open form and fixes with probability of order one. *Statement.* The expected number of closed variants produced while the open population lasts is q ∫ n dt, and closure occurs with probability ≈ 1 − exp(−q ∫ n dt); the integral is the window. If the open form's own copies are viable in the soup it creates, n stays near the carrying capacity, the window grows without bound and closure is a matter of waiting; if the soup becomes lethal to the open form before q ∫ n dt reaches order one, the open form dies without closing. *What the experiments found.* Z80, benign zeros: closure of the population in 20 of 20 worlds by 300,000 steps at L = 16, in 19 of 20 by one million at L = 20 and in 8 of 20 at L = 64, where the open form is least damaged and the motif must land in the longest tape (smallest q); at L = 50 a closed tape arises in 20 of 20 worlds but its offspring reopens, so the model's assumption that a closed variant breeds true fails there. BFF with the literal push and lethal brackets: under a wrapping pointer the open form held 0.58–0.63 of N = 2^17^ tapes at epoch 64 and 2–3 × 10^−4^ N at epoch 256, an integrated window ∫ n dt of 8.2–8.7 × 10^6^ tape-epochs over 16,384 epochs, 5.0–5.5 × 10^6^ of them before epoch 256; without a wrapping pointer the window was 2.5–4.1 × 10^5^; 0 of 12 closures in each. BFF with benign brackets: the window is unbounded, yet no closure by control flow in 12 of 12, consistent with q = 0, since no closed heritable tiling exists in that language up to period 10 (Methods); the open form persists for 16,384 epochs. Z80 with lethal zeros (Stage I): the open form never establishes, so the window is empty from the start, and the first replicator is closed and late (Kaplan–Meier median 38,000 steps against 450; 10 of 10 worlds). The registered lethality dial (zeros halt with probability p) tested the model's monotone prediction and refuted it: the first closed replicator comes at Kaplan–Meier medians of 18,000, 42,500, 60,000, 75,000, 57,500 and 52,500 steps at p = 0, 0.01, 0.03, 0.1, 0.3 and 1, so a damaged open phase delays closure beyond either extreme, and the window reading holds only for the contrast of the extremes. At the aligned length L = 32, 12 of 20 worlds closed by one million steps, and given ten million steps 20 of 20 closed at L = 16 and 20. In a world filled with the open form from step 0 at L = 50, n at carrying capacity throughout, the jump arose by mutation and spread to half the cells within 5,000–13,250 steps in 3 of 3, though its carriers are mostly not confined. The model's qualitative prediction, that an open phase feeds closure, is met at L = 16: the first closed replicator appears at a Kaplan–Meier median of 22,500 steps under benign tar against 38,000 under lethal tar, where there is no open phase (log-rank P = 0.007), although descent of the closers from the open form is not shown. When an unmatched bracket halts with probability p, the open form held 98% of the soup at p ≤ 0.1 and collapsed at p = 0.3 and 1; the registered expectation that the collapse time would scale as 1/p was not met, the lethality acting as a threshold between 0.1 and 0.3. When the literal writes two or three copies of its word, so that a copy completes in one pass, no partial copies are made, no lethal tar forms and the open form persists for 16,384 epochs in 12 of 12 soups, with zero inflow from birth. The model does not predict which regime a machine is in; that depends on how the open replicator's own by-products act on it, which is what the experiments measure.

**Theorem 6 (architecture decides the first replicator).** *Hypotheses.* (H1) Default flow, as in Theorem 2, with execution ending when the pointer leaves the pair (as in BFF) or continuing around a ring (as in the Z80 soups). (H2) *Copy cost:* in the no-op context, an execution that runs each address at most once cannot write a copy of the tape. (H2) is a hypothesis to be checked per machine. We do not prove it for BFF, where writes can land ahead of the pointer and be executed; a search of 16,447,860 straight-line BFF programs under a wrapping pointer found none that copies a tape of period two or more, only one-symbol fills (Methods). (H3) *Self-consistent literal write:* an instruction that writes its own operand bytes verbatim at a write pointer that starts in, or advances into, the partner's region, and whose operand can be its own encoding in the phase executed (`LD BC,nn ; PUSH BC` with nn = `c5 01`; `P a b` with a = b = `P`). *Statement.* (i) Under (H1) and either proviso of Theorem 2, every closed self-replicator contains a cycle. (ii) Under (H1) and (H3), the tiling of the literal instruction is a straight-line program, hence open, that writes its own content at the advancing write pointer; it is a self-writer wherever the encounter lets the written bytes cover the partner's region, which holds in the Z80 (eight pushes over two passes of the 2L-byte ring; Proposition 3); in BFF with `P` the tiling is observed to arise first (below), which the theorem does not decide. (iii) Under (H1) and (H2) without (H3), in a machine whose execution ends when the pointer leaves the pair, no straight-line program is a self-writer and every self-replicator contains a cycle: born loop-bearing (whether its pointer also stays out of the partner is a separate, measured property). (iv) Under uniform random initialisation over an alphabet of size σ, the expected number of occurrences of a fixed k-symbol pattern among N regions of length L is N (L − k + 1) σ^−k^, so a minimal open pattern shorter than every closed one is present with probability higher by a factor exponential in the length difference. This concerns the presence of the pattern, not of a replicating tiling, and nucleation is not modelled; in the Z80 the minimal working units, open and closed, are both four bytes long, so (iv) gives the open form no advantage there (Proposition 4). *Proof.* (i) is Theorem 2. (ii) The tiling executes no control transfer, so by (H1) its pointer runs into the partner's region: it is open; its writes are its operands, by (H3) the tiling itself; completion of the copy is the per-machine fact cited. (iii) In the no-op context a straight-line program runs each address at most once before the pointer leaves the pair, and by (H2) such an execution cannot complete a copy; so a self-writer executes some address twice. (iv) is linearity of expectation. ∎ *What it decides.* Whether a substrate's first replicator can be open is decided by (H3); whether it is open in practice is observed. The Z80 has a self-consistent literal write (open first, 80 of 80 worlds); BFF has none, since its instructions increment in place or copy from memory (28 of 28 first replicators loop-bearing, and none of the 28 enters the partner in more than 34% of encounters, 22 of them never); BFF with one literal-write instruction added is open first (12 of 12 soups under each pointer rule). The theorem says nothing about how long the open phase lasts (Model 5). *Scope.* (H1) is a property of a von Neumann architecture in which a program's code lies in a shared memory and execution runs on past it: it holds in the Z80 soups, BFF, Core War and Tierra, and not in Avida, whose genomes are circular, so that a straight-line Avida program returns to its own start. The theorem also inherits the provisos of Theorem 2, which fail under lethal tar and in budget arms shorter than the tape. (H2) is a property of the copy instructions and must be checked per machine; Theorem 1 does not imply it (one pass over 128 addresses meets Theorem 1's bound), and we do not prove it for BFF. In the ring Z80 the premise that execution ends when the pointer leaves the pair fails, so (iii) does not apply there: the two-byte self-writer completes its copy on a second pass of the ring. For the Z80 the open-first conclusion rests on observation; the census (Proposition 3) shows that the smallest self-writers are open, but not why they come first. For a chemistry the hypotheses are a reading, not a theorem: (H1) would be a copier that runs off its template into adjacent material by default, (H3) a template whose content specifies its own complement without an interpreter.

**Lemma 7 (closure as zero information inflow).** Let the offspring of x in context e be o = F(x, e), with e drawn from a context distribution, and define the inflow λ(x) = I(o; e | x), the information the offspring carries about the context given the parent. (i) If λ(x) > 0 then P(o = x | x) < 1: a replicator with inflow cannot be perfectly faithful across contexts. (ii) If P(o = x | x) = 1 then λ(x) = 0: perfect fidelity across contexts implies zero inflow. (iii) Fano's inequality bounds the fidelity: P(o ≠ x | x) ≥ (H(o | x) − 1) / log|O|, where O is the set of possible offspring. *Proof.* If o = x with probability one given x, then o is a function of x alone and I(o; e | x) = 0, which is (ii); (i) is its contrapositive; (iii) is Fano's inequality with H(o | x) ≥ I(o; e | x). ∎ *Measurement.* For a deterministic executor H(o | x, e) = 0, so λ(x) = H(o | x), the entropy of the offspring over partners; the partner test estimates it as the plug-in entropy over 256 uniformly random partners (ceiling 8 bits). *Reading, with the measured qualification.* The lemma is Krakauer et al.'s individuality, the organism's future determined by its own past rather than the environment's, specialised to one generation and made measurable by intervention. Pointer closure (execution never enters the partner) is neither necessary nor sufficient for λ = 0: it is not sufficient because offspring positions the organism never writes keep the partner's bytes (7 of the 22 BFF first replicators whose pointer never entered the partner have H(o | x) > 0, three of them ≈ 7 bits from a single unwritten byte), and it is not necessary because a copier that overwrites everything it touches has λ = 0 although its pointer enters the partner (the BFF literal that writes two or three copies of its word, whose offspring carry 0 bits of the partner while its pointer enters the partner in every encounter; Fig. 5d). Inflow comes from partial copying; openness of control flow is the mechanism that makes the Z80 first replicator's copies partial. Measured: the first replicator has λ > 0 in 80 of 80 worlds (medians 3.9, 6.2, 7.8 and 8.0 bits at L = 16, 20, 50 and 64, the last at the ceiling) and the loop-bearing successor λ = 0 in 63 of 67 (one offspring string in all 256 contexts), the four exceptions copying all but one to three bytes (Fig. 3c).

**Proposition 8 (the copy period).** *Setting.* The Z80 soup at tape length L ≤ 64: the pair memory is a ring of 2L bytes, addresses are taken mod 2L, every register except the stack pointer is zero at the start of an encounter, and an encounter executes 128 instructions, each repetition of `LDIR` counting as one. Let the organism's tape A begin with `XX 5e ed b0`, where XX is a one-byte instruction that writes no memory and leaves HL ≡ 0 and 256·D ≡ 0 (mod 2L) and BC either 0 or at least 126, as every working value does (Methods), and let d = XX mod 2L satisfy 4 ≤ d ≤ L and d | L (hence d | 2L). *Statement.* Whatever the partner holds, the encounter leaves both the organism's and the partner's L bytes equal to the d-periodic tiling of A's first d bytes. Hence a mutation at a position i with 4 ≤ i < d is carried by the offspring and by all its descendants, whatever the mutant byte; a mutation at a position i ≥ d is erased from the offspring and from the parent; and outside the four-byte core the number of transmissible sites is exactly d − 4. *Proof.* The first instruction writes nothing; the second, `LD E,(HL)`, reads address 0 and sets E = XX, so DE ≡ XX ≡ d (mod 2L); `LDIR` then writes, at its k-th repetition (k = 0, 1, …), the current byte at address k into address d + k (mod 2L), and because BC is 0 (65,536 repetitions) or at least 126 it repeats for all of the 126 executions left. By induction on k, the byte read at address k equals A's original byte at k mod d: for k < d the address has not yet been written (the first write to it comes at repetition 2L − d + k > k), and for k ≥ d it was written at repetition k − d with the original byte at (k − d) mod d = k mod d. So every written address a receives the original byte at a mod d (for writes that wrap past address 2L − 1 this uses d | 2L), which for a < d is the byte already there. The writes cover addresses d, …, d + 125, which include every address from d to 2L − 1 because 2L − d ≤ 124; addresses below d are unchanged. Hence every address a holds A's original byte at a mod d, and because d | L the partner's position j, at address L + j, holds the byte at j mod d. Bytes 4 to d − 1 are never executed (execution stays at addresses 0–3), so their values do not affect the copy and are copied to every position congruent to them mod d; the offspring therefore begins with the same core and the same d − 4 bytes, and the argument repeats in every generation. Bytes at positions d and beyond are replaced by copies of earlier bytes in both tapes. ∎ *Remarks.* The variant `1e NN ed b0` (`LD E,n ; LDIR`) has offset d = NN mod 2L and the same count. Mutations inside the core either preserve its function for a few values (another working value of XX with the same d) or change or break the copy; the single-mutant scan counts a site only if most of its values are carried, so the core contributes none. The law is confirmed in the soups (Fig. 6c): of the 2,343 confined block-copy cells whose offset divides L, 2,289 have exactly d − 4 transmissible sites in the single-mutant scan, the median equals d − 4 in all 15 (L, d) groups, and the three cells whose offset does not divide L have 0, 3 and 0 sites. A transmitter is the case d = L: it copies itself verbatim and carries L − 4 sites; every d < L regenerates, rewriting the organism's own body from its first d bytes.
