# Heredity before individuality in a digital primordial soup

*Review copy, 2026-10-09 (revision 1). Nature Article format: summary ≤ 200 words, main text ≈ 3,600 words, six figures, Methods, Extended Data legends and a Supplementary Information with the theorems and proofs. Every number is from a generated table of the project repository; every experiment reported is complete.*

---

Theories of the origin of life disagree about what came first but agree on the transition: a spreading pattern became an individual whose future depends on itself, not on its surroundings^1–4^. We watched, repeated and dissected it in thousands of worlds of random machine code. On tapes of 16 to 64 bytes with harmless by-products, in the Z80 and an 8080-like subset, the first replicator is a two-byte word that writes itself when executed, runs on into its neighbour and makes damaged copies that vary with the neighbour; its lineages soon stop copying. Closure, a return of control flow into the organism, follows where a closing design is within reach: in every world at 16 bytes, in some at 32 and 64, never at 50, where the closed form is a passing stage. Closed copies no longer depend on the partner; closers differ in heredity. Closers that copy the code they run regenerate it, erasing mutations; closers that also copy bytes they never run keep them through serial transfers. The first kind ends most worlds and won the one direct contest; the second dominates when by-products are lethal. In a second machine one instruction decides whether life begins open.

## Main

What distinguishes a living thing from the chemistry around it has two answers: a list of properties, metabolism, compartment and heredity, with the question of which came first^5–9^, or a relation: a living system is one whose operations produce the organisation that produces them, so that its future depends on its own past rather than on its environment, in Maturana and Varela's operational closure^1^, Rosen's closure to efficient causation^2^, Fontana and Buss's organisational closure^3,10^ and, made quantitative, the information-theoretic individuality of Krakauer and colleagues^4,11^. This is also the moment at which population genetics places the start of Darwinian evolution proper, when vertical descent comes to dominate communal exchange^12,13^. No experimental system has allowed this transition to be observed as it happens, repeated under controlled variation, and taken apart.

Digital organisms usually descend from a designed ancestor^14,15^; random-program soups come closer. When random byte strings for a simple machine execute one another, with no fitness function and no replacement, self-replicating programs arise and take over^16^, as they did in the Coreworld^17,18^, among random Lisp expressions^19^ and in artificial chemistries^20,21^. For the Z80 processor the first replicators are known to be load–push programs that give way to a block-copy family^16,22^, a takeover attributed to mutational robustness^22^. We treated such a soup as a test of one explanation: a copier that writes fewer symbols than it reads cannot copy itself in one pass over its own cells, so if life begins with the smallest thing that writes itself, it must begin open, running on into its surroundings, and individuality must be a later invention that closes the path of execution. We ran more than 3,600 worlds of random Z80 code, varying the instruction set, program length, execution budget, mutation rate and memory geometry, with confirmatory stages pre-registered with kill criteria (Methods). Heredity is measured by intervention, as in serial-transfer tests of self-replication^23^: a program is lifted out and cultured against fresh random partners, so that a pattern that merely spreads is not mistaken for one that inherits. We then repeated the central experiment on the Z80 restricted to an 8080-like subset of its instructions, and on a second, minimal machine whose instruction set we could edit. Because the most common tape can misrepresent a population, we also classified random cells, and we followed single mutations through serial transfers.

### Order before life

The soup holds 20,000 programs of L bytes on a 160 × 125 lattice (Fig. 1a). At each step 8,192 random cells are each paired with a lattice neighbour, the pair is executed as one program for 128 instructions and written back, and a background mutation replaces a fixed number of random bytes; nothing else happens (Methods). A tape is *heritable* if, lifted out and executed against random partners, it makes copies that copy in turn; emergence is dated by the first heritable tape among the most common, and the heritable fraction of random cells is the population measure.

The first thing to form is order that does not inherit. When the most common pattern first occupies a tenth of the soup, 24–34% of all bytes are zero at every tape length from 9 to 100 (Fig. 2). The zeros are self-written: push, call and reset instructions executed with empty registers write zeros wherever the stack pointer points, and zero is itself an instruction that does nothing, so the pattern spreads by being written and changes nothing when executed. We call this flood *tar*, after the asphalt that prebiotic chemistry makes from the reactions that also make sugars^24^. The tar is what detectors see: the high-order entropy used to detect the transition in random-program soups^16^ is excellent at L = 16 but near chance or inverted in unablated worlds at six of eight lengths from 25 to 100 bytes, a complexity-times-abundance measure of selection^25,26^ passes ten times its random-soup value before the first heritable replicator in 71 of 80 worlds, and the first replicator's assembly index is the minimum for its length, shared by every tape that repeats a two-byte word, tar included (Extended Data Fig. 1). Order comes first, it is as simple as what follows, and only intervention tells the two apart.

### The first replicator is open

The first heritable replicator is the same program everywhere: the two-byte word `01 c5`, or one of its register variants, repeated to fill the tape (Fig. 1a). On tapes of 16 to 64 bytes with benign tar it comes first in 170 of 170 worlds, across five lengths, the Z80 and its 8080-like subset, and two pairing rules, at Kaplan–Meier medians of 300–750 steps (100 with well-mixed pairing; Fig. 1b); on shorter tapes block copiers come first. Executed, the word loads its own next two bytes into a register pair and writes that pair where the stack pointer points, in the partner: the program is its own data, and it copies itself as a side effect of being run. It has no loop, no counter and no test of its surroundings. It is also nearly the only thing of its size that replicates: of all 65,536 two-byte words tiled to 16 bytes, exactly five, besides the trivial zero word, copy themselves into an all-zero partner, all of them load-and-push pairs (Extended Data Fig. 2; Supplementary Information, Proposition 3). Removing the instructions it uses removes it: without immediate loads 2 of 10 worlds produce a replicator by 300,000 steps, and without the stack writers life arrives 160- to 992-fold later as a block-copy replicator; beyond the copying instructions, no other family matters at 128 instructions per encounter (Extended Data Fig. 3).

The first replicator is *open*. Its pointer fetches partner bytes in every one of 256 encounters, for every traced first replicator under benign tar (100 of 100, Stages G and K, on a traced copy of the executor; Methods). Against 256 random partners it copies into a median 0.66–0.73 of them and loses a quarter or more of its own bytes in a median 0.06–0.43 of encounters (Fig. 3a,b), because after writing its copy it executes the partner's bytes, which can poison the register pair before the next push. With the organism fixed and execution deterministic, the information that flows from the partner into the offspring equals the entropy of the offspring over random partners (Methods). The offspring is an exact copy of the parent in a median 58% of encounters at L = 16, 32% at L = 20, 5% at L = 50 and almost never at L = 64; every one of its L positions varies across partners; and its entropy over 256 partners runs from 3.9 bits at L = 16 to the 8-bit ceiling of the sample at L = 64 (Fig. 3c): what a child looks like depends in part on the neighbour it was made against. In the population the median heritable fraction of random cells stays between 0.09 and 0.19 from step 2,000 to step 20,000 at L = 16 (Fig. 3d): the first replicator's reign is a world of damaged copies. Its lineages do not last either: copied serially into fresh random partners, 96–100% of them stop copying within eight transfers (Fig. 4a).

### Closure, where a closer is within reach

The first replicator does not stay. At L = 16 the dominant tape carries a control-flow instruction in 20 of 20 worlds by 300,000 steps, 17 of them ending on the identical eight-byte sequence whose conditional return sends the pointer back into the organism's own body (Fig. 3e; Extended Data Table 1). Every such successor copies into all 256 random partners without self-damage, whichever instruction closes the cycle (Fig. 3a,b,f), and its offspring carry nothing detectable from the partner: in 63 of 67 loop-bearing final dominants the offspring is one and the same string in all 256 encounters (Fig. 3c; Methods). Random cells show that this holds for populations, not only for their most heritable common tape: at the last snapshot every sampled heritable cell is confined in all 20 worlds at L = 16, and most heritable cells are confined in 19 of 20 worlds at L = 20, 12 of 20 at L = 32 and 8 of 20 at L = 64 (Fig. 4d; Methods). The median heritable fraction of random cells rises to 0.75–0.94 when the closed successor takes over at L = 16 (Fig. 3d).

We call this property *closure*. Three things travel together here and must be kept apart: a loop in the code (syntax), confinement of the pointer to the organism's cells (mechanism) and independence of the offspring from the partner (outcome); all three were measured. On the traced executor every loop-bearing final dominant fetches no partner byte as instruction in any encounter (89 of 89 traced, in Stages G, K and I); confinement and zero inflow agree in 106 of 110 finals, the four exceptions being confined finals that keep one to three partner bytes, and no final whose pointer enters the partner is free of inflow (Methods; Extended Data Fig. 4). In this operational sense closure is operational closure^1^ made countable and one component of individuality^4^ made measurable by intervention. It is not a membrane, since the organism's bytes are as exposed as before, and not a property of place^27^: well-mixed pairing left the open phase as heritable and closure later rather than sooner (7 of 10 worlds against 20 of 20; Methods). It is a cycle in the organism's control flow that never leaves the organism (Fig. 3f). The return and the block copy need nothing but the instruction set; the relative jump depends on a convention of the harness, a 16-bit program counter in a memory addressed modulo 2L, which lands a backward jump across address 0 inside the organism only at L = 20 and 50, and 25 of the 67 closers use it (Methods). At the aligned lengths, where no jump can use the wrap, closure is slower and monotone in L: 20 of 20 worlds by 300,000 steps at L = 16, mostly by the return, 12 of 20 by one million at L = 32, all by block copy, and 8 of 20 at L = 64, all but one by block copy; given ten million steps, every world closed at L = 16 and 20 (20 of 20; Extended Data Fig. 5).

At L = 50 the most common tapes suggested closure and random cells withdrew it. The most heritable common tape is the pusher tiling with three inserted relative jumps, which never enters its partner and writes the same offspring in every encounter; but that offspring carries a fourth jump and enters the partner in every encounter, the next generation carries six and fails the culture test, and the lineage stops copying at the third transfer (Methods). Tapes carrying the jump hold 70–76% of the cells, yet only 18–39% of heritable cells are confined, and in no world is the most common tape closed (Fig. 4d). The jump is selected: seeded at 1% into a world of its ancestor, it is carried by 73–75% of the cells by step 300, but the confined share stays at 0.19–0.25 (Extended Data Fig. 9a). At L = 50 the closed tape is a stage its lineage passes through, not an individual.

Where closure did not come, the closer was missing from what evolution had found, not from what the instruction set allows. In the 8080-like subset no world closed at L = 32 in a million steps (below), yet a 20-byte closer of 8080 instructions exists (Methods). Seeded at 1% into a world of the open pusher, its loop was in half of the cells within 300–320 steps and the pusher below 1% of cells within 490–1,000 steps in 5 of 5 soups, after which at most 2% of heritable cells were open; the pusher seeded into the closer's world did not spread (Extended Data Fig. 9b,c). There the barrier was discovery, not expressibility or competition.

### Closure regenerates or transmits

Closure need not cost a lineage its variation, but in most worlds it did. We followed every single-byte mutant of 14 genomes through eight serial transfers into fresh random partners, counting every lineage as lost, alive with the mutation erased, or alive carrying it (Fig. 4a–c; Methods). The pusher's mutations reach its first copies, at 16 of 50 and 30 of 64 positions, but not their descendants: 96–100% of its lineages stop copying within eight transfers, with or without a mutation, and with partners drawn from its own world the alleles are gone by the fourth transfer as well. The evolved closers, the return at L = 16 and the block-copy tilings at L = 20 and 32, are the opposite: no unmutated lineage is lost, but they copy the code they run and regenerate the rest of the tape from it, so 60–88% of their mutant lineages live on with the mutation erased, none keeps it, and the remainder are lethal. A third design, a short copying core followed by bytes it copies but never runs, a genotype in von Neumann's and Langton's sense^29,30^, keeps the allele in 46–63% of its mutant lineages after eight transfers, at 9 to 20 positions, 7 to 12 of them never executed (Fig. 4c): the lethal-tar closer with the most first-generation sites, three genomes that arose in the ten-million-step worlds, and the 8080 closer carrying 12 arbitrary bytes. That a block-copy core can carry arbitrary trailing bytes was shown by Cicala et al., whose prefix reproduced 28 random bytes^22^; what the soups add is which design arises, where, and what becomes of it. Erasure is not canalisation in Waddington's sense^31^, which buffers the phenotype while genetic variation persists: here the variation itself does not pass to the offspring.

Which design a world holds depends on the world, and its most common tape hides it. A regenerating population is nearly clonal, so its genome is the most common tape; a transmitting population is a cloud of distinct genomes, none of them common. We therefore classified random cells (Fig. 4d,e). [[GPOP]] Under lethal tar, closers that copy bytes they never run are the majority of heritable cells in 9 of 10 worlds, with a median of 11.5 such sites, while the most common tape holds 0.2–1.5% of the soup: life that begins closed under lethal tar begins with a genome. In the ten-million-step worlds at L = 20, such transmitters held at least a tenth of the heritable cells at some snapshot in 10 of 10 worlds and the majority in 4, and regenerators were the majority again in 8 of 10 at the end (Fig. 4e).

In the one direct contest we ran, regeneration won, and robustness as we measured it does not explain why. The evolved block-copy tiling of L = 32, seeded at 1% into a world of the payload-carrying closer, held 58–80% of the cells at 50,000 steps in 5 of 5 soups, and the payload carrier seeded into a world of the tiling did not spread (Extended Data Fig. 9c). Without that competitor, in the 8080 subset, the payload carrier's descendants kept 15–17 bytes they never execute, different from cell to cell, and carried them through eight transfers (Methods). The first replicator is at least as robust to single mutations as its successor in our assay (a median 0.74 against 0.59 of single mutants remain heritable at L = 16, 0.99 against 0.95 at L = 50; Extended Data Fig. 6), so a single-mutant robustness advantage^28^ does not explain the succession under our conditions; Cicala et al.'s comparison^22^ used other architectures, budgets, partners and criteria.

### Two properties of the substrate decide the beginning

If the order of events follows the literal channel, a machine without one should be born loop-bearing, and giving it one should start life open. BFF, the Brainfuck family of Agüera y Arcas et al.^16^, has no literal channel: its instructions increment in place or copy from memory, a program that keeps its pointer in its own 64 cells needs at least 129 instructions to copy a tape with two or more distinct bytes into every partner, and no straight-line program can write a copy of a tape with more than 32 non-zero bytes into an empty partner (Theorem 1). Soups of 2¹⁷ programs run for 16,384 epochs test the prediction (Fig. 5). In BFF as published and under a wrapping pointer, 28 of 48 worlds produced a heritable replicator; every first replicator contains a loop (28 of 28) and none is open; with harmless brackets, 7 of 12 did, all loop-bearing but not all confined (five confined, one entering its partner in 23% of encounters, one in every encounter), and four lost the replicator again by 16,384 epochs. The absence of a literal channel predicts a loop, not confinement. Adding one instruction that writes its own two operand bytes below the write head changes the beginning completely: in 12 of 12 worlds under either pointer rule the first replicator is the one-byte tiling of that instruction, loop-free, with its pointer entering the partner in every encounter (Fig. 5b). It is the `01 c5` of BFF, and without a wrapping pointer its offspring carry 8 bits of the partner, the ceiling of the measurement (Extended Data Fig. 7).

Then it dies: each soup fills with tar that halts the pointer, since an unmatched bracket ends BFF execution, copies into such partners are cut short and sterile, and by epoch 1,024 the open replicator holds under 1% of the soup; no closed class evolves, and none was found among periodic tilings of the literal, the brackets and a no-op up to period 10 (Methods). The open phase is a *window* whose length the tar sets. Making an unmatched bracket a no-op leaves the open replicator in place in 12 of 12 worlds for all 16,384 epochs (Fig. 5c); making it halt with probability p reveals a threshold rather than a dial: the replicator holds 98% of the soup at p ≤ 0.1 (18 of 18 soups), and at p = 0.3 it collapses within about 900 epochs and recovers only to a tenth of the soup (6 of 6; Fig. 5c). The write ratio sets the other boundary. When the literal writes two or three copies of its word, so that a copy completes within one pass, the first replicator is still the loop-free tiling that enters its partner, but its offspring carry 0 bits of the partner, information-closed from birth without a cycle, and it never collapses: every random tape is heritable at 16,384 epochs in 12 of 12 soups (Fig. 5d; Methods). The lethal column holds in the first machine too. Making a zero byte halt execution, so that the Z80's own tar becomes lethal, removes the open beginning altogether: in 10 of 10 pre-registered worlds no literal pusher ever establishes, and life begins closed and late, as a block-copy replicator with a hardware loop, at a Kaplan–Meier median of 38,000 steps against 450 under benign tar, and heritable material at 40,000 against 1,250 (Extended Data Fig. 8); most of its heritable cells carry bytes they never execute (above). And the beginning is not the Z80's alone. Restricting the Z80 to an 8080-like subset of its instructions, by making every Z80-only instruction a no-op, keeps the literal word and removes the block copies and relative jumps that close most Z80 worlds (Methods); this tests the machine family, not a second processor. The load–push word comes first in every world; at L = 16 the byte-identical return design, pure 8080 code and the Z80's own modal closer, dominates 20 of 20 worlds by 300,000 steps; at L = 32, where the return's popped addresses fall in the partner and there is no block copy, none of 20 worlds closes by one million steps and the open pusher holds every world to the end (Extended Data Fig. 5), consistent with the absence of looping variants on long 8080 tapes in ref. 16, although a closer exists and, seeded, takes over (above). The beginning is the machine family's; what follows depends on whether evolution finds a closer at that ring size. Together these fill a map (Fig. 5e): a literal channel decides whether life starts open; the lethality of the tar decides whether an open beginning lasts, or happens at all; and the closed designs within reach decide whether what follows is closure, a late closed beginning, or extinction.

### What is proved

Two counting statements explain the order of events, although they do not force it (Fig. 6; Supplementary Information). A straight-line program's pointer runs on into its partner, so a straight-line replicator is open by construction; and a closed replicator, whose pointer never leaves its own cells, must execute some address twice, because its 128 executions per encounter outlast a pass over at most 100 cells and nothing stops execution inside it, or, in BFF, because a copy costs more executions than the pair has cells: closure requires a cycle (Theorem 2; Fig. 6a). A straight-line replicator exists wherever the instruction set can write a literal at a self-advancing pointer, and it is the size of the literal; in this machine no closed design is as small (Proposition 3; Fig. 6b), so the open word is present in a random soup with exponentially higher probability than any closed design^32^, although how scattered words nucleate a tiled replicator is not modelled. Without a literal channel the count goes the other way: in BFF no straight-line program writes itself into an empty partner, and a confined copier must loop (Theorem 1). Theorem 2 concerns confinement, and the second machine shows that independence from the partner can be had without it: a copier that writes at least as much as it reads becomes independent by saturation, without a cycle and without confinement, as the wrapping literal pusher with harmless brackets and the literal that writes two or three copies of its word both do, entering the partner in every encounter yet importing almost nothing (0.04 and 0 bits). Confinement and independence are different properties that coincide in the Z80's loop-bearing finals. In information terms independence is zero inflow from context to offspring, I(o; e | x) = 0, which perfect fidelity requires (Lemma 7), and Fig. 3c shows the measured inflow falling to zero in the closed successors. The dynamics lie outside the theorems: closure arrives with probability about 1 − exp(−q ∫ n dt), q being the rate of closing mutations and n the open population, so benign tar lets the window run until a closer is found and lethal tar shuts it first (Supplementary Information, Model 5).

### Discussion

The order of events is observed, and counting explains it: once a machine's default flow and copy cost are given, the smallest thing that writes itself has no room for a return. So the first replicator is open; closure is a return, found where a closing design is within reach; and closure is not one thing for heredity. A closer that copies the code it runs regenerates itself and erases its mutants; a closer with a short copying core carries bytes it never runs and keeps their variants through serial transfers. Benign-tar worlds mostly ended with regenerators and lethal-tar worlds with genomes, and in the one direct contest the regenerator won. The explanation has few adjustable parts: the dichotomy is arithmetic, the literal channel is read off a substrate, and the lethality of by-products is measured. It answers questions it was not built for: why the first replicators are sloppy; why a cycle, not a membrane, is the first thing selection builds; why inherited variation can fall at the first transition in individuality, when the first closer regenerates; and why the first innovations after replication should concern where copying starts, stops and returns. When the literal road is blocked, by removing the stack writers, by a language without a literal channel or by lethal tar, life can still come, late and loop-bearing: the exponentially rarer closed beginning.

These are predictions for chemistry, not results about it. Template copying is a literal channel, a strand specifying its complement by physical fit with no interpreter, so the first replicators of such a chemistry should be the shortest self-templating repeats, dependent on their surroundings and productive mainly of partial copies; transfer into fresh material and the variation of the product with its context would measure both. By-products are a variable: whether a chemistry's tar is inert or inhibitory to copying should decide whether an open phase lasts long enough for control to evolve, which matched inert and inhibitory additives in nonenzymatic systems can test^24^. Processivity is the chemical write ratio, which in the second machine closes a copier in information without any control: vertical lineages should appear when one copying event spans the genome^33^, and in laboratory evolution of RNA replicators the earliest fixed changes should concern control, circularisation, terminators and primers^34,35^, and inherited sequence that is copied but not used should be commoner where by-products are inhibitory. Detection of life at its origin should be by intervention, since low-complexity order precedes the first replicators and trips compression and assembly measures alike^25,26^. What limits the claims is plain: the Z80, an 8080-like restriction of it, and one toy language; units of fixed size, start address and pairing imposed by the harness; a culture test that is our own ground truth, though the order of events held under every setting we tried; closure at the largest lengths in progress rather than complete within our horizons, and absent from populations at L = 50, where the closed tape is a passing stage; and one direct contest between the two kinds of closer. Within those limits the result stands: heredity came first; independence from context came later, as a return in control flow; and whether that return cost the organism its variation depended on whether it regenerated the code it ran or also copied bytes it never ran.

## Methods

**Soup.** The soup holds N = 20,000 tapes of L bytes (L from 3 to 100), initialised with uniformly random bytes, on a 160 × 125 square lattice. At every step 8,192 cells are drawn uniformly at random and each is paired with one of its four lattice neighbours (a step off the edge is reflected; the lattice does not wrap); a pair is active if neither member is already in another pair that step, which leaves ≈ 3,980 active pairs. An active pair is concatenated, organism first, into a pair memory of P bytes (P = 2L except in the padding experiment of Stage F, where P ≥ 2L and the padding bytes are never written back), executed by a Z80 emulator for a fixed budget of 128 instructions (32–2,048 in the budget arms) from byte 0 with all registers zero and the stack pointer at the last byte of the partner, and both tapes are written back. After each step 8,192/2^k bytes chosen uniformly at random over the soup are replaced by random bytes (k = 4 unless stated; k = 1–6 in the mutation arms). There is no fitness function, no replacement and no death. Instruction-set ablations replace whole opcode families by no-ops through a prefix-aware fetch hook that sees the operation after `DD`/`FD` and `ED`/`CB` prefix resolution; the ablations and their opcode counts are listed in Extended Data Table 2. The emulator was validated against an independent Z80 reference with zero divergences in randomised 5,000-pair tests with and without ablation; no claim depends on cycle-exact timing.

**Recording.** Every run writes a sample at fixed steps (every step for the first 30, then a geometric schedule, then every 500 or 1,000) with the ten most common exact tapes and their shares, quasispecies^36^ occupancy (fraction of cells within Hamming distance ⌈L/4⌉ of the dominant tape), byte histogram, zero-byte fraction and byte entropy, brotli-compressed bits per byte and the high-order entropy of ref. 16, species entropy and unique-tape fraction, per-interaction statistics (bytes written into the partner, copy events, destroyed programs), 16 random tapes for the functional census, and full soup snapshots at fixed steps and at emergence.

**Culture test and partner tests.** A tape runs as the first program against 64 uniformly random partners for the condition's budget; copy success is the fraction of partners that become ≥ 75% copies at the best cyclic shift. The score of a tape x is the mean, over partners j whose best-shift match to x before the encounter, s_j, is below 0.75, of (s′_j − s_j)/(1 − s_j), s′_j being the match after it. gen2 is the same quantity one generation on: the offspring of encounter j runs as organism against a fresh random partner, whose match to x is measured before and after, so offspring that are not copies contribute about zero and gen2 approximates the share of encounters whose offspring copies in turn. Heritable means gen2 ≥ 0.3, faithful additionally ≥ 50% of partners copied. Partner-independence tests use 256 random partners and record self-damage (≥ 25% of the organism's bytes changed in one encounter). In BFF the culture test also records whether the instruction pointer entered the partner in each encounter; closed means ≤ 5% of encounters entered and ≥ 95% of partners copied, open means ≥ 50% entered. Information inflow from the environment, I(o; e | x) = H(o | x) for a deterministic executor, is the plug-in entropy in bits of the offspring (the partner's bytes after the encounter) over 256 random partners, ceiling 8 bits.

**Events and statistics.** `t_rep` is the first sampled step at which one of the three most common tapes has a share ≥ 0.5% and is heritable by the culture test (a clonality criterion by design: it dates the first *clone*, not the first heritable material); `t_faith` additionally requires faithfulness; the *final dominant* is the most heritable of the three most common tapes at the horizon. It is the most common tape in 57 of the 60 Stage G worlds at L = 16, 20 and 64; at L = 50 it is the second most common in all 20, behind the plain pusher tiling (5.5–6.6% of cells), while the tapes that carry its jump word `20 f0` hold 70–76% of cells. Its offspring is then the parent with one more `20 f0` inserted, the same string in every encounter; that offspring enters the partner in every encounter, the next generation (six jumps) is confined but fails the culture test (gen2 0.19), and the one after (ten jumps) no longer copies (`l50_lineage.py`), so at L = 50 the final dominant is a stage of a lineage rather than a lineage. Because the most common tape can misrepresent a diverse population, population claims rest on random cells (Population sampling, below); `tq_10` (quasispecies occupancy ≥ 10%) is the pre-registered occupancy event and fires on zero floods as well as on replicators. Emergence fractions are quoted as worlds reaching the event within the horizon with Wilson 95% intervals; times as Kaplan–Meier medians with censoring at the horizon. Ablation effects are ratios of Kaplan–Meier medians with seed-paired sign tests (the same seeds run under every ablation); confirmatory contrasts use one-sided Fisher exact tests and the Cochran–Mantel–Haenszel test pooled over budgets; the paired gain of control flow uses the exact McNemar test; between-variant transition rates in BFF use two-sided Fisher tests and Mann–Whitney tests on event times with non-transitioning runs censored at the horizon. Across the 233 unablated worlds of the exploratory stages, 92 gained a control-flow instruction between the first replicator and the final dominant and 3 lost one (exact McNemar P = 7 × 10⁻²⁴). In BFF, where replicator populations are quasispecies with small exact-class shares, the first replicator is the most common tape class (a tape and its reverse count as one class) at the first sample at which it is heritable, and the population event is a heritable fraction ≥ 0.5 of 32 random tapes. All thresholds (0.75 copy, gen2 ≥ 0.3, 0.5% share, 10% occupancy, 95%/5% closure) were fixed before the confirmatory stages. Re-dating the first replicator from the recorded runs under heredity thresholds from 0.2 to 0.4 leaves the load–push word first in 170 of 170 benign-tar worlds of 16–64 bytes; at 0.5 and 0.6, near the pusher's own gen2, it is first in 155 of 169 and 107 of 164. Principal medians are given with 95% bootstrap intervals across worlds (Extended Data Table 3). Same-seed Z80 runs diverge because GPU scheduling makes the pairing order non-deterministic, so every Z80 claim is a population claim over seeds (ten same-seed repeats spread as widely as ten seeds); the BFF soup is bitwise reproducible.

**Definitions and theorems.** A *self-writer* is a tape that, executed against the all-zero partner, leaves the partner holding a copy of itself. *Closure*: the organism's bytes are forward-invariant under execution for every context in the class of uniformly random partners; equivalently its pointer never executes a partner address. Theorem 1: a BFF program whose pointer stays in its own 64 cells needs at least 129 instructions to copy a tape with two or more distinct bytes into every partner that holds none of the copy's bytes in place, so it executes some address twice, that is, carries a loop; and in an empty partner no execution that runs each address at most once can write a copy of a tape with more than 32 non-zero bytes. Theorem 2: in any machine whose pointer advances by default, a closed full replicator executes some address twice, provided a copy costs more instruction executions than the organism has addresses (BFF) or execution cannot stop inside the organism before the budget is spent (the Z80 soups, budget 128 > L). Theorem 6: a self-consistent literal-write instruction gives a straight-line, hence open, replicator the size of the literal; without one, under the copy-cost condition, every replicator contains a cycle. Lemma 7: I(o; e | x) > 0 implies imperfect fidelity across contexts; perfect fidelity implies I = 0. Proposition 3 (computer-assisted): the two-byte words fixed under their own execution against the zero partner are exactly the five load–push words, all straight-line. Full statements and proofs are in the Supplementary Information.

**Traced execution.** A derived copy of the culture-test executor records every address fetched as instruction stream (opcodes, prefixes, operands) in a 256-bit map per encounter, with byte-identical execution (checked). Pointer confinement is the fraction of 256 encounters with a seeded random partner in which no partner address is fetched; the executed set is the union of fetched organism positions; a transmissible position of the mutational scan is called unexecuted if it is never fetched. Every first replicator and final dominant of Stages G, K and I (110 of each) was traced. Confinement concerns the instruction pointer only; a confined copier may read its partner's bytes as data.

**Mutational scan.** For each Stage G first replicator and final dominant, every single-byte mutant (all 255 alternative values per position at L = 16 and 20; 32 seeded values per position at L = 50 and 64) was run as organism A against 32 shared random partners for 128 instructions and its offspring against 32 fresh partners, with the culture test's thresholds. A mutant is heritable if gen2 ≥ 0.3; its mutation is transmitted if the mutant byte is present at the aligned position in at least half of the offspring that are ≥ 75% copies; a position is a transmissible site if at least half of its tested values are both heritable and transmitted; the single-mutant variation score is Σ_i log₂(1 + 255 f_i) over positions, f_i being the fraction of transmissible values at position i, a summary of how many single mutants are heritable and reach first-generation copies, not a channel capacity (it ignores epistasis and later generations). Transmission is scored in first-generation copies only; serial retention follows alleles further. Three predictions were written before the run (the successor more robust to mutation than the first replicator; the closed form with more transmissible sites; the matched pusher-with-jump of L = 50 with more sites than its pusher); all three failed and are reported as such.

**Pair invasion.** Soups of 20,000 cells at L = 50 under the Stage G dynamics, every cell set to one tape of the matched pair (the pusher tiling, or the same tiling with `20 f0` inserted three times, the final dominant of 14 Stage G worlds) and 1% of cells to the other at step 0; five seeds per direction, three seeds per resident with no invader; 20,000 steps. The pre-registered outcome, the invader's nearest-class share passing 50%, could not be met at this length because the quasispecies cloud splits between the two classes (a closed tape that has lost two jumps is nearer the pusher); the share of cells carrying the jump word, reported instead, was defined after the data were seen. In single-tape invasion assays the pusher tiling `01 c5` × 8 seeded into 1% of the cells of a random soup at L = 16 (128 instructions, mutation 1/16, five soups) peaked at a median occupancy of 0.32 at step 250. The jump-word marker was checked against traced confinement before use (registered): in three re-runs of the closed-into-ancestor invasion, 512 random cells at each of eight steps were traced against 16 random partners. The marker share reached 0.73–0.75 while the share of cells confined in all 16 encounters stayed at 0.19–0.25, and only 26–33% of marker-carrying cells were confined; the registered agreement failed, so the L = 50 invasion is reported as the spread of the jump, not of closure (Extended Data Fig. 9a).

**Serial allele retention.** Fourteen genomes (Fig. 4a) were scanned with every single-byte mutant (all 255 values per position at L ≤ 32, 32 seeded values at L ≥ 50): four open pushers, the most common closed final dominant at each of L = 16, 20 and 32, the L = 50 final dominant, the lethal-tar final dominant with the most first-generation sites, the most common tape of three Stage L worlds at the first snapshot at which a genome-bearing block copier was most common, and the 20-byte 8080 closer with two random 12-byte payloads. Each mutant founded 16 lineages and the unmutated tape 256. In each of eight transfers the lineage tape ran as organism against a fresh uniformly random partner under the genome's own rule (8080 subset, lethal tar) and the partner's bytes after the encounter became the next lineage tape; lineage r met the same partners for every mutant. A transfer is a copy if the offspring matches its parent at ≥ 75% of positions at the best cyclic shift, and a lineage is alive while every transfer has been a copy. The allele is carried if the mutant's five-byte window centred on the mutation occurs anywhere in the lineage tape, cyclically, which needs no alignment, so inserted jumps and shifts do not hide it; mutants whose window occurs in the unmutated tape would be excluded (none did). Every lineage is counted as lost (not alive), erased (alive, allele absent) or carrying the allele. A position is serially transmissible after g transfers if, for at least half of its values, the share of alive lineages carrying the allele exceeds by at least 0.5 the share of alive unmutated lineages that contain the same window by chance. Design and predictions were registered before the run: the pusher's sites were predicted to persist (failed: its lineages are lost); the evolved closers to erase, the transient genomes to keep at least five sites and the constructed closer its payload (met). A post hoc variant drew partners from cells of the organism's own world (Stage G, seed 2001) instead of random bytes.

**Population sampling.** At the final snapshot of every Stage G, I, K, L and M world, 256 random cells were culture-tested (32 partners) and traced against 16 random partners; a cell is confined if no partner address is fetched as instruction in any of the 16 (registered). At every snapshot of the same worlds, 64 random cells were culture-tested and traced, and up to 16 random heritable cells were given the single-mutant scan (16 values per position, 16 partners) and their executed set (defined before the run and exploratory, since the first sample motivated it). A heritable cell is open if not confined; a confined one is a regenerator (at most two transmissible sites), intermediate (three or four) or a transmitter (five or more); the classes of unscanned confined cells are inferred from the scanned ones. A world is closed at the population level if most of its heritable cells are confined. For confined cells the first-generation scan agrees with serial retention (Fig. 4a,c); for open cells it overstates it, so open cells are reported as open.

**Invasions of a constructed closer.** Stage M dynamics at L = 32. A1: the 20-byte 8080 closer with payload 1 seeded at 1% into a world filled with the 8080 pusher, and the reverse, under the 8080 subset, five seeds each and three unseeded controls per resident, 20,000 steps. A2: the same closer against the evolved block-copy tiling `04 5e ed b0` × 8 under the full Z80, 50,000 steps. The registered outcome, the share of cells carrying the closer's exact 20-byte core, failed on definition: the core mutates within a few hundred steps while its descendants hold the soup. We report the share carrying the closer's loop body (post hoc) and the classes of 64 random cells per soup at the last step (Extended Data Fig. 9).

**Address mapping and aligned lengths.** Pair memory is a ring of 2L bytes: every memory access uses the 16-bit address modulo 2L, and the stack pointer starts at the largest 16-bit value that aliases the last byte of the partner. The program counter and the stack pointer themselves are 16-bit, so a relative jump or a stack move that crosses address 0 reaches (65,536 + target) mod 2L, which equals the true-ring target only when 2L divides 65,536. Of the 67 closed final dominants of Stage G, 25 (all 20 at L = 50 and 5 of 19 at L = 20) use a relative jump whose target depends on this aliasing; the pair-invasion and mutational-scan results at L = 50 are results about the machine so defined. Stage K (L = 32, 20 worlds, one million steps, seeds 5001–5020; pre-registered: first replicator a load–push word in ≥ 18 of 20, a closed successor in ≥ 15 of 20, kill below 10) tested closure at an aligned intermediate length: 20 of 20 and 12 of 20, all closers block copies.

**The 8080 subset.** The `i8080` suppression makes every Z80-only instruction a no-op: the 256 CB-page opcodes, the 78 defined ED-page opcodes (block copies and their repeats, 16-bit ADC and SBC, IN and OUT (C), NEG, RETN and RETI, IM, LD A,I and A,R, RLD and RRD) and EX AF,AF', DJNZ, the five JR forms and EXX. This is an 8080-like restriction of the same emulator, not an 8080. Two deviations from a real 8080 are kept: IX and IY prefixes still select the indexed form of an 8080 instruction, and suppressed bytes are no-ops rather than the 8080's undocumented aliases. Stage M: Stage G conditions at L = 16 (300,000 steps) and L = 32 (one million), 20 worlds each, seeds 7001–7020, with the predictions registered before the run. The registered reading that the subset expresses no closer on a 32-byte ring was wrong and is withdrawn: a reviewer's 20-byte closer of 8080 instructions (`21 20 00 31 40 00 16 10 2b 46 2b 4e c5 15 c2 08 00 c3 11 00`, a counted push loop closed by a conditional jump, then a jump to itself) copies itself and any 12 following bytes exactly into 256 of 256 random partners without entering them, in our executor under the subset (`check_8080_closer.py`).

**Ten-million-step runs.** Stage L: the Stage G conditions at L = 16 and 20, ten worlds each (seeds 6001–6010), run for ten million steps with samples every 5,000 steps and full snapshots at the Stage G steps and at 2, 3, 5, 7.5 and 10 million. At every snapshot of these and of the Stage G, K and I worlds, the most common exact tape was scanned (32 values per position, 32 partners) and traced (64 partners), giving its transmissible sites, single-mutant variation score, executed set and pointer confinement against step (a modal-tape analysis; population classes are in Fig. 4d,e). Predictions registered before the run: L1, in at least 5 of 10 worlds at each length the dominant at ten million steps has more transmissible sites than the closed dominant at 300,000 (L = 16) or one million (L = 20) steps; L2, every such recovery is in a block-copy lineage and its sites are copied-but-unexecuted positions; L3, no return to an open dominant. Outcomes: L1 not met (1 of 10 at L = 16, 0 of 10 at L = 20); L2 not met at L = 16 (the one recovery, from 0 to 4 sites, is a block-copy lineage with one of its four sites unexecuted) and not applicable at L = 20; L3 not met as written, because the open pusher at L = 20 holds two to three transmissible sites and the jump-based closer two; the kill criterion fired (final dominant with at most one site in 9 of 10 and 8 of 10 worlds). Every world closed, at 10,000–75,000 steps (L = 16) and 20,000–500,000 (L = 20); the heritable fraction of random cells at ten million steps is a median 0.89 and 0.95.

**Controls.** Stage H, well-mixed pairing (the partner drawn uniformly from the soup) at L = 16, ten worlds, seeds 3001–3010; pre-registered: the open phase as heritable as on the lattice (met) and closure sooner (not met: 7 of 10 worlds against 20 of 20 by 300,000 steps); the first replicator met its own kind in 57% of encounters on the lattice and 11% when mixed. Stage I, lethal tar: a zero byte fetched as an opcode halts the pair for the rest of the encounter (a change to the machine's semantics, not only to the fate of its by-product), at L = 16, ten worlds, seeds 4001–4010, with the culture and partner tests under the same rule; the heritable fraction of random cells rose from 0 at 20,000 steps to 0.41 at 30,000 and 0.88 at 50,000 (medians over worlds).

**BFF.** cubff default semantics verified from source: two 64-byte tapes concatenated, pointer and heads at byte 0, ten instructions, every other byte a no-op, unmatched brackets halt, 2¹³ steps per encounter, 2¹⁷ programs, random ordered pairs each epoch, mutation 2⁻¹² per byte per epoch. Switches: `ip_wrap` (pointer wraps modulo 128); `literal` (byte 0x50 = `P a b`: write a at head1−1 and b at head1−2, then head1 −= 2, skip the operands; designed so that its one-byte tiling writes itself, the analogue of the Z80's load–push word); `nohalt` (unmatched brackets are no-ops); `halt_p` (an unmatched bracket halts with probability p, decided by a hash of the pair, the step and the seed); `lit_rep` (the literal instruction writes r copies of its word per execution). Our GPU kernel reproduces cubff's validation replicator and matches a CPU reference on randomised tests. 24 seeds each for the published and wrapping variants, 12 for each literal variant, 12 for the published rule with no-op brackets (run after review; pre-registered: every first replicator loop-bearing, met 7 of 7; a transition in at least 9 of 12, not met at 7; pointer-closed first replicators in every world, not met in two, one intermediate (pointer enters in 23% of encounters) and one open), 6 for each of p = 0.01, 0.03, 0.1 and 0.3 under wrap + literal push, and 6 for each of r = 2 and 3 under the literal push without a wrapping pointer; events every 64 epochs; high-order entropy with brotli quality 2 as in ref. 16. Dial predictions, registered before the run: D1-1, the open population persists (share ≥ 10% at 16,384 epochs) in at least 4 of 6 soups at p = 0.01 (met, 6 of 6) and in at most 1 of 6 at p = 0.3 (not met: the share falls below 1% at 832–896 epochs in 6 of 6 and recovers to 0.10–0.11 in 5 of 6, with 0.31–0.44 of random tapes heritable); D1-2, the epoch of collapse falls monotonically with p within a factor of three of 1/p (not met: no collapse at p ≤ 0.1, 896 at p = 0.3, 192 at p = 1); D1-3, no closure by control flow at any p (met); D2-1, inflow at most 1 bit at r ≥ 2 (met: 0.00 bits in 12 of 12 soups at r = 2 and 3, against 8.00 in 12 of 12 at r = 1); D2-2, the first replicator still the loop-free, pointer-open tiling (met, 12 of 12); D2-3, collapse slower or absent at r ≥ 2 (met: absent, with an all-`P` share of 22–31% at r = 2 and 4–7% at r = 3 at epoch 1,024 against 0–0.2% at r = 1, and every random tape heritable at 16,384 epochs).

**Searches.** Two-byte census: all 65,536 words tiled to 16 bytes, culture test (32 partners) and partner test (256), plus the deterministic fixed-point test against the zero partner. BFF straight-line search: 16,447,860 programs (prefix ≤ 3 instructions + tiling unit of period 2–5 with a copy instruction), 2 random partners, wrapping pointer, alphabet prefilter then exact similarity, full culture test for survivors. Closed-replicator search: all 1,309,528 periodic tilings of {`P`, `[`, `]`, no-op} up to period 10 under the literal, wrapping, no-halt rules, three partners each.

**Pre-registration and stages.** Stages D, F and G, the BFF cells and every stage added in revision (K, L, M, the mutational scan, the pair invasion, the traced execution and the dials) were pre-registered with predictions and kill criteria before launch; every amendment is dated in the change log. Misses are reported in the text: the heritable-fraction thresholds of G1(c) failed at L = 50; the syntactic loop criterion of G2 omitted hardware loops (6 of 20 and 3 of 20 syntactic against 19 of 20 and 8 of 20 behavioural); the expectation of 15 of 20 closures at L = 32 was not met (12); the mechanism first claimed for an open BFF replicator under a wrapping pointer was falsified by the harness test before any soup ran. Exploratory stages: A (630 runs, L = 16: seven ablations × budgets {32, 128, 512} × mutation {1/4, 1/16, 1/64}, ten seeds, 300,000 steps), B (640: tape lengths 4–100 under two ablations and three budgets), C (490: thirteen ablations at L = 16, seeds 101–110, with one-million-step nulls), E (1,230: the size axis under four control arms, budget and mutation sweeps at L = 100). Confirmatory stages: D (280: the L = 9 reversal, seeds 1001–1020), F (210: padded pair memories), G (80: closure, seeds 2001–2020, L = 16 and 50 for 300,000 steps, L = 20 and 64 for one million), K, L, M as above.

## Supplementary Information: theorems and proofs

*What kind of statement each item is: a **Theorem** has a pen-and-paper proof and uses no simulation; a **Proposition** is an exact statement decided by finite computation, with the computation described; a **Model** is a result about a stylised model with stated assumptions, not about the simulator. Empirical premises are marked as such, and every number quoted is from the generated tables behind the main text.*

**Notation.** An encounter executes a program held in a shared memory. The organism A occupies addresses 0 to L − 1 and the partner B the next L addresses; in the Z80 soups the 2L addresses form a ring, in BFF the 128 addresses do not wrap. Execution starts at address 0 with a fixed initial machine state (registers zero; the Z80 stack pointer at the last byte of B) and lasts at most T instruction executions (T = 128 in the Z80 soups, 2¹³ in BFF). A *control transfer* is a jump, call, return, repeat-in-place or halt; a *straight-line program* executes none, so that under default flow its pointer advances past each executed instruction. The *no-op context* is the partner all of whose bytes are no-ops (zero in the Z80, where byte 0 is NOP; zero in BFF, where every byte outside the ten instruction characters is a no-op). A tape x is a *self-writer* if its encounter with the no-op context ends with B holding a copy of x (up to a cyclic shift, as in the culture test), and a *full self-replicator in context e* if the encounter (x, e) ends with B holding a copy of x. It is *closed* if its pointer executes no address of B in any context and *open* if it does in some context. A value is *prescribed* if it is determined by x alone, the same for every partner.

**Theorem 1 (the cost of a copy in BFF).** *Setting.* BFF as published: a 128-byte pair; the instruction pointer p and the two data heads h0 and h1 start at address 0; p advances by one after every instruction unless a bracket jumps; `<` `>` and `{` `}` move h0 or h1 by one cell; `+` `−` change the byte under h0 by one; `.` copies the byte under h0 into the cell under h1 and `,` the byte under h1 into the cell under h0; every other byte is a no-op; execution ends when p leaves the pair, on an unmatched bracket, or after 2¹³ steps. *Statement.* Let A be a 64-byte tape with at least two distinct byte values. (i) *Uniform copying.* Let P be the class of partners that hold, in every cell, a byte different from A's byte there. If one instruction sequence, the same in every encounter, leaves B holding a copy of A for every B in P, it has at least 129 instructions. Any program executes such a sequence for as long as its pointer stays out of B, since nothing it fetches depends on B; so a program that copies A into every partner of P without its pointer entering B executes at least 129 instructions at the 64 addresses of A and therefore executes some address twice. (ii) *No-op context.* If B holds only zero bytes (no-ops) and A has m non-zero bytes, an execution that runs each address at most once, as every straight-line execution does, leaves a copy of A in B only if m ≤ 32. Writes, increments and moves are counted as disjoint sets of instructions: w counts `.` and `,`, p counts `+` and `−`, v counts the four moves. *Proof.* (i) Because the instruction sequence is the same for every B in P, so is every head position at every step; call a cell's value *prescribed* at a step if it is the same for every B in P. Initially exactly the cells of A hold prescribed values, since P contains, for each cell of B, partners with different bytes there. `+` and `−` applied to a cell holding an unprescribed value leave it unprescribed (they act bijectively), and a write copies whatever its source holds, so the first prescribed value to reach a cell of B arrives by a write from a cell holding a prescribed value: w ≥ 64. Let the copy have D ≥ 2 distinct values. The first write of each value into B reads it from a cell that holds it; before any cell of B holds a prescribed value only cells of A do, and a cell of A acquires a value it did not hold initially only by a write from a cell that held it or by an increment, so, if p increments run, the heads occupy at least D − p distinct cells of A that held distinct values initially. Both heads start at address 0 and each move changes one head's position by one, so after v moves the heads have occupied at most 1 + v distinct positions; the 64 target cells lie in B and these source cells in A, so 64 + D − p ≤ 1 + v. The instruction count is at least w + v + p ≥ 64 + 63 + D ≥ 129. If D = 1 the tape is one repeated byte and, as a program, one repeated letter: a move letter never writes, a write letter with both heads at address 0 copies a cell onto itself, and `+` or `−` never write into B. (ii) The partner's zero bytes are no-ops, so an execution that runs each address at most once performs at most 64 instructions other than no-ops, those at the addresses of A. Each of the m cells of B whose target byte is non-zero must be changed, and every write, increment or decrement changes one cell, so at least m such instructions run; the heads must occupy those m positions of B, none of which is address 0, and each move adds at most one new position, so at least m moves run. Hence 2m ≤ 64. ∎ *Corollary 1.1.* A BFF program that copies its tape into every partner of P while its pointer stays in its own cells carries a loop, and a self-writer whose tape has more than 32 non-zero bytes carries one. A program whose pointer enters the partner and copies into a random partner is not covered: it then fetches the partner's bytes. That every observed BFF first replicator carries a loop (35 of 35 under the published rule, the wrapping pointer and harmless brackets) is an observation explained, not forced, by these counts; whether the loop also keeps the pointer out of B is a separate, measured fact (0 of 28 first replicators were open under the published rule and the wrapping pointer, 1 of 7 with harmless brackets).

**Theorem 2 (closure requires a cycle).** *Hypothesis (H1), default flow.* In the absence of an executed control transfer the pointer advances past each instruction, so that a straight-line program runs from the organism's region into the partner's. *Statement.* Under (H1), a closed full self-replicator of L bytes executes some address at least twice, provided either (i) a copy of it costs more than L instruction executions, or (ii) execution cannot end inside the organism's region before a budget T > L is spent. *Proof.* A closed execution visits only the L addresses of A. If no address repeats, it comprises at most L instruction executions. Under (i) these cannot complete the copy; under (ii) the budget forces a further execution, which can only be at an address already executed. ∎ *The machines.* BFF satisfies (i) for a closed program that copies into every partner of the class P of Theorem 1: its fetched instructions do not depend on the partner, so a copy costs at least 129 executions, more than the organism's 64 addresses (Theorem 1(i)). The Z80 soups satisfy (ii): the budget is 128 executions for tapes of at most 100 bytes, there is no exit instruction, and HALT holds the processor at its own address, which we count as a one-state cycle. *The bandwidth reading.* Condition (i) holds in any machine whose straight-line code writes fewer bytes than the addresses it consumes, since a pass over L addresses then writes fewer than L bytes. This is the form used in the main text, and it describes the replicators observed: the first replicator writes two bytes per four bytes of code (the executor trace shows eight bytes written when the pointer leaves its sixteen cells), BFF's copy instructions move one byte per instruction and need a head move per cell, and the literal push of modified BFF writes two bytes per three. It is not a machine-wide property of the Z80, in which a chain of pushes writes two bytes per byte of code; for the Z80 the theorem rests on (ii). *Corollary 2.1.* Under (H1) every straight-line program is open: with T > L its pointer reaches the partner's region. A closed self-writer therefore contains an executed control transfer besides the instructions that write its content, and where a straight-line self-writer exists the closed one is longer by at least that instruction. For the Z80 the comparison is exact: by Proposition 3 the self-writers of period one or two are exactly five straight-line load–push words, so the smallest closed self-writer has period at least three (the closed replicators observed have period four or more).

**Proposition 3 (the two-byte self-writers of the Z80; computer-assisted).** Among the 65,536 two-byte words tiled to 16 bytes and executed for 128 instructions as A against the all-zero partner, exactly six leave B holding a tiling of the word: the trivial `00 00`, which B already holds, and the five load–push words `01 c5`, `11 d5`, `21 e5` (`LD rr,nn ; PUSH rr`) and `2a e5`, `e5 2a` (`LD HL,(nn) ; PUSH HL`), decided by exhaustive execution of all 65,536 words. None contains a control transfer, so each runs on into the partner: every two-byte self-writer of this machine is open, and no tiling of period one or two is closed. The stochastic census agrees: the same five words copy into 0.66–0.81 of 256 random partners, none into 0.95 or more, and the 246 words that pass the heredity threshold by smearing return addresses produce no 75% copy.

**Proposition 4 (a count on the initial soup).** In a soup of N tapes of L uniformly random bytes, the expected number of occurrences of a given k-byte word, at any of the L − k + 1 positions of a tape, is N (L − k + 1) / 256^k^: for N = 20,000 and L = 16, 4.6 for a given two-byte word, 0.017 for a given three-byte word and 6.1 × 10^−5^ for a given four-byte word. A given two-byte word is present at step 0 with probability 1 − (1 − 256^−2^)^N(L−1)^ ≈ 0.99, a given three-byte word with probability ≈ 0.017. *What the count does and does not show.* It counts occurrences of the two-byte word, not of the self-writer: the self-writer is the word tiled over the whole tape, and a full tiling is absent at step 0 (its expected number is of order N / 256^L^). How scattered occurrences of the word nucleate a tiling is not modelled. The count therefore makes the open word's raw material abundant from the start and a given closed design's (period at least three, Proposition 3) rare, which is consistent with the observed order, but it does not prove that order. That the first replicator is open is an empirical result (170 of 170 worlds on tapes of 16 to 64 bytes with benign tar); the copying constraint of Theorem 2 and this count are the explanation we offer for it, not a derivation.

**Model 5 (the closure window).** *Assumptions.* An open replicator population of n(t) tapes; each tape-step produces a closed functional variant with probability q (a mutation placing the right bytes at the right phase: q is of order sites × μ × 256^−m^ for an m-byte closing motif); a closed variant, once present, grows faster than the open form and fixes with probability of order one. *Statement.* The expected number of closed variants produced while the open population lasts is q ∫ n dt, and closure occurs with probability ≈ 1 − exp(−q ∫ n dt); the integral is the window. If the open form's own copies are viable in the soup it creates, n stays near the carrying capacity, the window grows without bound and closure is a matter of waiting; if the soup becomes lethal to the open form before q ∫ n dt reaches order one, the open form dies without closing. *What the experiments found.* Z80, benign zeros: closure of the population in 20 of 20 worlds by 300,000 steps at L = 16, in 19 of 20 by one million at L = 20 and in 8 of 20 at L = 64, where the open form is least damaged and the motif must land in the longest tape (smallest q); at L = 50 a closed tape arises in 20 of 20 worlds but its offspring reopens, so the model's assumption that a closed variant breeds true fails there. BFF with the literal push and lethal brackets: n fell from 0.6 N at epoch 64 to 3 × 10^−4^ N by epoch 256 (N = 2^17^ tapes), a window of at most about 1.5 × 10^7^ tape-epochs (0.6 N over the 192 epochs of the decline), and 0 of 12 closures. BFF with benign brackets: the window is unbounded, yet no closure by control flow in 12 of 12, consistent with q = 0, since no closed heritable tiling exists in that language up to period 10 (Methods); the open form persists for 16,384 epochs. Z80 with lethal zeros (Stage I): the open form never establishes, so the window is empty from the start, and the first replicator is closed and late (Kaplan–Meier median 38,000 steps against 450; 10 of 10 worlds). At the aligned length L = 32, 12 of 20 worlds closed by one million steps, and given ten million steps 20 of 20 closed at L = 16 and 20. In a world filled with the open form from step 0 at L = 50, n at carrying capacity throughout, the jump arose by mutation and spread to half the cells within 5,000–13,250 steps in 3 of 3, against a median of order 10^5^ steps from a random start, though its carriers are mostly not confined. When an unmatched bracket halts with probability p, the open form held 98% of the soup at p ≤ 0.1 and collapsed at p = 0.3 and 1; the registered expectation that the collapse time would scale as 1/p was not met, the lethality acting as a threshold between 0.1 and 0.3. When the literal writes two or three copies of its word, so that a copy completes in one pass, no partial copies are made, no lethal tar forms and the open form persists for 16,384 epochs in 12 of 12 soups, with zero inflow from birth. The model does not predict which regime a machine is in; that depends on how the open replicator's own by-products act on it, which is what the experiments measure.

**Theorem 6 (architecture decides the first replicator).** *Hypotheses.* (H1) Default flow, as in Theorem 2, with execution ending when the pointer leaves the pair (as in BFF) or continuing around a ring (as in the Z80 soups). (H2) *Copy cost:* in the no-op context, an execution that runs each address at most once cannot write a copy of the tape (true of BFF for tapes with more than 32 non-zero bytes by Theorem 1(ii), and of any machine whose writes change one cell at a head that moves one cell per instruction and whose partner bytes execute as no-ops). (H3) *Self-consistent literal write:* an instruction that writes its own operand bytes verbatim at a write pointer that starts in, or advances into, the partner's region, and whose operand can be its own encoding in the phase executed (`LD BC,nn ; PUSH BC` with nn = `c5 01`; `P a b` with a = b = `P`). *Statement.* (i) Under (H1) and either proviso of Theorem 2, every closed self-replicator contains a cycle. (ii) Under (H1) and (H3), the tiling of the literal instruction is a straight-line program, hence open, that writes its own content at the advancing write pointer; it is a self-writer wherever the encounter lets the written bytes cover the partner's region, which holds in the Z80 (eight pushes over two passes of the 2L-byte ring; Proposition 3) and in BFF with `P` under both pointer rules (12 of 12 soups in each; the straight-line search, Methods). (iii) Under (H1) and (H2) without (H3), in a machine whose execution ends when the pointer leaves the pair, no straight-line program is a self-writer and every self-replicator contains a cycle: born loop-bearing (whether its pointer also stays out of the partner is a separate, measured property). (iv) Under uniform random initialisation over an alphabet of size σ, the expected number of occurrences of a fixed k-symbol pattern among N regions of length L is N (L − k + 1) σ^−k^, so a minimal open pattern shorter than every closed one is present with probability higher by a factor exponential in the length difference. This concerns the presence of the pattern, not of a replicating tiling; nucleation is not modelled (Proposition 4). *Proof.* (i) is Theorem 2. (ii) The tiling executes no control transfer, so by (H1) its pointer runs into the partner's region: it is open; its writes are its operands, by (H3) the tiling itself; completion of the copy is the per-machine fact cited. (iii) In the no-op context a straight-line program runs each address at most once before the pointer leaves the pair, and by (H2) such an execution cannot complete a copy; so a self-writer executes some address twice. (iv) is linearity of expectation. ∎ *What it decides.* Whether a substrate's first replicator can be open is decided by (H3); whether it is open in practice is observed. The Z80 has a self-consistent literal write (open first, 80 of 80 worlds); BFF has none, since its instructions increment in place or copy from memory (28 of 28 first replicators loop-bearing, and none of the 28 is open by measurement, entering the partner in at most 34% of encounters); BFF with one literal-write instruction added is open first (12 of 12). The theorem says nothing about how long the open phase lasts (Model 5). *Scope.* (H1) is a property of the von Neumann architecture, shared code and data under sequential control, and holds in Core War, Tierra, Avida, the Z80 and BFF alike. (H2) is a property of the copy instructions and must be checked per machine: it holds in BFF by Theorem 1 and fails in the ring Z80, whose two-byte self-writer completes its copy on a second pass of the ring, so for the Z80 the open-first conclusion rests on Propositions 3 and 4 and on observation. For a chemistry the hypotheses are a reading, not a theorem: (H1) would be a copier that runs off its template into adjacent material by default, (H3) a template whose content specifies its own complement without an interpreter.

**Lemma 7 (closure as zero information inflow).** Let the offspring of x in context e be o = F(x, e), with e drawn from a context distribution, and define the inflow λ(x) = I(o; e | x), the information the offspring carries about the context given the parent. (i) If λ(x) > 0 then P(o = x | x) < 1: a replicator with inflow cannot be perfectly faithful across contexts. (ii) If P(o = x | x) = 1 then λ(x) = 0: perfect fidelity across contexts implies zero inflow. (iii) Fano's inequality bounds the fidelity: P(o ≠ x | x) ≥ (H(o | x) − 1) / log|O|, where O is the set of possible offspring. *Proof.* If o = x with probability one given x, then o is a function of x alone and I(o; e | x) = 0, which is (ii); (i) is its contrapositive; (iii) is Fano's inequality with H(o | x) ≥ I(o; e | x). ∎ *Measurement.* For a deterministic executor H(o | x, e) = 0, so λ(x) = H(o | x), the entropy of the offspring over partners; the partner test estimates it as the plug-in entropy over 256 uniformly random partners (ceiling 8 bits). *Reading, with the measured qualification.* The lemma is Krakauer et al.'s individuality, the organism's future determined by its own past rather than the environment's, specialised to one generation and made measurable by intervention. Pointer closure (execution never enters the partner) is neither necessary nor sufficient for λ = 0: it is not sufficient because offspring positions the organism never writes keep the partner's bytes (7 of 28 pointer-closed BFF first replicators have H(o | x) > 0, three of them ≈ 7 bits from a single unwritten byte), and it is not necessary because a copier that overwrites everything it touches has λ ≈ 0 although its pointer enters the partner (the wrapping literal pusher of BFF, 0.04 bits). Inflow comes from partial copying; openness of control flow is the mechanism that makes the Z80 first replicator's copies partial. Measured: the first replicator has λ > 0 in 80 of 80 worlds (median 7.0 bits) and the loop-bearing successor λ = 0 in 63 of 67 (one offspring string in all 256 contexts), the four exceptions copying all but one to three bytes (Fig. 3c).
